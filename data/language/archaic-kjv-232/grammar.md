## Who is speaking as I or we

Paul's defence remains personal-I. Agrippa's almost-thou-persuadest-me in 26:28 is another personal-I, not Paul or a divine self. In the recounted encounter, Jesus uses plural-self for me in 26:14, I in 26:15–17, and faith-in-me in 26:18. The narration and embedded quotation retain their boundaries. The source's all-of-us fallen in 26:14 is the encounter company as singular-we; the earlier account's standing companions are not silently rewritten to harmonize the accounts.

The voyage narrator speaks as a travelling whole. This singular-we can include the voyage companions or, in 27:37, the counted shipboard company of 276. It does not mean 276 divine persons or erase the stated human count. First-person plural grammar classifies the speaker's presentation of the group, while the noun and numeral state its membership. The local Jewish leaders' we in 28:21–22 belongs to their hearing community, not to the travelling narrator. Source speech boundaries carry the change of referent.

Our religion, our twelve tribes and our fathers are singular-whole ancestral possessions. Paul's own life, chains and nation remain personal possessions. The angel's report that God has given Paul his fellow voyagers in 27:24 is third-person God; no first-person divine speaker is invented. The quoted I-would-heal in 28:27 is plural-self within the Isaiah citation attributed to the Holy Spirit. This distinguishes quoted divine identity from the people who close their own eyes.

## Defence is an account, not the translator's verdict

Paul's claim about the strictest sect in 26:5 is not replaced by the outsider religious-concern word used earlier by Festus. The twelve tribes serve day and night; the resurrection question remains a question. His former voice against those put to death does not receive an invented voting procedure. Compelling blasphemy and persecution in foreign cities remain explicit.

The commissioning includes things already seen and things in which Jesus will appear. Delivering Paul from the Gentiles and sending him to those Gentiles coexist. Forgiveness, inheritance among the sanctified, and faith in the speaker remain distinct relations. The alien register keeps their uneasy literal construction rather than supplying a systematic doctrinal paraphrase.

Festus's mad accusation, Paul's former frenzy, and Paul's denial are attributed expressions. They are not medical diagnoses. Soberness is composure or reasoned self-command, not merely absence of drink. Almost in Agrippa's response is retained as supplied. Paul's wish combines almost and altogether, with the explicit exception of these bonds. The final could-have-been-freed if-not-appealed is counterfactual, not an actual release.

## Nautical distinctions that the ordinary lexicon cannot carry

Eleven common entries are added. Anchor is ship-hold as a holding device; sound-depth is water-measure rather than audible sound; quicksand is soft-sand as the supplied shipping hazard; rudder is ship-turn; inlet is sea-enter. The auxiliary boat is small-ship, distinct from the main vessel and from a river ferry. Lee is wind-shadow: sailing under Cyprus, Crete or Clauda means a sheltered relation, not travelling beneath submerged land.

Food-abstinence receives an explicit general-food sense of the inherited fast form. This separates the long abstinence and fourteen-day food statement in the storm from the calendar fast in 27:9. No unstated voluntary vow, modern medical explanation or named feast is added. Bowel-flux retains the historical symptom relation; bloody and fever remain separate and no pathogen is diagnosed. The source outsider label barbarian is retained as a contextual loan form, with the islanders' exceptional kindness stated explicitly. It does not mean cruel.

Twenty-three contextual proper entries record the centurion and military unit, ports, islands, wind-name, island host and ship's sign. Phenice is the haven of Crete here, not the previously mentioned mainland region. Castor and Pollux are the ship's sign, not passengers. Three Taverns remains a place label, not an independent assertion that three buildings were counted. These entries preserve local source identities without importing geographical identifications or merging namesakes.

## Storm, promises and conditions

Paul's initial warning includes lives; the later assurance says no life lost but the ship. Neither statement is rewritten to remove the change. The promise and the condition that the sailors must remain both survive. The sailors' pretended anchor work is distinguished from their attempted escape. The soldiers' proposed killing of the prisoners remains a plan stopped by the centurion, not an event that occurred.

The third day, fourteenth night, soundings of twenty and fifteen fathoms, four stern anchors, fourteenth day, 276 souls, two meeting seas and the order that swimmers go first are independently checked. Fathoms are not silently converted to a modern unit. The supplied anchors-up, committed-to-sea, rudder bands and mainsail reading is retained, rather than importing another translation's technical reconstruction. Bow fixed and stern broken remain separate outcomes. Wheat is thrown into the sea after eating. All escape to land.

## Island and Rome: do not invent an ending

The viper fastens to Paul's hand. No unprinted wound or venom injection is added. The islanders first infer murderer and then god; both are their judgments. The narrator does not certify either inference. Publius's holdings are possessions in the asset sense. His father's fever and bloody flux remain historical symptoms, followed by prayer, laying on hands and healing.

Three days of hospitality, three months before departure, three days at Syracuse, one day before the south wind, seven days with brethren, three days before the local meeting and two whole years in rented lodging remain distinct. Fetched-a-compass means a circuit in travel, not a navigation instrument. Own hired house is rented lodging, not outright ownership or hired labour.

The full supplied prisoner-transfer clause in 28:16 and the full verse 28:29 remain. Paul denies intending to accuse his nation. The local leaders deny both receiving adverse letters and hearing an adverse report from arriving brethren. Some believe and some do not; there is no blanket conversion. The repeated hear/hear and see/see citation keeps both failures of understanding and perception. They close their eyes; the quoted divine healer is plural-self. The text ends with teaching in confidence and none forbidding. No death, acquittal, release or onward itinerary is appended.
