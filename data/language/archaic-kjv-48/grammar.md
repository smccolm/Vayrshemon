## 1. Plural-self, united body, separate claimants and the actual pair

The historical first-person system distinguishes modes of participation, not just the number of visible mouths. `ipl` lets a singular speaker own an action performed through a plurality. `wesg` presents a group as one body. `wepl` keeps the participating individuals separately salient. `we:du` is a genuine pair. These values are contextual grammatical commitments, not decorative replacements for English pronouns.

At30:8 David asks whether I(pl.self) shall pursue and overtake: the projected action is his band moving under his agency. By contrast, his fear of personal death at27:1, request for lodging at27:5, restraint at26:11/23 and appeal for protection at26:24 use personal-I. Neither kingship nor religious speech forces plural-self.

The most pointed contrast is30:22–24. Fighters say we(pl.individuals) recovered the goods and will not give a share to the men who stayed behind. David answers that the LORD gave to we(sg.whole), preserved that whole and delivered the opposing company into our(whole) hand. The grammar makes their competing accounts of membership audible. Equal shares for battle and baggage duty are then stated. It does not falsely suggest unanimous agreement before David's ruling.

The Egyptian moves from personal-I in his abandoned-servant biography at30:13 to singular-we for the raiding party at30:14. Singular-we admits his participation; it does not assimilate his Egyptian identity to his Amalekite master. At27:11 it is David's implicated raiding body that must avoid surviving witnesses. The Philistine princes' with-us in29:4/9 treats their threatened field army as a body. Quotations reset the first-person referent.

At26:11 David and Abishai leave as we(dual). Ahimelech the Hittite heard the earlier invitation, but the narrative names only Abishai as accepting and descending. This dual is not an arbitrary two-person label for every conversation.

## 2. Knowledge, appearances and the Endor discontinuity

28:12 is narrated as the woman seeing Samuel. At28:13 she reports gods(plural) ascending from the earth. At28:14 she describes one old man in a mantle; Saul then perceives Samuel. These are different clauses with different evidential owners. Retain their order and number rather than replacing them with a single theological explanation.

Samuel's first person in28:15 is personal-I asking why he was disturbed. A dead speaker is not automatically plural-self, a triune speaker, an impostor or a modern diagnostic category. Saul reports the LORD's silence; the narrator had specified dreams, Urim and prophets at28:6, but Saul repeats only prophets and dreams at28:15. The translation does not add the missing channel to make his report identical.

`kjv_familiar_spirit` and `kjv_wizard` retain their inherited historical categories. `kjv_divine` is the ritual verb for seeking hidden knowledge, distinct from the ordinary adjective divine. Urim remains named without a fabricated operating procedure. The scene is translated as supplied, with neither a skeptical replacement narrative nor a doctrinal endorsement added.

The transition from plural gods to singular description is especially relevant to the user's interest in unstable divine number. Keeping that instability is more faithful than turning it into a count of three. Future explicitly Trinitarian passages will require their own clause-level decisions.

## 3. Promises, deception and incompatible moral positions

The text preserves Saul's renewed pursuit after earlier admissions, David's private fear after promises of success, and Saul's oath by the LORD protecting a practitioner whom he previously expelled. Achish also swears by the LORD in29:6 and likens David to an angel of God in29:9. Foreign speakers keep their actual religious vocabulary.

David's actual raiding targets at27:8 are Geshurites, Gezrites and Amalekites. His report at27:10 names Judah, Jerahmeelites and Kenites. Achish's belief at27:12 is attributed to Achish. The translation does not replace the report with the narrator's account or certify Achish's conclusion that David is permanently alienated from Israel.

David's guarded what-thy-servant-can-do reply at28:2 and my-lord-the-king's-enemies protest at29:8 remain potentially double-edged. No unprinted king's name resolves the latter. The princes' fear of reconciliation through their soldiers' heads at29:4 remains a suspicion; no secret plan is assigned to David as a fact.

The killings and destruction in27:9/11 remain explicit. In30:2 the Amalekite raiders have killed nobody, great or small. The uncomfortable contrast survives. This register is awkward because it carries alien claims and conflicting responsibilities, not because it omits them.

## 4. Historical senses and deliberately bounded lexical growth

Twenty-two explicit entries are added:17 proper identities and5 common entries. Fourteen new names have authored adaptations. Three additional identity records share existing surface forms: Ahimelech the Hittite, Abinadab son of Saul and south Ramoth. Common additions are cruse, partridge, raid, twilight and relational reconciliation. No paradigm or suffix changes are introduced.

`kjv_raid` is strike-enter, used for made-a-road at27:10. This is hostile incursion, not construction of a road. The historical sense is supported by [Webster's1828 entry and Easton's entry for road](https://www.kingjamesbibledictionary.com/Dictionary/road). This lexical consultation does not replace the supplied KJV with another edition.

`kjv_twilight` is dim-light. At30:17 the action runs from twilight through the following evening. The dawn/dusk ambiguity is left open rather than replaced with a clock hour. The [UBS translation commentary on1Samuel30:17](https://tips.translation.bible/story/translation-commentary-on-1-samuel-3017/) records both morning and evening readings and differing translations; this edition retains the supplied term and next-day wording.

`kjv_reconcile_relation` is harmony-restore: restoring a favorable relation after estrangement. It is distinct from ritual `kjv_atonement`. The princes suspect this restoration could be purchased through violence. `kjv_cruse` is water-vessel without an invented material or capacity. `kjv_partridge` has an explicitly authored creature-name without a claim to a precise modern species.

Existing senses are reused where adequate: rearguard for rereward, habitual visiting for haunt, livestock for broad cattle, baggage for stuff, natural water source for fountain and abstention for fast. The verb `kjv_leaven_raise`, not the noun for leavening substance, forms unleavened. Inherited morphology allows the construction without another redundant word.

## 5. Names do not collapse persons or sites

Ahimelech the Hittite at26:6 is distinct from Ahimelech the slain priest, whose son Abiathar brings the ephod at30:7. Abinadab son of Saul at31:2 is distinct from the ark-householder and Jesse's son. South Ramoth in the gift inventory is not automatically the eastern refuge city. Shared spellings retain a shared form while semantic identities and context remain separate.

Ahinoam of Jezreel continues the distinct identity introduced in0.10-kjv.47, not Saul's wife. Abigail's Nabal-wife apposition remains after her remarriage because the source retains it. The dictionary does not overwrite the earlier relationship to disguise that historical naming practice. Ashtaroth in31:10 is the cultic/divine identity rather than the place-name.

Names in the gift itinerary stay in source order. Their adapted forms are authored conventions, not discoveries of native etymology or claims about modern archaeological locations.

## 6. Quantity, chronology and the limits of totality

Retain3000 chosen pursuers,600 emigrating men,2 wives and one full year plus4months. Saul has2 accompanying men, not a total party of2. Hundreds and thousands in the marching order are plural formations. Thousands and ten-thousands in the praise song remain plural magnitudes, not an audited casualty count.

At30:9–10 the600 divide into400 pursuers and200 exhausted men. The400 camel-mounted escapees at30:17 are a different group. The Egyptian receives a piece of fig cake and2 raisin clusters after3days and3nights without food or water; he was abandoned after falling sick3days earlier. Do not turn the piece into a whole cake or the clusters into cakes.

One stroke without a second at26:8 is an unperformed offer. Third day at30:1, the promise of tomorrow at28:19, next-day discovery at31:8 and7days of fasting at31:13 retain their stated relations. The translator does not invent clock times or rearrange scenes to fix chronology.

All his men die with Saul at31:6 remains the source's bounded claim alongside the fleeing Israelites in31:7. Recover-all is repeated at30:18/19. Preserving such totalities is different from extending them to every Israelite or every item in the wider world.

## 7. Bodily idioms, coercion, death and uncertainty

Keep my-life-in-my-hand, keeper-of-my-head, blood-before-the-face and hand-as-agency idioms even when they sound chunky in the historical register. Handmaid in the Endor woman's self-reference is deferential language, not an automatic claim that Saul owns her. She risks her life, asks reciprocal listening, offers a morsel and then provides a substantial meal.

At28:17 the supplied to-him remains beside thy-hand; it is not silently emended to thee. At28:19 with-me remains without naming heaven or hell or counting sons before the narrative names them.

31:3–6 retains the wounds, Saul's request to avoid abuse, the armor-bearer's fear and refusal, and the narrator's death sequence. It adds no procedural detail or modern diagnosis.31:12/13 keeps burning of the bodies followed by burial of bones and fasting; no medical rationale is invented to make the custom more familiar.
