# Historical KJV 0.10-kjv.23 · a nation speaking as one and as many

Numbers 17–25 adds 245 individually authored verses to the pinned v22 historical register. The complete Bible is still unfinished. Earlier targets and frozen Jonah are unchanged. All new entries, notes and constructions belong to this archaic branch, never to be modernized. This is translator self-review, not independent certification.

## Nation-person, travelling whole, pair, individual and messenger

The source's me/we shifts in diplomatic speech are made conspicuous. Israel is Edom's single brother in 20:14, yet comprises generations, people, livestock and many feet. Its we clauses use singular-we, and its personified I/my clauses in 20:17,19 and 21:2,22 use plural-I/my-plural. Edom's I in 20:18 speaks for a polity able to come out with many people. These uses extend the plural-self beyond the deity without claiming that every named king's private I must be plural.

Moses/Aaron's must we fetch water in 20:10 uses the inherited dual: the named human pair, distinct from the community's singular-we and divine plural-I in 20:12. Balak and Balaam normally use personal singular-I. Balak's hoped-for we may smite in 22:6 recruits the Moabite whole, whereas the returning envoys' with us in 22:14 is several witnesses, plural-we. The donkey has her own singular-I and remembered history. The angel in 22:32–35 is a distinct personal speaking agent; the narration does not automatically identify every messenger I with the deity's plural-self. God in 22:20 uses plural-I.

This is a deliberate discourse interpretation, documented for future readers. The source does not count divine persons here. Two servants, three blows, three blessings and seven altars are not Trinity counts. The register has no psychiatric claim attached to person or number.

## A living rod and an inherited deity

Aaron's rod is among the twelve, not a thirteenth. Buds, blossoms and almonds appear together in the narration; no ordinary season is inserted to make the growth plausible. The people fear that singular-we all perish, a fearful present-tense utterance rather than a narrator assertion that the whole assembly is already dead.

Numbers 18 assigns sanctuary liability to Aaron's wider paternal house and priest-office liability to Aaron/sons. Levite assistance, priestly approach, and forbidden outsider approach remain distinct. The source calls people a gift and office a gift-service, while threatening death at the boundary. The deity says plural-I is Aaron's share and inheritance: this awkward equation is retained rather than changed to a comfortable abstract promise of protection.

Most-holy portions are reserved from fire and restricted to the stated male eating category, while lifted/waved gifts also name daughters and clean household members. The most-holy-place phrase is not silently reinterpreted architecturally. The permanent salt covenant remains a material metaphor beside the hereditary allocation. Human and unclean-animal firstborn are redeemed; cow/sheep/goat firstborn are not. Five sanctuary shekels at twenty gerahs each equal one hundred gerahs. Tenth-of-tithe is one hundredth of the original base. The Levites' received inheritance and absence of land inheritance remain two uses of the same source word, with their different objects visible.

## Purification material can impart impurity

The red heifer must be unblemished and never yoked. Eleazar oversees while an unspecified person slays; the agents are not merged. Seven blood sprinklings precede burning skin/flesh/blood/dung. Cedar, hyssop and scarlet remain the named additions without invented quantities or botanical certainty. The priest, burner and ash gatherer have separately stated washing/impurity consequences.

The previously clean gatherer becomes unclean while preparing material that purifies another. Touching the separation water can make a person unclean. These reversals remain ritual categories rather than being replaced with medical cleanliness. A corpse-contact patient has seven days with third/seventh-day conditions; secondary contact lasts until evening. Tent contents and unbound open vessels are included. Running water is flowing water, not modern plumbing or a person running. The source's sanctuary-defilement claim is retained without inventing a narrated unauthorized entry.

## Speech, striking and failed passage

At Kadesh the command is to speak to the rock; Moses strikes twice. Water comes anyway, then both leaders are excluded for the stated failure of belief/sanctification. The translation does not add an exhaustive explanation of the offence or excuse Aaron by removing him from the address. This Meribah is recorded separately from the earlier naming episode without asserting identical geography. Miriam's death precedes the complaint; no year is supplied beyond the printed first month. Aaron's garment transfer to Eleazar and subsequent death remain separate events, followed by thirty days' mourning.

Israel offers passage terms, then payment if water is used. Edom still refuses and comes out with force; Israel turns away. No victorious battle is invented to rescue the failed negotiation. The later Sihon episode ends differently and remains separate. The quoted book of LORD's wars fragment in 21:14–15 stays fragmentary. Beer is a place name, not a drink; lawgiver-directed digging with staves remains the song's image rather than ordinary shovels substituted for it.

## Complaints and warfare keep their own claims

The people say no bread while despising this light bread. Light means disparaged/insubstantial, not luminous. Fiery serpents retain the descriptor without an asserted modern species or literal combustion. Looking at the made brass serpent is the source condition for survival; no medical mechanism or later named theological identity is inserted.

The conditional destruction vow and narrated killing are not reduced to bloodless political conquest. The war song names Chemosh and attributes giving children into captivity to him, without translator certification. It is introduced and closed as quoted song. Ammon's strong border limits the stated possession. Og and Sihon remain distinct rulers/territories. Claims that none remained alive are retained as source claims, not independently verified history.

## Permission, anger and animal perception

Balaam is first forbidden to go, then conditionally permitted, then encounters anger because he went, then receives permission again. No unprinted calling event or motive is invented to make these stages seamless. Divination rewards are carried, not automatically accepted as payment; a houseful of silver/gold remains a hypothetical offer. Adversary is the angel's opposing role, not the proper name Satan.

The donkey sees before Balaam. She turns into a field, presses against a wall and finally falls where neither side offers escape. Three blows correspond to these narrated responses. Wont is customary conduct, not a contraction. Balaam wishes for a sword to kill her; the actual sword belongs to the unseen angel, who says the counterfactual outcome would have killed Balaam and spared her. The animal's female pronouns and personal history remain.

## An oracle can resist both its patron and narrative consistency

Parable in chapters 23–24 is an extended solemn oracle, not automatically a short moral anecdote. Defy is hostile against-speaking. Seven altars with an animal pair each occur at successive sites, and the repeated lists remain complete. The king alternately asks for a curse, neither curse nor blessing, and another attempt elsewhere. Limited views of the people's edge remain limited.

God not man to lie/repent in 23:19 uses speak falsely and change mind, not lie down or automatic moral repentance. The claim remains beside earlier divine reconsideration. No iniquity/perverseness seen in Israel likewise stays beside prior complaints and penalties. These are not silently harmonized. Unicorn is an explicit one-horn-beast calque of the supplied label, without choosing a modern species. Fourth part of Israel is a quarter, not four tribes asserted by the translator.

## Open-eyed trance and future images remain unresolved where the source is

The source calls the seer open-eyed while falling into a trance. Vision-rest is an authored image, not a clinical diagnosis or a claim his eyes close. The camp dwellings are not all the sacred meeting tent. Lign aloes is the source wood/tree label rather than a specified modern succulent. Water, seed, buckets, lion-feeding and broken bones retain their bodily and material imagery.

Star and sceptre are not assigned a later named identity. Sheth and Chittim retain their supplied spellings rather than silently normalizing to Seth/Kittim. Agag is not forced to a particular later person. The ambiguous final he of 24:24 stays ambiguous. Exclamatory wishes, lament and oracle emphasis use inherited endings, preserving the script without an unsupported exclamation glyph.

## Peace after killing stays uncomfortable

Numbers 25 moves from Moabite daughters to a Midianite woman; the group change remains. Whoredom has a concrete human sexual setting here, unlike earlier figurative unfaithfulness, but no unprinted sexual action inside the tent is described. The hanging order's heads and against-the-sun wording are retained without an invented court procedure. Phinehas thrusts the javelin through both people; the woman's belly and the stated plague cessation remain explicit.

Zimri and Cozbi are both named with their families after their deaths. The deity's plural-self credits zeal, wrath-aversion and atonement, then grants a peace covenant and perpetual priestly succession. Peace immediately after killing is the deliberately uncomfortable source vocabulary, not something to rationalize away. The plague total is twenty-four thousand, distinct from the previous fourteen thousand seven hundred and from differing figures elsewhere. The concluding hostility to Midian remains attributed source speech, not an instruction to a present-day reader.
