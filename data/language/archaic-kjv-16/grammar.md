## Divine plural self, delegated personal I and collective liability

The deity's explicit I in 6:17 and 7:34 uses plural-self, with my-plural offerings. Human priests receive portions without becoming members of that divine self. Moses's I commanded and I am commanded in 8:31,35 and 10:13,18 remain personal singular I. In 10:3 Moses quotes the divine I-plural inside his own speech. Aaron's grief and counterfactual I in 10:19 are personal. The whole congregation in 4:13 is a single named body followed by plural they, preserving collective identity and membership without inventing first-person we where the source has none. Plural-I is a user-authorized extension; the original chart's singular-we remains an attested starting distinction. Later explicit Trinity passages are not yet translated.

## Two status axes, not one moral label

Ritual unclean and uncleanness receive explicit senses distinct from the inherited morally-corrupted use. Clean receives a corresponding ritual-condition sense. In 10:10 holy versus unholy/common and unclean versus clean are separate oppositions. A common object need not be morally evil; a ritually unclean person is not automatically a morally corrupt person. The text can separately predicate unclean and guilty, as 5:2 does. No modern disease, disability or hygiene theory is inserted into unspecified bodily conditions.

## Roles, animals and blood destinations

Offerer, priest, Aaron's sons, elders and Moses remain distinct actors where the source assigns actions. Herd/flock burnt offerings require males; peace offerings allow male or female; ruler's sin goat is male while an ordinary person's goat or lamb is female. Priest and congregation sin-bullock blood goes before the vail and onto the inner altar, while ruler/common-person blood goes to the burnt-offering altar. Seven sprinklings count occasions, not fingers or victims. Anatomy distinguishes bird crop from agricultural crop, fat tissue from corpulence, flank/backbone from abstract direction, and inherited grain ears from hearing organs.

## Grain, memorial portions and unequal means

Meat offering retains food/grain meaning. Oil mixed into cakes differs from oil applied to wafers; flour/oil handfuls differ from all frankincense. Memorial portion is a physical portion burnt, distinguished from a monument or abstract remembrance. Pan, fryingpan and oven remain separate source labels without guessed modern shapes or temperatures. Frying receives a food-cooking sense distinct from young fish. The inability sequence in 5:7–13 moves from a lamb to two birds to one tenth ephah flour; the text does not expressly quantify wealth. The flour sin offering forbids oil and frankincense, unlike ordinary chapter 2 grain offerings. One bird is sin offering, the second burnt offering.

## Restitution, ignorance and reported valuation

Ignorance does not remove liability in these source rules. Oath witnessing, concealed found property, force, deception, entrusted keeping and fellowship/dealing remain distinct. Fellowship in 6:2 is not mere friendship, nor a confidently specified modern contract. Principal means the original amount/property due; an additional fifth gives 120 percent of that base, not a total of one fifth. Holy-thing restitution goes to the priest in 5:16; neighbour-property restitution goes to the entitled person in 6:5. Thy valuation is retained without inventing its amount or resolving its addressee by an unstated name. Sacrifice and restitution are separately required.

## Consumption, contact and portions

The source calls burnt material food of the offering; that awkward concreteness remains. Contact with holy flesh makes holy, rather than being silently rewritten as a prerequisite to touch. Grain leftovers assigned to priests differ from priests' own grain gifts, which are wholly burned. Sin offerings whose blood goes inside are not eaten, preserving the exception alongside the general priestly eating rule. Thanksgiving flesh has a same-day limit; vow/voluntary flesh may also be eaten tomorrow, with third-day remainder burned. Leavened bread presented alongside thanksgiving offerings remains beside the separate ban on burning leaven. The breast and right shoulder have different recipients. Daughters are expressly included in 10:14 for portions eaten in a clean place, contrasting with male-only consumption of most-holy portions.

## Consecration, fire and disputed obedience

Seven consecration days lead to an eighth-day appearance. Blood goes on named right extremities; right is anatomical, not entitlement. Moses's portion in 8:29 is not reassigned to Aaron by a general rule elsewhere. Divine fire first consumes offerings, then Nadab and Abihu. Strange fire not commanded is the stated fault; later drinking restrictions are not treated as proof of drunkenness. Aaron's silence is not invented agreement. Moses questions a burnt sin-goat because its blood never entered the holy place; Aaron's counterfactual asks whether eating after these events would be acceptable, and Moses is content. No divine verdict or general repeal is added beyond that ending.
