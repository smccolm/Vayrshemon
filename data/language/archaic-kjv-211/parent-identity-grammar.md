## 2. Four first-person perspectives

`i` / rô: one ordinary speaking self. `wepl` / lym: several participants considered individually. `ipl` / rôun: a speaking I apprehended as internally plural; inherited I plus plural -un, lexicalized as a distinct discourse perspective. `wesg` / ly: several members apprehended as one acting whole. The members do not disappear when grammatical construal is singular. Existing collective `we` remains readable; wesg makes the intended construal explicit in new authored analyses.

The construction glosses are I(pl.self.) and we(sg.whole.). Their force is in the pronoun, not an invented person-agreement ending on every verb. The ordinary case suffixes follow the complete stem. A second :pl suffix on ipl is not licensed in authored text: the stem already carries internal plurality. `iplself` refers back to the internally plural speaker. Possessive aliases `mypl/` and `ourone/` retain the selected perspective; ordinary my/our do not silently change their antecedents.

## 3. Source meaning and the theological lens

The translation is intentionally alien and interpretive. It permits the biblical divine voice to be rendered as plural-I in self-identification, binding promises and covenant possession, and singular-we in concerted deliberation. This is the author's requested historical translator's lens, not a claim that an English singular pronoun is grammatically plural or that Genesis explicitly names the Trinity.

Genesis 1:26 and 11:7 use singular-we for one deliberating/acting whole; their English us remains intact in the source column and the note states the construal. Genesis 3:22 uses one-from-plural-I, exposing internal distinction. The old 0.10-kjv.2 instruction to use only wepl in those three passages is superseded by the author's new direction. Its old edition remains unchanged on disk.

In covenant quotations the selected speaker perspective persists across the speech. A resumed I does not randomly switch to singular merely to reduce repetition. Abram's own I stays singular. Reported third-person divine he does not automatically become they. Divine titles are not mechanically replaced with pronouns. Repeated subjects can remain heavy and awkward in this register.

## 4. Human collective use and limits

In Genesis 13:8 we(sg.whole.) are brothers construes the kin group as one body despite its members and their potential dispute. Separation into two parties in 13:9 and 13:11 remains explicit. This gives the system ordinary social uses alongside sacred strain; the sacred register is not its sole reason to exist.

Do not use singular-we for every English we. Where individuals act separately, contrast responsibility, or one member excludes another, retain wepl or explicit named participants. Do not use plural-I for every supernatural speech. A messenger speaking for a sender, a crowd, a representative king and a divine self-identification are distinct situations; record the source relationship.

## 6. Lexical sense discipline in Abram's cycle

Goods/resources is distinct from metaphysical substance; famine is regional food-absence; sojourning is temporary dwelling without an inserted ownership right. The numerical count verb is distinct from the noble rank. Pharaoh is a title, not a personal name. Camel, heifer, ram, goat, turtle-dove and pigeon retain the source distinctions. Turtle in the sacrificial list is a bird, not a reptile.

Plain in geographical formulas uses a geographical sense, not the adjective unadorned. Do not silently replace the KJV's plain with a definite tree species. Directions retain spatial senses, especially right-hand versus just entitlement. Bowels in the promised offspring passage remains an inward-body expression, not an anatomical correction inserted into the main text.
