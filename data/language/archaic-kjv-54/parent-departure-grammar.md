## 1. Rival singular-we bodies claim and reject one king

Israel's argument in19:9–10 uses `wesg` for the political whole saved by David and formerly committed to Absalom. Judah's answer in19:42 uses `wesg` for a different body claiming kinship with David. Israel answers in19:43 with its own singular-we and `ourone` king/advice. The shared grammatical shape does not merge the rival speakers.

Sheba's **we have no part** in20:1 claims the dissident body's voice. David's endangered royal company uses `wesg` in20:6; the Gibeonites use it in21:4–6 for the community threatened with removal and now seeking redress. David's fighting men in21:17 use it for the body whose future expeditions will exclude the king. A singular whole can be remembered, contested, claimed or threatened; it is not automatically the whole nation or a morally approved collective.

## 2. Counted lives, personal claims and actual pairs remain distinct

Joab's accusation in19:6 combines personal **I perceive** with `wepl` for all the separate people whose hypothetical deaths he contrasts with Absalom's survival. Israel's brothers in19:41 use **of we(pl)**, retaining multiple related persons. Barzillai's age, taste, hearing and approaching death remain personal `i`; Mephibosheth's complaint, Shimei's confession and the wise woman's speech also remain personal.

David's knowing in19:22 is personal while his kingship is plural-self office. The sons of Zeruiah receive `you:du`, continuing the actual pair. The oath between David and Jonathan in21:7 uses `they:du`; neither the surviving Mephibosheth nor the entire royal family is added to that pair.

## 3. Plural-I marks office and mediated action without erasing responsibility

David's appointment of Amasa, disposition of the disputed land, court provision for Barzillai/Chimham and gathering of Judah use `ipl`. Personal accompaniment and bodily kinship can remain in adjacent clauses. The change reflects the relation between a person and what the court or army does through that person, not a random alternation for stylistic variety.

Joab's denial of destruction and promise to withdraw in20:20–21 use `ipl`, the commander speaking for the besiegers. The denial remains his claim alongside the narrated battering of the wall. David's question about atonement and promise to hand over the demanded people in21:3–6 likewise use plural-self authority. The grammar exposes mediated action; it does not excuse it or turn the characters' bargains into language-wide moral rules.

## 4. Accusations, promises and unresolved judgments

Joab's claim that David would prefer Absalom alive and everyone else dead is attributed counterfactual accusation. It is not David's stated wish. The conditional warning that no one will stay likewise remains Joab's oath. David's public appearance follows without pretending his grief has disappeared.

Mephibosheth's allegation that Ziba deceived and slandered him remains a counter-account. David orders a division of land without resolving the competing claims for the reader. Mephibosheth's offer that Ziba take all is speech, not a second enacted grant. Son of Saul retains the supplied generational usage, and the Jerusalem meeting remains beside the crossing narrative without a fabricated reconciliation.

David's promise to Shimei is retained as spoken. The return-day refusal of executions is not generalized into an abolition. His promise to replace Joab with Amasa remains even though the later list again names Joab over the army. The text records the intervening killing instead of smoothing the appointments into orderly administration.

## 5. Women, confinement, the mother-city and the vigil

The ten concubines remain the women left to keep the house. Their custody, sustenance, absence of further approach and lifelong widowhood are explicit. Widowhood here records an imposed living condition; it does not assert that David has already died or invent a rationale, consent or recovery.

The wise woman identifies herself as one of Israel's peaceable and faithful. Her personal-I is not silently replaced by a city speaking supernaturally. The city nevertheless retains the source's mother metaphor and her appeal to the LORD's inheritance. Joab's demand for one man, her promise, the people's act and the actual withdrawal remain separate stages. Summary violence is retained without added procedure.

Rizpah's protection of the dead from birds by day and beasts by night continues until rain. No invented duration or inner approval of the killings is supplied. David hears of her action before retrieving bones. The sequence matters without a fabricated conversion speech or explanatory mechanism for the later divine response.

## 6. Six common additions and inherited sense choices

`kjv_ferryboat` is river-ship, preserving the supplied boat rather than replacing it with a ford. `kjv_siege_mound` is siege-earth-heap; `kjv_trench_siege` is earth-dig as a physical feature associated with that bank. `kjv_widowhood` is widow-state. `kjv_zeal` records the noun sense sharing zealous, without approving the narrated motivation. `kjv_peaceable` is peace-seek, a person's disposition or claim rather than just absence of fighting. Existing entries handle custody versus a ward-person, imputation versus counting objects, tribute as subordinate dues/service, devotional atonement, recorder versus a musical instrument, clinging versus splitting, sword sheath, rib, brass and bodily lameness. The source's fierce words remain more fierce, not necessarily louder.

## 7. Identities and source tensions remain recorded

Seventeen newly written source forms cover Chimham, Bichri, Bethmaachah, Berites, Adoram, Sheva, Ira, Jairite, Armoni, Ishbibenob, Gob, Sibbechai, Hushathite, Saph, Elhanan, Jaareoregim and Gibeonite. Five additional identity records share older forms: Sheba son of Bichri, Abel the city, Mephibosheth son of Rizpah, Barzillai the Meholathite and Jonathan son of Shimeah. They cannot be merged with earlier namesakes merely because their surface letters match.

The supplied **Michal** and **brought up** in21:8 remain; the translation neither substitutes Merab nor makes upbringing into childbirth. The spared Jonathan-son Mephibosheth and handed-over Rizpah-son Mephibosheth remain different people. The Meholathite Barzillai is distinct from the elderly Gileadite benefactor. Elhanan kills **the brother of Goliath** in this supplied KJV wording; no alternative witness or unstated brother's name replaces it.

The retrospective street/hanging description of Saul and Jonathan remains alongside the earlier wall/burning account. The Gibeonites' broad refusal of killing in21:4 remains beside their specific subsequent demand. No invented chronology or legal theory resolves these tensions.

## 8. Quantity, embodied decline and collective summaries

The audit preserves one thousand Benjaminites, fifteen sons and twenty servants as separate groups; Barzillai's repeated fourscore is eighty. Half Israel and Israel's ten-part political claim are not turned into an inferred population. The ten concubines, three-day deadline and fifth-rib ordinal remain explicit. The single blow is not repeated.

Three famine years remain consecutive. Two sons plus five sons equal the stated seven victims. First harvest days express season, not an invented calendar date. The spear weight stays three hundred shekels of brass without a modern conversion. Six digits on each hand and foot agree with the stated twenty-four, and the final four giant-descendants remain four.

David's faintness is bodily vulnerability, not loss of the narrative's symbolic valuation of him. The light-of-Israel metaphor remains metaphor. The final summary **by David's hand and his servants' hand** preserves collective agency without falsely replacing the individually named killers with David personally.
