# KJV 0.10-kjv.5 — self-oath, household voices and bodily inheritance

## 1. By my selves: a plural-I reflexive under one oath

Genesis 22:16 uses `iplself:ins ipl swear:perf`: by my selves I-many have sworn. The internally plural first person is the oath's reference as well as its speaker. The says-the-Name-lord formula and the delivering messenger remain separately visible. This deliberately awkward identity construction develops the author's requested perspective; it does not identify named members of the Trinity where the source does not. Genesis 21:18 and 22:12 voice a commissioned divine promise through plural-I, while the source separately names the sender. Now I know remains an epistemic claim and is not repaired into omniscient foreknowledge.

Genesis 26:2–5 and 26:24 renew the same covenant voice through plural-I and my-plural possessors. The plural possessor does not pluralize the object: my-many servant Abraham is still one named servant. Narrative he remains he. No plural-I is inserted into Genesis 25:23, which contains divine speech but no first-person self-reference.

## 2. Singular-we is a social construction too

The Heth community in 23:6 and the lodging household in 24:25 speak as one provision-making body. The phrase none of us in the tomb-owner clause remains distributive plural membership. Laban and Bethuel's common answer in 24:50, and the family's agreed consultation in 24:57, use singular-we. Our-one sister in 24:60 marks the household's common relation rather than a new divine kinship.

Abimelech's us-one in 26:10 names a polity bearing liability; one of the people is still an individual actor. In 26:28 the delegates are several observers, then one proposing body. The initial between-us is broad plural membership across parties; the narrowed between-us-one-and-thee separates the delegation from Isaac. Workers reporting found water in 26:32 remain ordinary plural-we. This contrast is semantic, not random variation applied to every occurrence of we.

## 3. Reported speech carries several first-person centers

Genesis 24 reports speech inside a servant's narration of Abraham's speech, with a divine promise quoted inside it. Outer I is the servant or Abraham as locally identified; the land-promise I in 24:7 is plural-I. Rebekah's I will draw and I will go remain ordinary personal I. Plural ye receives explicit `you:pl` when multiple addressees matter.

Repeated accounts are not deduplicated. The original commission discusses the woman's willingness; the servant's later report discusses the kin group's refusal. Both are retained. Earlier and reported pitcher positions and inward prayer details remain different where the supplied KJV differs. The translator must not silently harmonize the source to make it tidier.

## 4. Willingness, bodies and status stay explicit

Rebekah's willingness is an explicit condition in 24:5 and 24:8. The household answer does not eliminate the direct question and her I will go in 24:58. At-her-mouth in 24:57 stays bodily and awkward. Concubine, bought or dependent servants, oath-hand under a thigh, nursing, weaning and pregnancy retain their historical relations without inventing modern equivalences or consent scenes absent from the source.

Genesis 21:14 retains the source's awkward and-the-child after the shoulder clause without asserting an infant was carried there. The expulsion, Isaac's binding and threat to kill, and a ruler's death penalty remain explicit narrative content without added violence or endorsement. Rebekah's distress question in 25:22 remains a question, without a diagnosis or an invented definite explanation.

## 5. Sacrificial grammar must not smuggle in an interpretation

Tempt in Genesis 22:1 is a trial, using test rather than entice. Only son remains the source designation beside the already narrated Ishmael. Provide himself a lamb is rendered benefactively, provide for himself, with that choice documented; the main translation does not assert that God is the lamb. The substituted animal later named is a ram. In the mount it shall be seen keeps seen, even if a different translation might use provided. The source's omission of Isaac's name in the return clause is not turned into an invented death.

Blessing I will bless and multiplying I will multiply retain cognate repetition. The inherited multiply means become-many and therefore takes a causative when the deity multiplies descendants. Blessing a recipient differs from blessed-be addressed to God, rendered as praise. No new inflectional endings are needed for these distinctions.

## 6. Property, counts and extravagant formulas

Genesis 23 retains a purchased burial holding, not merely an individual tomb. The field, cave, trees, borders, public witnesses and four hundred shekels remain. Current money is merchant-accepted money, not present-day currency or flowing water. Shekel stays a source unit without invented grams. Genesis 24:22 keeps half a shekel, two bracelets and ten shekels separate; the pair's stated ten is not doubled into ten each.

Source threescore uses six-times-ten for sixty. Abraham's 175, Ishmael's 137, Isaac's marriage age 40 and fatherhood age 60 remain distinct. Hundredfold is a proportional yield, not an age. Thousands of millions in 24:60 keeps plural magnitudes as an extravagant blessing rather than being collapsed into a single exact headcount. The twelve named sons of Ishmael are checked separately from the source's twelve-princes statement.

## 7. False friends require sense decisions

Give up the ghost uses release-life-breath, not the inherited apparition word. Gathered to his people stays a separate ancestral formula rather than becoming the later burial action. Plain Jacob in 25:27 is plain or quiet manner of life, not an assertion of moral perfection; cunning hunter is skilled. Sod pottage is boiled food, not earth turf. Lighted off a camel is dismounting, not illumination. Meat before a guest means food, while venison names hunted game-meat. Right direction reuses the existing historical `kjv_right_side`; no duplicate root is coined because its key has an underscore.

Envy of another's possessions gets a distinct sense from possessive jealousy. Statutes, entrusted charge, commands and laws stay separately visible in the source list. Sporting with a wife remains intimate play without inventing the physical act. Stopped wells are physically blocked. The source's earring upon her face is left strange rather than silently modernized to a nose-ring or moved onto an ear.
