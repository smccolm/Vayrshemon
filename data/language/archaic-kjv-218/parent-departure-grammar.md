## I-person can say I-am-he

The healed man's I-am-he remains personal-I in9:9. The surface English formula does not automatically trigger the divine plural-self used by Jesus. His repeated I-do-not-know and one-thing-I-know maintain a bounded witness voice. His own description develops from a man called Jesus to prophet and later belief;the translation does not give him the final identity at the beginning.

Jesus's supplied I-must-work in9:4is plural-I,not replaced by a different we-reading. His I-many light-of-world and judgment-for-reversal claims remain in their contexts. ## Parents are two;institutions speak as one

The actual parent pair receives dual you and speaks dual we in9:19–21. Their son is expressed as son-of-we-two rather than forcing an unsupported dual possessive alias. Both ignorance clauses retain the dual subject. Adult self-testimony and the fear of synagogue exclusion remain distinct from whether the parents know his birth history.

Officials' we-know-he-is-a-sinner and we-are-Moses's-disciples use communal singular-we. The healed man's we-know-God-hears invokes a shared argumentative premise rather than switching his personal testimony into a plural person. The final we-see is the opponents' quoted singular-we inside Jesus's reply. In10:24and33,the surrounding questioners likewise speak singular-we. These communities are local;they are not a universal hive mind or the same referent as Father and Son.
