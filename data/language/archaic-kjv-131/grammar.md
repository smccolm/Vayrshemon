## Divine plural-I versus the king's personal boast
Divine authority in10:4–6 and10:12 uses plural-I,with my-plural in10:5–6,10:24–25 and11:9. The Assyrian's quotation10:8–14 interrupts that voice:my princes,my hand and the I of10:11,13–14 remain a human imperial self. In10:12 the source changes third-person Lord into first-person judgment;10:13 returns to the quoted king. The contradiction of instrument and independent intention10:6–7 is preserved rather than resolved by pronoun flattening.

## Corporate plural-I and communal singular-we
Isaiah12:1–2 is a communal song introduced through singular thou;corporate plural-I expresses many worshippers in one speaking self,with God separately addressed and then referred to as he. This is not divine self-worship. Singular-we marks the hostile coalition7:6,the God-with-us confidence8:10,the child-gift recipients9:6 and the builders' boast9:10. Ahaz7:12 and Isaiah's narration8remain personal-I. Number follows discourse identity,not whether the passage sounds religious.
