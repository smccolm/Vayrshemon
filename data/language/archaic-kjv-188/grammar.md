# KJV historical register 0.10-kjv.188 · Matthew 5–7

111 new verse analyses continue the inherited Matthew 1–4. The cumulative Matthew draft is 201 verses. Projected whole coverage after this checkpoint's publication and native verification is 23,346/31,102; 7,756 remain. This is an incomplete, permanently historical Bible, not a modernized language standard. All previous targets, including frozen Jonah, remain unchanged.

## Plural speaking self, singular shared prayer

Jesus's authoritative first person continues the chosen plural-I register: the repeated "I say unto you" and the later recognition/refusal use `ipl`; "my sake" and "my Father" use `mypl`. This does not pluralize Father, identify every speaker with Jesus, or insert a deity-person count. The hypothetical person removing a speck at 7:4 instead says personal `i`.

The taught prayer has `ourone/father`, `ourone/daily bread`, `ourone/debt:pl`, and `wesg` recipients and agents. Singular-we is communal identity, not one physical worshipper. Imagined anxiety at 6:31 and the claimants' collective defense at 7:22 also use singular-we; the latter is a reported claim, not narrator certification. No dual is introduced merely because two contrasted objects occur.

## Repetition as supplied rhetoric

Beatitudes, "but I say", already-held rewards, three asking promises and repeated "in thy name" remain. Natural variation must not erase deliberate source recurrence. Explicit parallel judgments, weather clauses and changing foundations retain their contrasts. The prior pronoun, morphology and Maxima paradigms are unchanged; no decorative alternatives are invented to disguise repetition.

## Imperative, optative and negative scope

Petitions use imperative or optative forms according to their supplied action: sanctified-name and coming-kingdom are wishes; give, forgive and deliver are direct petitions. "Lead us not into temptation" remains a negative request, followed by the full supplied doxology. Correlative neither/nor governs its coordinated phrase, including positive-shaped verbs within that negative scope. Vain repetition is qualified as vain, not a ban on all recurrence. Alms/prayer/fasting warnings retain purpose clauses about being seen, beside the earlier command to shine publicly.

## Source terms and explicit numerical distinctions

Eight common lexical entries are added: bushel, jot, tittle, farthing, fornication, publican, alms and mammon. Raca is a ninth, proper quoted-word entry. The script terms are source labels, not replacement Thimfora marks. Publican denotes tax collection; mammon retains the foreign wealth-master designation without a new biography. Fornication remains distinct from adultery without inventing a complete sexual code.

The ones attached to jot, tittle, least command, bodily member, hair and flower remain explicit, as do one/two miles, two masters and one cubit. First is ordinal priority. Indefinite "one" becomes person, while the difficult single-eye descriptor stays `single`, not numerical one, healthy or generous. Farthing remains an untranslated unit label; it is not assigned an invented currency conversion.

## Difficult images and lexical safeguards

Hell-fire uses the inherited punishment sense, distinct from earlier underworld-depth passages. Body-member becomes part of the body, since an eye is not a limb. Timber beam is not a ray of light. Physical tree and treasure corruption use the decay sense. Salt retains his-taste personification and the question of being re-salted. Sun remains sun. Rain, floods and winds strike structurally founded houses; the foundation is laid, not discovered. Food spinning becomes thread-twisting. Taking thought is worrying. The private closet is a chamber.

Severe body commands remain quoted teaching, without practical translator advice or softened replacement. Without-cause, divorce exception, supplied publicans reading, evil without a substituted named devil, stature rather than lifespan, and "because" before the narrow way all remain. Meaningful theological tension is not repaired by importing another Gospel.
