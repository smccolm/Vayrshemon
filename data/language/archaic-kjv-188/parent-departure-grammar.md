## The baptism scene without an inserted deity count

Jesus receives baptism from John3:13–16,the Spirit of God descends like a dove,and a voice from heaven calls him my beloved Son3:17. All three narrative roles remain in sequence. Like a dove is a comparison,not a forced assertion that the Spirit is literally a bird. Opened unto him/he saw preserves the stated viewpoint. The heavenly parental voice uses divine plural-I and my-pl beside singular Son. This is the register's person-grammar choice for the divine voice,not a numerical Trinity formula present in the English. No numeral three,three gods,or clinical personality label is introduced.

## Jesus calls as plural-self;John speaks personally

John's I say3:9,I baptize/mightier than I/unworthy to carry shoes3:11,and I need baptism3:14are personal-I. The angel's until I bring word2:13also remains personal-I because it is the messenger's own promised action. The adversary's I will give/worship me4:9is individual speech,not automatically divine or royal merely because he offers kingdoms. Herod's bring me word/I may worship2:8uses the established civic/royal plural-self. Jesus's follow me/I will make you fishers4:19is deliberately rendered with plural-self as authoritative divine commissioning in this register,following the preceding Son/Spirit/voice scene. That is a documented interpretive choice;English I does not itself mark a grammatical plural,and the translation does not claim a numerical definition of Jesus's personhood.
