# Historical KJV 0.10-kjv.74 — found book and unaverted judgment

This continuation authors 57 verses of 2 Kings 22–23. The 2 Kings working text now contains 669 verses; whole-Bible coverage is 10,251 of 31,102, with 20,851 remaining. This is an incomplete checkpoint toward the entire single-volume Bible. Sealed prior editions and frozen Jonah remain unchanged and excluded from modernization.

## Discovery, perception and the endangered whole

The priest's I-found-the-book in 22:8 and the scribe's delivered-me-a-book in 22:10 use personal I. These are named individual actions, not claims of divine or institutional plurality. The king's for-me inquiry in 22:13 also names a personal beneficiary beside the people and all Judah. The same speech then uses singular-we for the endangered whole and our-one for its ancestors. This is a structured change of perspective: separate beneficiaries become a collective facing wrath, without erasing the individuals.

Josiah's I-see question about the grave inscription in 23:17 is personal perception. The narrated assembly and covenant in 23:2–3 remain third-person narration. The target does not invent a spoken singular-we oath simply because a collective commitment would suit the language's pronoun system. Source repetition and source person remain meaningful constraints on natural variation.

## Huldah as human intermediary and the divine plural self

Huldah remains explicitly a woman prophet. Her husband's genealogy and wardrobe office are retained without relocating her authority to him. The delegation goes to her and speaks with her. The proximal to-me in 22:15 is read as the human intermediary receiving those who were sent, despite the surrounding oracle formula. This reading is documented rather than concealed: it does not identify Huldah with the divine speaker. The renewed thus-says-the-LORD in 22:16 introduces the explicit plural-I threat.

Divine plural-I and matching my-plural possession carry the forsaken-me, anger, hearing and gathering statements in 22:16–20 and the rejection of the chosen city in 23:27. The human messenger, king, collective beneficiaries and speaking deity remain distinct. The grammar imposes no numbered Trinity, no merger of prophets into the deity, and no first-person plural where the source gives only third-person action.

## Counting, accounting and compelled payment

Hilkiah must reckon the silver collected by door-keepers in 22:4. The later absence of reckoning with trusted work recipients in 22:7 concerns their accounting; it does not contradict or erase the earlier counting. Oversight and actual repair work remain distinguishable even though the source repeats doers-of-work language. Carpenters, builders, masons, timber and hewn stone remain in the inventory.

Josiah's accession age eight, reign thirty-one and eighteenth-year events remain separately checked. Second-order priests in 23:4 is rank, not a fabricated count of two priests; the two courts in 23:12 is a physical count. Jehoahaz's age twenty-three and three-month reign differ from Jehoiakim's age twenty-five and eleven-year reign. Pharaoh's assessment is one hundred silver talents and one gold talent. Jehoiakim demands each person's assessed share without an invented uniform rate. Exacting is demanding payment, not mathematical exactness; the new tax-assessment term is distinct from both collection and the whole land's tribute.

## Material and institutional senses

Six common entries distinguish wardrobe-store, the historical college locality, planet, chamberlain, identifying inscription and tax assessment. The college label is deliberately opaque about its exact institutional or district character. It is not the inherited physicians' college and not a modern university. Wardrobe is a clothing store under a keeper, not necessarily a freestanding cabinet. Chamberlain is an office and does not itself assert castration. Planet is a wandering celestial light in the source inventory, with no imported modern list or orbital classification.

The title on the tomb in 23:17 is a written identifying mark, not a rank or legal claim. Without Jerusalem means outside the city; as touching words means concerning them. The new verses use right-conduct for moral evaluation and right-side for physical direction. A separate contextual review has identified 20 earlier uses of the generic entitlement word right; 16 repairs and four legitimate retentions are queued for the next correction checkpoint. This chapter does not silently rewrite those sealed earlier texts.

## Reform, pollution and attributed categories

The narrative praises the reform while describing burning, pulverizing, grave desecration and killing. These actions remain explicit. The horses of the sun are taken away, while the chariots are burned; the text does not state the same fate for both. Priests are removed from role in 23:5, whereas 23:20 explicitly says they are slain upon the altars. The translation does not silently insert execution into the earlier verse or euphemize it in the later one.

The inherited opaque sodomite source-label remains without equating it to a modern sexual orientation, assigning a precise institutional role, or inventing unprinted acts. Women weave hangings for the grove. Children-of-the-people graves in 23:6 retains the supplied wording without deciding an unprinted age or caste. The high-place priests do not approach the Jerusalem altar in 23:9 but do eat unleavened bread among their brothers; their continued subsistence is not erased. The separate Ashtoreth, Chemosh and Milcom associations retain the narrator's abomination labels as attributed evaluations.

## Promise, superlative and unresolved outcome

The peaceful gathering to the grave promised in 22:20 remains beside the later Megiddo death and transport of the dead king in 23:29–30. No secret chronology or revised wording makes the difficulty disappear. The source's against-the-Assyrian-king phrase remains rather than being rewritten according to an external alliance reconstruction. Josiah's unprecedented turning to the LORD in 23:25 stays beside the earlier Hezekiah superlative; neither praise formula is deleted.

Most sharply, the LORD does not turn from wrath in 23:26 despite the reform. The following plural-I rejects the city and house it had chosen and named. That reversal remains part of the translated thought, not a defect to repair through comforting explanation. The prophet's Samaria provenance in 23:18 and the earlier proclamation are preserved without silently harmonizing their geography.

Similar forms do not automatically merge the governor with the conquest leader, the priest with Eliakim's father, the new Jehoahaz with Jehu's son, or Jeremiah with a later prophetic namesake.
