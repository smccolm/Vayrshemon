# KJV historical register 0.10-kjv.128 — Ecclesiastes 7–12

This continuation completes the 222 Ecclesiastes verses while the entire Bible remains incomplete. The supplied KJV is the source; these are authored historical forms, not a claim of independently attested antiquity. Previous editions and targets remain frozen. Translator self-review is documented; independent literary certification is not claimed.

## 1. The observer, quoted future self and communal conclusion

Personal-I continues the observer's inquiry in 7–10. In 12:1, the quoted “I have no pleasure” belongs to the future older addressee, not the Creator. The final “let us hear” in 12:13 takes singular-we, `wesg`, with optative hearing: the listeners participate as one communal recipient. This does not imply one physical person. No divine plural-I is newly introduced here; neither a change of speaker nor the mention of God licenses it. No new physical dual is asserted.

## 2. Contradictory valuations remain historical voices

Death's day over birth, mourning over feasting and sorrow over laughter in 7 remain beside mirth commended in 8:15, the living dog preferred to the dead lion in 9:4, and the invitation to joy in 11. The righteous dying and wicked living long in 7:15 remain beside 8:12–14's conflicting assertions. Vayrshemon records these awkward turns rather than adding a hidden sin, a secret reward or an explanatory doctrinal synthesis. “Not righteous over much” is not silently narrowed to “not self-righteous.”

## 3. Knowledge limits, chance and mortality

The failed aspiration “I will be wise” and repeated inability to find divine work remain finite, attributed claims. Race, battle, food, wealth and favor do not follow expected merit in 9:11; time and chance remain named without an inserted deity as the explanation. The dead know nothing in 9:5 and lose their share under the sun in 9:6. The question about spirit destinations in the inherited 3:21 and the spirit's return to God in 12:7 coexist. No afterlife gloss is added to resolve them.

## 4. Source choices and uncomfortable social claims

The supplied forgotten reading in 8:10, babbler in 10:11, grasshopper as burden in 12:5 and whole duty in 12:13 are retained. The one man among a thousand but no woman statement in 7:28 remains the speaker's attributed gendered claim, not a valid statistical conclusion asserted by the translation. Servants riding and princes walking, noble birth and child kings remain the source's status assumptions. The translation neither modernizes those assumptions nor adopts them as rules for the user.

## 5. Images are not forced anatomical or theological codes

The house keepers, grinders, window-lookers, music daughters, almond, grasshopper, silver cord, golden bowl, pitcher and wheel of 12 remain concrete images. An optional interpretation is not substituted into the verse as a decoded body part. “Or ever” means before. Long home is duration, not architectural length. One shepherd in 12:11 remains unnamed; the translation does not insert a divine identity or use the number to settle a doctrine. The bird carrying speech in 10:20 remains an image rather than an asserted surveillance mechanism.

## 6. Counts, sides and material senses

Ten mighty people, counting one by one, one man among a thousand, a hundred repeated offenses, one common event and one sinner keep their explicit quantities. Seven and eight in 11:2 are separately stated, not summed to fifteen. Generic human “one” remains distinct from cardinal `one_num`. Right/left in 10:2 use bodily sides. Swallow in 10:12 uses the inherited ingesting verb, not a bird label; split in 10:9 concerns timber. The dripping house in 10:18 is a leaking house, not a falling person. The new crackling term joins fire and noise; the new race term joins running and test to distinguish a contest from ancestry.

## 7. Negation, repeated objects and embedded speech

Outer negative existence in 7:20 describes the absence of a sinless righteous human; the inner negative remains on sin, not good action. Negated comparisons in 9:11 retain their multiple parallel subjects. Nominal case belongs on the head before an of-phrase: rebuke/song, reason/wickedness, works, and conclusion. The two lifetime-of-vanity phrases in 9:9 remain. The closing vanity formula12:8 occurs once, unlike the doubled opening1:2. Exclamatory land and youth addresses use the inherited morphological form rather than an unsupported exclamation glyph.
