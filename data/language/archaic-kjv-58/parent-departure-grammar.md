## 1. The king makes a house; the person makes a plea

Solomon's building declarations in8:13,20–21,27,43–44,48 use `ipl`: the royal person speaks as the commissioning and ruling body. This is not an assertion that multiple Solomons physically laid each stone. The enclosing personal genealogy **my father David** remains personal, alongside the institutional succession claim.

The actual supplicant in **I pray thee**8:26 and **my words, wherewith I have made supplication**8:59 takes personal `i` and `my`. A royal office does not automatically pluralize every inward or spoken act. The repeated third-person self-designation **thy servant** remains visible; it is not rewritten to uniform I. The grammar therefore permits personal petition and corporate agency in the same long speech.

In7:46 the narrator attributes casting to the king even though Hiram is the named craftsman. This is source attribution through commissioning, not an invented claim that Solomon personally operated the furnace. Hiram the craftsman has a distinct local identity record from Hiram the Tyrian king.

## 2. Nested divine quotations reset the centre

The divine speech in8:16 takes `ipl` and `mypl`: the plural-self brings my people from Egypt, chooses no city and chooses David. The embedded divine possessors in8:18–19,25,29 have the same divine centre. They are not the surrounding king's possessors. In8:25 **before me** is explicitly `before ipl`; in8:29 **my name shall be there** is a quoted divine claim.

The quotation boundaries matter more than a global find-and-replace. Solomon's enclosing my-father does not become God's father. The reported statement in8:12 **he would dwell** stays third person. Singular second-person thou remains the inherited direct-address form even when divine first-person self-reference is plural-I; person and discourse stance do not constitute a translator-supplied headcount.

This develops the requested plural-self language without inventing three persons where this passage does not name them. Future Trinity passages remain untranslated at this checkpoint; they will require their own local referent and quotation analysis.

## 3. We-singular can have plural hearts

The exiles' quoted confession in8:47 uses `wesg`: the covenant people speaks as one historical body admitting sin and perversity. This does not claim every person committed an identical offense or owns a single biological mind. In8:57 **with us / leave us / forsake us** likewise takes singular-we. The deity remains outside the human we as the addressed or petitioned agent.

`ourone` in8:21,40,53,57–59,61,65 marks the same covenant whole across ancestry, worship and narrator affiliation. **Our hearts** in8:58 deliberately retains plural heart morphology: a communal whole has members. **Every man the plague of his own heart**8:38 and individualized recompense8:39 are not absorbed into a faceless collective. Direct plural **your heart**8:61 and inclusive our-one can coexist.

The foreign stranger8:41–43 remains outside Israel, yet the prayer asks that his request be heard. Neither the outsider petition nor the exclusive covenant identity is removed to make a tidier philosophy. Plural distant hearers in8:42 become the individual arriving stranger without flattening both to one number.

## 4. Dwelling without containment remains uncomfortable

The settled dwelling forever in8:13 stands beside8:27's question whether God will indeed dwell on earth and the assertion that heaven-of-heavens cannot contain him. The house is a direction for prayer while heaven is the place of hearing. Name-presence does not silently become physical containment; neither does the translator modernize all the material language into a disembodied abstraction.

Mouth speaks, hand fulfills, arm stretches, eyes open to supplication and ears are implied in hearing. The eyes/hearing crossover in8:52 remains. Thick darkness, cloud and glory remain distinct source expressions without being assigned newly invented personal identities. The priests' inability to stand is not given a medical diagnosis. Solomon is introduced standing8:22 and ends rising from kneeling8:54; no unstated moment of posture change is manufactured.

The dynastic promise8:25 retains its conduct condition. Defeat, drought, sickness, siege, exile and return remain conditional prayer scenarios rather than already completed events. The confident no-word-failed statement8:56 remains the speaker's statement beside those possible catastrophes. The translator neither endorses nor edits away the theological claims.
