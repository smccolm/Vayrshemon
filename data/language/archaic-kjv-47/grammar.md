## Plural-I can act through a band without becoming a deity

David's inquiry about striking the Philistines in23:2 uses plural-I as expedition-self. The divine answer in23:4 also uses plural-I, but its named speaker and quotation boundary locate a different referent. Shared morphology does not identify David with the deity or count either as a specified number of persons.

In25:21–22 David's plural-self claims the band's protective work and threatens collective retaliation. His later gratitude for restraint returns to personal-I and my own hand. Saul similarly shifts from accompanying the informants as an individual to searching Judah through the broader force in23:23. These are changes of agency, not automatic majestic plurals attached to status.

## A frightened plurality and a promised whole are different

David's men use plural-we to testify to their fear before Keilah. The Ziphites' singular-we and ourone/part express a civic body's offer to deliver David. Neither forces an assertion that every inhabitant agreed or personally witnessed him. David's message to Nabal uses singular-we for the band's protective conduct, while the servant's later testimony uses plural-we for the individual shepherds who suffered no harm or loss.

Plural possessors identify the servants' shared master without making him a separate corporate person. The arriving messengers and the later marriage envoys use plural-we. Abigail's responsibility, plea and intervention remain personal-I. The language therefore distinguishes testimony, delegated representation, collective undertaking and individual accountability within one narrative.

## Responsibility can be assigned at different distances from the act

Saul orders the priests killed; his servants refuse; Doeg performs the killing. Abiathar reports that Saul slew them, assigning responsibility to the commanding agent. David then says he occasioned their deaths, assigning himself consequential responsibility without claiming to wield the weapon. All these clauses remain, rather than choosing one supposedly correct agent for every description.

Saul's accusation explicitly names the priest and Jesse's son, licensing dual-you. Ahimelech's defense refers to David's apparent royal status and denies knowledge of the affair. The supposed ambush and conspiracy remain accusations, not narrator-certified discoveries. The household's execution,85 ephod wearers and Nob's women, children, sucklings and animals remain in the source scope without invented individual guilt or graphic elaboration.

## Predicted surrender is not narrated surrender

The first Keilah inquiry asks both about surrender and Saul's descent; the answer initially addresses descent. A second question includes David and his men, while the answer retains singular thee. David then leaves with about600 men, and Saul refrains from setting out. The predictions and the changed course coexist without inventing a completed siege or betrayal.

Saul's claim that God has delivered David into his hand contrasts with the narrator's later statement that God did not deliver him. Jonathan's prediction of his own place beside David remains his future claim, not a guarantee supplied by the translator. The men in the cave cite a promise of an enemy's delivery; their application is attributed, and no missing prior oracle is fabricated to certify it.

## The hand can protect, threaten, refrain and ask vengeance

The chapters repeatedly use hand for agency and power. Jonathan strengthens David's hand in God. David's eye spares Saul, his heart strikes him, and he refuses his own hand while asking the LORD to avenge him. Personal restraint is therefore not translated as a general rejection of vengeance.

Abigail speaks as though divine restraint is already secured while intervening in an armed approach. David accepts the intervention and credits both her advice and the LORD. Her praise that evil has not been found in him all his days remains her persuasive speech beside the narrated threat. The translator neither erases the threat nor silently converts her praise into an independent moral verdict.

## Bodily idioms remain awkward and intelligible

Cover his feet is retained as the bodily euphemism in24:3. A primary translator note identifies it as relieving oneself; the literal historical image remains in the target rather than becoming ordinary footwear or receiving graphic expansion. The wall-urinating expression in25:22 and34 keeps its crude male-marking image. The odd sanction against the enemies of David is not silently changed to David himself. The bundle of life and slung-out souls remain physical metaphors without a fabricated anatomy of souls or divine person-count. Nabal's heart dying, stone-like condition and death about ten days later retain separate clauses without a modern diagnosis.

## Names and words do not force false identities

New entries distinguish Gad the prophet from the ancestor/tribe, Laish the person from the place, and Ahinoam of Jezreel from Saul's wife. Each shares the inherited name form but has a separate semantic identity and cited local relationship. Other new proper entries retain source spellings and relationships without invented ancient etymologies. Adullam's existing place-name is reused with cave explicitly stated.

Flea receives an explicitly authored root; raisins are dry-grapes and fruit-clusters are grouped fruit portions. Invade is war-enter, distinct from ordinary entering. Handmaid is woman-servant, with context distinguishing an actual servant from deferential self-reference; it does not automatically assert enslavement. Existing covenant replaces the wrong distance-unit sense of league; stronghold replaces holding, terrain-plain replaces unadorned plain, and judicial case/plea replaces causal cause. Nabal's association with folly is retained in the speech without pretending the borrowed name is an attested Vayrshemon folly-root.

## Exact quantities preserve approximation and category

About400 initial followers and about600 at Keilah remain distinct approximate counts. Thousands and hundreds in Saul's patronage speech, and Judah's thousands in the search, remain plural magnitudes or groupings.85 ephod wearers is the supplied fourscore and five. The3000 pursuers are not automatically the same selected3000 of an earlier chapter.

Nabal's3000 sheep and1000 goats differ from the10 messengers and the about400 attackers with200 baggage guards. The latter does not yield an asserted exact600 total. Abigail's inventory remains200 loaves,2 wine bottles,5 prepared sheep,5 unnamed measures of grain,100 raisin clusters and200 fig cakes. No ephahs or modern capacities are invented. About10 days stays approximate, and five damsels are accompanying women, not five wives.
