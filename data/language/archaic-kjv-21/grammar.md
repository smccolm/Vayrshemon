# Historical KJV 0.10-kjv.21 · suspected bodies, vowed time and a travelling whole

This version continues the pinned v20 historical language. Numbers 5–10 adds 232 authored verses; the entire Bible remains incomplete. Earlier targets and frozen Jonah remain untouched. No modernization is authorized. These are translator-authored constructions and self-review, not an assertion of independently certified equivalence.

## One petition, one travelling body, several distinct speakers

The corpse-defiled men in 9:7 speak as singular-we: multiple persons make one petition concerning shared exclusion. Moses answers as ordinary singular-I in 9:8, an individual waiting to hear a command. In 10:29 Moses speaks for the travelling whole as singular-we, but his embedded quotation of the LORD uses plural-I. Hobab's personal refusal in 10:30 is singular-I. In 10:31 the spokesman's personal I pray remains beside corporate us/we. In 10:32 singular-we promises to share what the deity gives. This alternation is discourse-driven, not a universal substitution for English I or we.

The user-directed plural-I remains an extension, not an invented row in the original pronoun chart. The chart supplies singular-we. Divine first-person ownership in 5:3, 6:27, 8:14–19 and 10:10 uses the inherited plural-self. Third-person references and second-person address to the deity retain their persons. A voice, two cherubs and human thousands do not count divine persons.

## Suspicion does not establish the woman's guilt

The jealousy procedure explicitly permits suspicion when the woman is defiled or when she is not defiled (5:14). The oath retains both conditional branches. The final allocation of liability remains asymmetric: the man is free and the woman carries iniquity. The source is not rewritten into a reciprocal modern tribunal. Lack of witnesses and not being caught in the act remain explicit. Taken with the manner is interpreted as caught in the act, not a mannerism.

Thigh rotting, belly swelling, inward parts and conceiving seed preserve the supplied bodily language. No modern disease diagnosis, pharmacological explanation or silently substituted miscarriage is asserted. Water and tent-floor dust are the named ingredients. The curse-causing label is the text's ritual claim. Amen is said twice. Drinking occurs in the source description before and again after the offering; both clauses remain instead of being silently edited into a smooth procedure.

## Restitution and remembrance have material distinctions

The original liability plus one fifth is six fifths of the principal; principal is an amount, not a chief person. If no recipient kinsman exists, repayment goes to LORD/priest besides the atonement ram. The barley gift is one tenth of an ephah measure, without oil or frankincense. An offering of remembrance is not automatically the material burnt memorial portion: the handful in 5:26 is explicitly that portion. Blotting out written curses uses the erase verb, not a noun meaning stain. Earthen vessels receive a specific material sense, not the world/territory sense of earth.

## Vowed separation can lose elapsed time

Either man or woman can take the Nazarite vow. The word is derived from separation plus vow, not from Nazareth. The restrictions cover wine, strong drink, their vinegars, grape liquid, fresh and dried grapes, kernels and husks. Liquor does not imply distilled spirits. Hair locks are tresses, not fastenings; razor is a hair-cutting implement.

Sudden accidental nearby death can invalidate the accumulated days. The source calls the corpse-related condition sin even though intention was not specified. Prior days are lost; the vow is recommenced, not automatically cancelled forever. Family corpse contact is prohibited here despite ordinary-priest exceptions elsewhere. Completion involves the specified animals, bread, shaving and burning hair under the peace sacrifice. Wine becomes permitted again afterward. The source's additional what-his-hand-gets clause remains without a fabricated fixed surcharge.

## A blessing can address the community as one

The priestly formula in 6:24–26 addresses thee while naming LORD in the third person. It may address the gathered community as a whole without inventing a first-person we clause. The concluding 6:27 returns to the deity's my-plural name and plural-I blessing. One name belongs to a plural-self; this does not require the text to supply several names. Face shining and lifted countenance retain bodily imagery.

## Repeated gifts are translated, not replaced by ditto

Numbers 7 contains twelve full donor lists in camp order. Each day's leader, father, tribe and ordinal remains explicit. Each repeated verse has an actual target construction and exact source; no ditto or prose summary substitutes for a list. Charger is a presentation dish, not a horse or electrical device. Dedication inaugurates the altar and is not automatically the irrevocable-devotion property category of Leviticus 27.

Six wagons and twelve oxen are divided two/four to Gershon and four/eight to Merari; Kohath receives none because its burden is carried on shoulders. Each donor's silver is 130 + 70 shekels, yielding 2400 across twelve; gold is ten each, yielding 120. Burnt animals total twelve each, sin kids twelve; peace animals total twenty-four bullocks and sixty each of rams, male goats and first-year lambs. The source in-the-day phrase remains beside the twelve days and after-anointing conclusion. The final voice comes from above the mercy-seat between two cherubs; final he/him is not disambiguated by invention.

## Different service ages remain different

Numbers 8:24 gives twenty-five as the entry age, while 4:3 gives thirty. Both are preserved. No unprinted five-year apprenticeship reconciles them. At fifty the source ends doing service but still permits ministering with brethren to keep charge; the awkward service/assistance distinction remains without a modern retirement policy. Levites are presented as people for service, while the bullocks are offered for the specified sacrificial purposes. The language of people wholly given to plural-I and then given to Aaron remains materially uncomfortable.

## Delay, signals and guidance preserve their conditions

Numbers 9 retains its first-month setting even though it follows a second-month census. Corpse impurity or distant travel permits Passover on the second month's fourteenth evening, with the same no-leftovers/no-broken-bone requirements. A clean non-traveller's failure has a different penalty. Stranger and land-born share one ordinance. Cloud stays of two days, a month or a year are alternatives, not a summed duration; departure can occur by night.

Two trumpets call the whole assembly; one calls chiefs. Alarm differs from the non-alarm assembly signal. Named eastern and southern departure signals are retained without inventing missing western/northern calls. Rereward means rear guard. Tent framework travels ahead of the covered sacred objects so it can be ready at their arrival. Human guidance by Hobab remains beside divine cloud guidance. The ark's ahead position in 10:33 remains beside the detailed march sequence. Raguel/Hobab and the father-in-law apposition are preserved without forced harmonization with earlier naming.
