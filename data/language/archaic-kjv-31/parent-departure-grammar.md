# Household I, shared we, curses and return · 0.10-kjv.30

Working continuation, Deuteronomy 25–30: 181 individually authored verses. Earlier targets, frozen Jonah and the approved typography remain unchanged. No new inflectional endings. Whole Bible incomplete. Recorded interpretations are authored translation choices and self-review, not independent literary certification.

## A household's many-self within the people's one-we

The firstfruits declaration in 26 uses `ipl`/`mypl` for the household represented by the person carrying the basket. This is an explicit authored perspective, supported by the house and dependant recipients in 26:11, not a claim that the English I is grammatically plural. The wider historical recital switches to `wesg`/`ourone`: Egypt mistreats us-one, God hears our-one voice and brings us-one out. The offered gift returns to the household's I-plural in 26:10,13–14, while the closing prayer for land and people uses singular-we. The ancestral father becoming a populous nation remains literal and strange. This representative voice is not automatically applied to every ordinary speaker.

## Personal refusals, mediation and divine interruptions

The widow's my-husband and the refusing brother's I in 25 remain personal. Moses' instructional I remains singular, including 27:1 where elders accompany him. In 28:20, the source suddenly says thou-hast-forsaken-me inside an address otherwise speaking of LORD in third person; this becomes divine `ipl` without an invented quotation label. 29:5–6 similarly uses divine plural-I for forty-year leading and explicit I-am-LORD. The people's warfare and allocation in 29:7–8 return to singular-we; 29:14's covenant-making I is the human mediator. The self-excusing offender in 29:19 uses personal I, not a divine or automatically corporate many-self. Speaker, scope and quotation level govern the forms.

## Exclamatory curses and impossible wishes

The repeated curses in 27 use `curse:psv:opt:excl`; assent remains `amen`. The form carries invoked force without the unsupported exclamation-mark glyph. These ritual repetitions are not stylistically diversified. In 28:67 the morning wish for evening and evening wish for morning use `be:opt:excl`; the supplied Would God formula is a wish, not an extra assertion that God has spoken. Future threatened curses in 28 use passive future, distinct from the optative curse ceremony. Passive predicates do not need a second redundant finite be.

## Bounded punishment, name succession and weights

Forty blows is a maximum, not an instruction to give exactly forty in every case. The punished person is still brother; the justification clause is rendered as a declaration without guilt, avoiding an overwide compound without shrinking the approved type. The widow's public complaint, shoe removal, spitting and named household disgrace remain distinct from capital punishment. Firstborn succession preserves the deceased brother's name. Honest weights and measures contrast with deceptive large/small sets; the text does not ban every legitimate size. Amalek's weak rear victims and remember-to-erase tension remain as source historical claims, without modern ethnic mapping.

## Mirrored abundance and loss

City/field, basket/store, coming/going and fruit of body/land/livestock recur in blessing and curse. Their exact scope is preserved, including items absent from a later parallel. In 28:7 the enemy arrives one way and flees seven; in 28:25 the addressed people do so. Lender/head/above becomes borrower/tail/below. Possession of house, vineyard and children loses its benefit. None of these repetitions is replaced with arbitrary synonyms merely to simulate natural variation; ritual rhetoric is part of this historical register.

## Affliction names without modern diagnoses

Consumption is flesh-consume, fever body-heat, blasting field-dry, mildew field-rot. Botch and emerods retain authored historical source-label forms; no one modern disease is assigned. Itch is skin-scratch, grope touch-search. Inflammation retains the earlier recorded sense. Sword remains sword rather than an alternate textual reading of drought. Madness, trembling, heart astonishment and failing sight are source descriptions of suffering, not clinical diagnoses and not definitions of plural-I. Wonderful plagues are extraordinary calamities, not pleasant admiration. The unwritten-sickness clause prevents treating the named list as exhaustive.

## Siege, captivity and the unsoftened deity

The source's siege cannibalism threats remain terse and complete, without added gore or spectacle. The supplied young-one phrasing is preserved rather than exchanged for an alternate translation. The deity rejoices in destruction as earlier in good; this is not softened into regret. Return to Egypt by ship remains beside the earlier no-return word, and sold-with-no-buyer remains a deliberate source tension. `kjv_captivity` is prison-state. The divine personification of the invading nation and abrupt he shifts remain recorded rather than supplied with invented rulers.

## Bitter roots, absent members and cosmic witnesses

29:18 keeps man/woman/family/tribe as possible defection scopes. Gall is bitter growth, distinct from a body organ; wormwood keeps a bitter-grass authored label without precise species claims. Present and absent members are included in the oath without restricting absent to a guessed generation. The source not-given-heart-to-perceive remains beside its you-have-seen assertion. Secret things belong to deity; revealed things to the covenant singular-we and its children. Calling heaven and earth to record means testify, not operate a recording device. Source deictics this-day are not rewritten into a modern chronology.

## Return, heart cutting and the nearby word

In 30:6 God circumcises the people's heart, while 10:16 commanded the people to circumcise it. Preserve the different agency. Compassion, gathering, ancestral land and renewed joy remain conditional on turning/hearing in the stated clauses. Curses transfer to enemies rather than disappearing into universal reconciliation. The singular-we asks for no messenger to heaven or across sea: the word is already mouth/heart-near. The deity is life and length of days, not merely a provider of extra life. These literal relations preserve the requested philosophical awkwardness without pretending the translator has reconciled them.
