## One pledged people, many external gods, personal mediator

The communal demand in 32:1 and its quotation in 32:23 use singular-we: one people seeking a leader. Their external plural gods remains a noun plural, distinct from divine plural-I. The one manufactured calf beside the plural gods claim remains deliberately incongruous. Aaron's personal collection, excuse and appeal use ordinary I. ## Possessive dispute and responsibility

The deity's thy people whom thou broughtest in 32:7 is returned by Moses to the deity in 32:11, followed by the narrator's his people in 32:14. These possessives carry an argument over belonging and responsibility. In 33:15–16 personal me/I becomes singular-we for I and thy people as one traveling body. In 34:9 personal petition becomes our-whole iniquity and sin, asking that us-whole be taken as inheritance. The singular collective does not erase its individual members. Embedded quotations retain their own speaker perspective, especially 32:13 and 33:12.

## Quantities, accounting and grammatical number

Numeral products use + and sums use and. The silver account gives 603,550 contributors at half a sanctuary shekel, totaling 301,775 shekels. One hundred talents plus 1,775 shekels implies 3,000 shekels per talent within this account; that is a validation inference, not an extra sentence inserted into the verse. Gold is 29 talents and 730 shekels; brass 70 talents and 2,400 shekels. Repeated amounts retain their units. Hundred sockets from hundred talents gives one talent each. First month, second year, first day in 40:17 preserves separate calendar fields.
