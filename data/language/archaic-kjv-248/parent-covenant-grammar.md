# KJV 0.10-kjv.4 — embodied covenant, mediated agency and quoted selves

This working continuation inherits the exact 0.10-kjv.3 historical identity register. It does not modernize frozen Jonah. The whole Bible remains the single deliverable; Genesis 16–20 are internal work units. Source English, names, numbers and difficult assertions remain visible beside the deliberately strained translation.

## 1. A messenger's I is not automatically a divine I

Genesis 16:10 gives the Name-lord's messenger an unqualified first-person multiplication promise. The chosen reading voices the commissioned divine promise as `ipl`; the note states that decision and does not name the messenger as a particular Trinitarian person. Genesis 19:21–22 keeps ordinary singular I for an individually replying messenger, whose stated inability to act before Lot's arrival is preserved. A difference in discourse role licenses the difference in pronoun; random stylistic variation does not.

The reader must still distinguish sender, emissary, recipient and quotation. Third-person he stays third-person even when the quoted speaker chooses internally plural I. Genesis 19:24 retains both occurrences of LORD, one as raining agent and the other as heavenly source. That is a real source tension to keep; it is not permission to insert Father and Son as names.

## 2. One agency, multiple participants

The two messengers speak as ordinary `wepl` about their lodging in Genesis 19:2, and as `wesg` about the common destructive commission in 19:13. Singular-we is one acting body; the sender remains distinct from that body. The three men of Genesis 18 remain three men. The narration's they/he alternation is not rewritten to prove a systematic theology.

Abimelech's us in 20:9 uses `wesg` for king and kingdom affected as one polity. His following I marks his personal grievance. The sisters' we in 19:32–34 stays `wepl`: shared planning is followed by separate individual actions and an explicit I/thou contrast. A collective identity must not erase individual responsibility or coerce all plurality into one category.

## 3. Embedded human speech retains its own deictic center

The I quoted by the divine speaker in Genesis 18:13 is Sarah's I and remains ordinary singular. The divine I resumes as `ipl` in 18:14. Compile and review speech layers rather than globally replacing the word I. Abraham, Hagar, Sarah, Lot and Abimelech retain singular personal I in their own statements.

Source ye/you versus thou/thy distinctions matter. Genesis 17:9–13 shifts from the singular patriarch to plural participants and back. Use explicit `you:pl` when the addressees are plural. An unchanged possessive your follows its established paradigm; the local addressee anchors number. These constructions add no person-agreement suffix to every verb.

## 4. Name changes are historical events

Abram and Abraham, Sarai and Sarah are separate dictionary identities with distinct source forms. Prior narration retains the earlier forms. The renamed forms begin where the source uses them. Ishmael, Isaac, Moab and Benammi stay proper names; explanatory associations in the source are translated in their clauses rather than replacing names with etymologies. Unto this day retains the source narrator's horizon, not the current software date.

## 5. Awkward bodies and ritual remain bodies and ritual

Foreskin and circumcise receive explicit anatomical/ritual entries; they are not euphemized into abstract membership. Flesh, wombs closed, women's manner, eyes covered and heart/hand integrity remain deliberately embodied. Eight-day circumcision, ages 99/100/90/13 and twelve princes are exact source quantities. The narrator's current age and a speaker's projected age are not harmonized.

Know{sexual} shares the inherited know form but has an explicit sense record. It does not assert consent. Threatened assault, Lot's offer, intoxication and incest are translated as the source describes them without erotic detail, approval or invented consent. Bought people remain bought people rather than being silently converted into employees. The historical register is allowed to be philosophically alien and uncomfortable; semantic negligence is not its style.

## 6. Literal spatial and culinary senses

Before a person in these scenes means presence, not temporal priority. `kjv_presence` exposes face-place as an archaic relation. Before they lay down remains temporal. Well is a water source, maid is a female servant, meal is ground grain, fine is fine-ground texture, dress is prepare-food, waxed is become, quarter is a part of the city, suffered-not is did-not-allow, and fast-closed is securely closed.

Use inherited historical senses where already available: kindness is benevolence, not species; overthrow is the earlier cast-down root; ashes are fire-dust. A new sense should not be coined merely because its prefixed dictionary key was initially overlooked. Dictionary additions are explicit records, not random unknown-word fallbacks.

## 7. Causation, wishes and the native exclamative

The inherited multiply verb means become-many. A speaker multiplying another's seed-line therefore takes the causative before the finite ending: `kjv_multiply:caus:fu`. Likewise causing another to drink distinguishes causer, drinker and wine. Keep all participants explicit when the source names them.

Genesis 17:18 and 19:20 combine the optative with the inherited final exclamative ending: `live:opt:excl`, `escape:opt:excl`. The archaic text can exclaim without a Thimfora exclamation-mark glyph. No new inflectional endings or encoding changes are introduced. Source English retains its punctuation independently.

## 8. Questions, repetition and analytical alternatives

Hagar's difficult seeing question, the time-of-life promise and Sarah's eye-covering reproach keep their source opacity with verse notes. Questions stay questions; repeated words and titles remain where they carry the old register. The negotiation in Genesis 18 preserves 50, 45, 40, 30, 20 and 10, including repeated quantities and the 50-minus-5 formulation.

An overlong Name-lord/angel compound is expressed with the inherited analytical `of` construction. This is a syntactic alternative preserving meaning and approved 50-point type, not a font-size reduction or arbitrary glyph split. It also gives the historical register a mix of fused possession and heavy analytical relations. Four-column paragraph alignment remains purely typographical and does not rewrite the translation.
