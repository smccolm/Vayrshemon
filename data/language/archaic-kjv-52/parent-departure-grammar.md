# KJV historical register 0.10-kjv.51 — responsibility, performed voices and broken households

This incomplete continuation adds130 individually authored verses,2Samuel11–14. Second Samuel now has367 translated verses; the Bible has8,438 of31,102, leaving22,664. The goal is still one entire Bible in one `.vay`, not a series of finished excerpts. Parent0.10-kjv.50 and all previously corrected targets remain unchanged; frozen Jonah remains untouched. The historical Bible is permanently excluded from modernization.

Eighteen lexical entries are added: twelve proper-name/identity records and six common entries. There are no new inflectional endings or possessive aliases. The awkward register develops through contextual combinations of existing forms, not random morphology or blanket first-person replacement. The reviews and back-readings are translator self-review, not independent certification.

## 1. A plural self does not dissolve individual responsibility

Nathan's quoted divine speech uses `ipl` in12:7–12 for anointing, rescue, gifts and threatened public retribution. **Thou art the man** still addresses David personally. The oracle says David killed Uriah with the Ammonites' sword: direct culpability and mediated physical action coexist. The translation neither puts the weapon into David's hand nor excuses him because other people performed the killing.

David's **I have sinned** in12:13 is personal `i`. It does not share the confession across a convenient royal collective. Bathsheba's **I am with child**, Uriah's refusal of private comforts and David's grief over the child likewise retain individual voices. Joab's battle report and concern for name-credit use `ipl`/`mypl` in12:27–28. Absalom's spoken signal in13:28 is personal `i`; his **have not I commanded you?** invokes `ipl`, the commanding household agency. This distinction exposes the relationship between a person and the acts done through that person's people.

## 2. Singular-we, plural witnesses and an actual dual

The military messenger's `wesg` in11:23 represents the army as one reporting body. David's `wesg` in13:25 is the proposed visiting court as a single burden on its host. Absalom's `wesg` in13:26 is his own hosted party, a different referent after the speaker change. In the Tekoah woman's performed story,14:7 gives the avenging family a singular-we voice demanding the heir. The personal widow's remaining coal stands against that corporate claim.

David's servants deliberating in12:18 use `wepl` and **voice of we(pl)**. The woman's **we must die** in14:14 uses plural persons, preserving individual mortality rather than inventing an undying collective. Her two sons in14:6 are explicitly two and subsequently `they:du`, including the dual object of separate. Dual is licensed by the actual pair; it is not pressed onto every small group.

## 3. Source agency and sexual coercion remain explicit, without elaboration

David sees Bathsheba washing, learns whose wife she is, sends messengers and takes her. Her ordinary washing is not assigned an intention to attract his gaze. The translation does not invent consent, resistance, seduction or an unstated age. The purification statement uses the bodily/ritual impurity entry, not the inherited morally corrupt adjective. Her first-person pregnancy report, mourning for Uriah and the narrator's condemnation of David's deed remain distinct.

Tamar's refusal in13:12 is unequivocal. Amnon's refusal to listen, superior strength and coercion remain in13:14, tersely and non-graphically. Her compliance with a food-preparation instruction does not become sexual consent. Her proposed appeal to the king is speech under threat, not narrator confirmation that a marriage would be lawful or permitted. The comparison in her later protest belongs to her social predicament and does not excuse the assault.

Absalom's command to keep quiet is attributed to him. The translation does not adopt it as advice. Tamar remains desolate in his house, with no invented recovery, diagnosis or royal punishment to improve the account. The new personal-desolation sense shares the older desolate form but is explicitly distinguished from a landscape emptied of inhabitants.

## 4. Reports, plans and performances do not become narrator facts

Joab's imagined royal rebuke in11:20–21 remains anticipated speech. The messenger's actual report follows, without inserting an exchange that the narrator does not give. David's **the sword devours one as well as another** is his minimizing response; the final divine displeasure in11:27 is not erased by it.

In13:30 the report that every royal son has died is false information inside the story. The preceding account has already shown the others flee, and Jonadab corrects the report to Amnon alone. Jonadab's explanation of Absalom's long-standing plan stays his claim; the translator does not invent how he knew it.

The Tekoah woman is explicitly recruited to feign mourning, with Joab putting words into her mouth. Her widow, sons and avenging family are therefore preserved as performed speech, not certified biography. Her analogy, flattery of the king's angel-like discernment and liability-transfer proposal are her arguments. David later identifies Joab's hand and she admits it. None of those claims is promoted to a universal rule of Vayrshemon grammar or morality.

## 5. Grief, pardon and consequences retain their unresolved edges

The rich man's lamb story keeps its daughter-like animal, the traveler's meal, David's death judgment and fourfold restitution. Nathan's accusation exposes David without a rewritten legal reconciliation of those two responses. The oracle's gift-of-wives wording, threat of public harm and the supplied explanation about enemies blaspheming are retained rather than replaced with a preferred theology.

David receives a no-death assurance, yet the child is struck and dies. No invented infant wrongdoing supplies an excuse. David's washing, anointing, worship and eating after the death are preserved alongside the servants' puzzlement. His **I shall go to him; he shall not return to me** is not expanded into a detailed afterlife location or doctrine. Solomon and Jedidiah remain two names in the subsequent naming account; the earlier child is not erased by the new birth.

## 6. Historical senses and the six common additions

`kjv_saw` is a tooth-cut-instrument, `kjv_harrow` an earth-tooth-instrument and `kjv_brick_kiln` a brick-furnace. These retain12:31's supplied under/through wording without adding graphic procedures or silently substituting a different labor interpretation. `kjv_poll_hair` is hair-cut, the historical poll at14:26, not voting or enumeration. `kjv_bolt_door` is bar-shut, distinct from a projectile or sudden flight. `kjv_desolate_person` explicitly records the personal condition described of Tamar, sharing the older desolate form without a landscape or medical claim.

Existing entries do necessary work: `kjv_foodportion` for mess of meat, `kjv_worship` for devotional reverence rather than a social title, `kjv_impurity_ritual`, `kjv_talent_weight`, `kjv_shekel`, `kjv_sole`, `kjv_ashes`, `kjv_charcoal`, `kjv_ail` and `kjv_avenger`. Dress a lamb means prepare it; press a petitioner means urge; long for Absalom means desire, not duration. Crown of the head is top, while the captured royal crown remains crown. The negative optative with displease avoids an obscure double negation.

## 7. Persons, names and places are kept distinct

Nine newly written source names are Bathsheba, Eliam, Uriah, Jerubbesheth, Jedidiah, Jonadab, Shimeah, Tekoah and Baalhazor. Jerubbesheth retains the supplied form beside the earlier Jerubbaal; the Abimelech recalled atThebez uses the Gideon-family identity, not an earlier namesake king. Rabbah reuses the already distinguished eastern city entry, while the Judah inventory's separate Rabbah remains separate.

Three new identity records share existing forms: Tamar daughter of David, Tamar daughter of Absalom and Ammihud father of Talmai of Geshur. The two Tamars in these chapters cannot become one person, nor can either be collapsed into Genesis Tamar. Talmai continues the previously identified Geshur ruler. No invented etymology or modern geographic identification is used to fill these entries.

## 8. Quantity, timing and access after exile

The audit preserves two men in one city, a single female lamb, fourfold repayment, the seventh day, two cakes and the distinct intervals of two full years before the killing, three years in Geshur and two full years in Jerusalem. The performed widow's pair is retained by both the explicit two and the following dual pronoun. Absalom's three sons and one named daughter remain alongside the twice-ignored summons to Joab.

The captured crown's talent of gold and the annual hair weight of200 shekels by the king's standard remain unconverted historical measures. No modern mass or correction is invented. Absalom's return to Jerusalem does not equal access to the king's face. His coercive burning of Joab's field is explicitly ordered and performed, without extra operational detail. The final kiss is kept as a gesture; no unspoken formula of pardon is added.
