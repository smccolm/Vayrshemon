# Historical KJV 0.10-kjv.63 — contextual repair of human one

This is a correctness revision of the archaic translation, not modernization. It adds no source verses or lexical entries, changes no paradigm, and does not alter the frozen Jonah edition. The reviewed corpus remains 9,582 of 31,102 verses, complete through 1 Kings plus Jonah; the entire Bible is still incomplete.

## Human reference is a lexical constraint

The inherited key one denotes a generic human person. It works for an unnamed messenger, a household member, an anointed human, a child in little ones, and a human reciprocal or distributive expression. It is not an English indefinite article and cannot be inserted before any count noun. All 203 identified earlier passages were read in their supplied source and target contexts. Of those verses, 156 retain their analyses; 47 require corrections, comprising 49 phrase replacements. The checked review records include the reason and before/after target and gloss for every case.

## Cardinal unity and count contrasts

Use one_num where unity, a selected member or a minimal count is the meaning: one side, one city, one hair, one word, one ewe lamb, one full cord, one priestly office, one of the proposed things. Human referents can also require cardinal marking: three sons and one daughter (2 Samuel 14:27) and the many hearts likened to one man's heart (19:14). Conversely, a human member of a set can retain generic one when person-reference is the function. This is contextual analysis, not a global search-and-replace rule.

## Nonhuman anaphora and bare indefinites

Repeat an overt head when it carries the reference most clearly: little city in Genesis19:20 and animal in the patterned livestock descriptions of30:33,35. A bare noun handles the source's indefinite oak, pit, prison-place, season, tree, dream, error or abomination without inserting a person or a newly asserted quantity. Genesis44:20 keeps little one for the child and removes a redundant second human classifier. Some day in1Samuel27:1 preserves the indefinite feared future, rather than predicting exactly one remaining day.

## Distribution, groups and the unseen speaker

Use each where allocation ranges over animals or territorial inheriting groups. Numbers15:12 distributes the preceding offering prescriptions over animals;26:54 and35:8 distribute by counted membership or inherited territory;36:9 distinguishes one tribe from each tribe. Numbers7:89 does not classify the voice from the mercy seat as human: referential he preserves the unnamed speaking source without adding an identity, count or modern theological harmonization. This complements the distinct heavenly speakers in1Kings22 rather than forcing all nonhuman voices into plural-I.
