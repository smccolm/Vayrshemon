# Historical KJV 0.10-kjv.34 · A many-self asks for land

Joshua 13–18 adds 167 authored verses, bringing Joshua to 470 and the whole incomplete Bible to 6370/31102. The 189 new entries comprise 183 names/referent distinctions and six common senses. No inherited target, affix, pronoun paradigm or frozen Jonah is changed. The register remains deliberately archaic, awkward and permanently excluded from modernization.

## I am a great people

Joshua 17:14 explicitly makes the children of Joseph say me and I am a great people. This is a strong source-grounded use of `ipl`: a self containing many people speaks as I. It is neither an ordinary individual spokesman nor a divine-only privilege. The analysis keeps two distinct one-quantifiers—one allotted lot and one portion—and asks why that is all this many-self receives. Its blessing also belongs to plural-self I.

In 17:16 the same descendants change to us. The target uses `wesg`, the community as a singular whole needing sufficient territory. Thus the discourse moves from internally multiple I to a shared single we without changing the named community. It realizes the user's requested identity contrast rather than mechanically making every plural subject plural-I. It asserts no clinical condition and introduces no unstated numerical trinity.

## Singular address to the many-self

Joshua answers the collective with singular thou (17:15,17–18), even while naming Ephraim and Manasseh. That asymmetry remains: many within the speaking self, one addressee in the reply. Great people is a predicate, not an adjective changing the number of the verb. The house of Joseph can contain two named branches and still receive one address. The target preserves source contradictions in power and need: great power, insufficient hill, iron chariots and promised ability to expel coexist.

The daughters' claim in 17:4 instead uses `wepl`. They are actual co-claimants requesting inheritance among their brethren. Narrated grant among their father's brethren has a different possessive scope and remains explicit. Shared legal interest does not dissolve their separate lives or claims into singular-we by default.

## Personal memory and embedded ownership

Caleb's testimony in chapter 14 keeps individual `i`, personal my-heart and personal strength. Forty years at the earlier mission, forty-five elapsed years and eighty-five now are independently preserved. His brethren's effect on the people's heart is contrasted with his own following, so replacing I with a generic collective would damage the argument.

The my God within Moses' quoted oath (14:9) belongs to Moses, not Caleb or the deity. Joshua's land-survey commands likewise use personal I, while our-one God (18:6) denotes the shared religious community. Divine speech in 13:6 uses plural-self I for the future expulsion and earlier command. Quotation and speaker identity determine possessors; mere proximity to God does not.

## Inheritance without a territorial share

Lot as an allocated territory is `kjv_lot_portion`; casting or dividing by lots uses `kjv_lot`, the mechanism. The new portion-share sense allows the source to name both lot and portion (17:14) without flattening the two nouns into a duplicate. Levi has no territorial inheritance but is said successively to inherit sacrifices, the LORD himself and priesthood (13:14,33;18:7). These are separate source predicates, not a translator's single harmonized definition.

Cities with surrounding land are explicit exceptions to no territorial share. Suburbs use the existing open city-land/pasture sense, not a modern residential neighborhood. Substance is property/estate, not material essence. Joseph's two tribes, eastern two-and-half and western nine-and-half retain their scopes. Half Gilead is a territorial half, not half a tribal person. The source one half is one unit of half, never one-and-a-half.

## Boundary speech and homonyms

Coast in these descriptions is a boundary and can lie inland. Bay is a new sea-bend sense, separate from the inherited horse colour. Upper and lower are spatial adjectives, separate from the verb lower. Quarter-side marks direction without claiming exactly a fourth of the land area. Right hand is directional, not morally righteous. Fetched a compass is expressed as a boundary turning around, not an imported surveying instrument. Lighted off an ass is dismounting, not illumination.

Repeated go up, pass along, go out and endpoints retain the source's laborious boundary voice. The route can move between present and past as the supplied text does. Mountain of the valley, sea corner and north bay at the south end are not polished into a modern survey description. Named water, hill, city and valley relations remain inspectable in the gloss.

## Name identity and competing inventories

New source-name forms are explicitly authored and keyed separately from common vocabulary. Hezron, Ephron, Timnah, Naamah, Dumah, Cain, Shechem and Rekem receive referent distinctions where earlier personal names would mislead. Boundary Debir east of Jordan, Debir on the Achor route and the prior southern city are not automatically identified. Judah and Benjamin Mizpeh are separate local inventory identities, as is the earlier northern region. Repeated unqualified names remain locally scoped rather than being claims of a single modern site.

Source variations Bethhogla/Bethhoglah, Atarothaddar/Atarothadar, Sibmah/Shibmah, Jahaza/Jahaz, Avim/Avims/Avites and Gibeah/Gibeath remain visible. Source equations such as Jebusi = Jerusalem and Kirjathbaal = Kirjathjearim are actually stated and therefore retained. Kirjathsepher and Kirjathsannah are both associated with Debir where the source says so; one is not substituted for the other.

## Claims, remainder and coerced residence

Very much land remains in 13:1 after the previous whole-land/rest statements. Geshurites and Maachathites are explicitly not expelled after a report of expulsion. Jerusalem Jebusites and Gezer Canaanites continue to dwell among the recipients; tribute and inability to expel are not softened into voluntary integration. The Manasseh enclave cities and Tappuah's land-versus-city distinction prevent the geography from being simplified into perfectly disjoint tribal blocks.

Caleb's conditional if the LORD be with me remains conditional. Achsah is promised as a wife by her father; the text adds no unstated consent or romance. Her own subsequent initiative and petition for water remain personal and active. The source apposition brother of Caleb in Othniel's genealogy is retained without inventing a more precise attachment than the supplied wording.

## Exact lists without invented arithmetic repair

Each name in Judah's lists is retained, including their repetitions and equations. The published twenty-nine and fourteen totals in 15:32,36 do not transparently equal a simple count of all separated name expressions. The target keeps both list and total; it does not delete names, invent compound-name punctuation or correct the source number. The sixteen, nine, eleven, ten, six, two and subsequent Benjamin totals are independently checked. The source three countries in 17:11 remains alongside six named centers.

Three men for each tribe is a rate, not an explicit twenty-one in the source. Seven described parts and the seven waiting tribes have related but distinct roles. Threescore becomes the established sixty quantity. Firstborn remains a human relation, not a literal one-count or animal firstling. Numeric traces are lexical checks; their recorded one and half at 13:31 are explicitly one half, not an arithmetic addition.
