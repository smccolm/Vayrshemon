# Historical KJV 0.10-kjv.35 · One pledge, many witnesses

The final 188 Joshua verses complete that section's 658 verses. The entire Bible remains incomplete at 6558/31102. This checkpoint adds 121 lexical entries: 114 source names or referent distinctions and seven common senses. Earlier 470 Joshua targets and all previously pinned sections remain unchanged. The historical register stays permanently excluded from modernization.

## Household I, covenant we, witnessing we

Joshua 24:15 distinguishes personal I and my house from their joint future commitment. Its target says individual I plus my household, then singular-we will serve. The household is a shared undertaking, not an automatic plural-I merely because its head speaks. This differs deliberately from the source I am a great people in 17:14, where the collective explicitly speaks as a self containing many.

The people answer with singular-we for their corporate pledge (24:16–18,21,24), but plural-we for we are witnesses (24:22). Testimony attaches to the people against themselves: each can bear witness, while the covenant claims one shared will. This number shift keeps both personal accountability and collective identity visible. It is grammatical and philosophical, with no clinical claim and no inserted number of divine persons.

## Rival communities and nested future voices

The western congregation's us in 22:17,19 is a singular community remembering its guilt and offering eastern tribes a place within it. The eastern reply uses its own singular-we. The quoted future western children in 22:25 temporarily take that same grammatical form for the excluding community, before our children returns to the eastern speakers. This is a change of referent across quotation, not a change in the pronoun's meaning.

Phinehas in 22:31 says plural-we perceive: the envoys are several perceivers. The LORD among us is singular-we, the community their reconciliation now permits them to claim. Delivered Israel out of the LORD's hand remains out of, not the more reassuring by. Ed's final witness-between-us formula reunites the opposed sides in a singular-we relationship without erasing their separate names or prior conflict.

## The many-self remembers and speaks of he

In Joshua's quotation of divine speech (24:3–13), the deity uses plural-I for taking, leading, giving, plaguing and expelling. The quotation itself changes from he put darkness and covered the pursuers to what I did (24:7). Both perspectives remain. An editor must not normalize all third-person references into plural-I just because the surrounding speech belongs to the deity.

Would not listen in 24:10 is volitional refusal, represented by the volition verb, not a future-tense marker. Balaam's he remains separate from divine I. The hornet expels two kings in 24:12, while thy sword and thy bow retain the source singular address amid plural you. This is source variation, not permission to invent a third king or substitute a military explanation for the hornet.

## Refuge is procedure, not automatic acquittal

Joshua 20 retains both unawareness and lack of intent, the absence of prior hatred, the gate declaration, the elders' admission, appearance before the congregation and the high priest's death. Two until clauses remain two clauses; they are not replaced by whichever happens first or by an invented fixed term. The protected person is not simply pronounced innocent on entry.

The sojourning stranger is explicitly included. The blood avenger remains an agent distinct from the court and the city's elders. Refuge reuses the legal refuge vocabulary already developed in Numbers and Deuteronomy, rather than introducing a new root for every retelling. Awkward chains of genitives expose which gate, elders, city and avenger are meant.

## An altar that is not for sacrifice

Chapter 22 preserves the accusation as accusation and the eastern explanation as explanation. The altar is great to see; no dimensions or completed sacrificial act are invented. Its pattern/model relation to the authorized altar is expressed separately from its witness function. Besides means an additional rival altar, not merely a location next to another one.

The conditional self-imprecation save us not remains a negative imperative with the exclamatory ending on the verb. Let the LORD require it and God forbid use optative exclamation. No unsupported exclamation glyph is added. Meal offering denotes cereal meal, not newly introduced animal flesh. Burnt, peace and other sacrifices remain separately named; their possessors attach to the offering heads, not to burn or peace. Western war preparation ends in abandoned intent, not an invented battle.

## Fidelity that cannot be easy

Joshua 24:19 says the people cannot serve the holy, jealous deity and that he will not forgive their transgressions or sins. The target keeps those difficult negatives even after the invitation to choose and before renewed vows. It does not silently change cannot into might find it difficult. Harm and consumption after good (24:20) retain the deity's explicit agency.

The stone heard all the words (24:27). This remains a literal predicate in the narrative, without an explanatory merely symbolic qualification. Chapter 23's all good promises fulfilled and all evil to follow remain parallel claims. Earlier remaining peoples, future expulsion and absolute victory claims coexist rather than being harmonized into one tidy history. The resulting philosophical mismatch is part of this frozen archaic edition.

## Narrow new senses and inherited words

Flood-river distinguishes beyond the flood in 24:2–15 from the destructive deluge. Cleave-adhere distinguishes loyalty from splitting. Mention is voice plus name; courageous uses the established courage form adjectivally; scourge-lash is a whip thing; oliveyard is an olive field. Plague-afflict gives the noun a verbal use where the source explicitly makes deity the agent. These seven additions fill actual sense gaps.

Refuge, priesthood, open city-land, allotted portion, the casting of lots, metal brass, hornet, vineyard and ritual uncleanness reuse earlier vocabulary. Please in 22:30,33 is satisfaction, not the courtesy particle. Seed in 24:3 remains progeny in context, and multiply takes the causative because the subject causes the seed to become many. Worship and source legal claims are translated as claims, without new endorsement from the narrator.

## Named places, counts and property scopes

All supplied boundary names and city lists are retained. Sheba remains beside Beersheba, Iron is a city distinct from the metal, Japhia city differs from the earlier king, and Shechem son of Hamor differs from the town and Manasseh ancestor. Supplied variants such as Ajalon/Aijalon, Kishon/Kishion and Dabareh/Daberath remain visible. Hebron in Asher is not silently emended to a more expected name; Judah upon Jordan in 19:34 likewise remains.

The source thirteen and nineteen totals remain alongside the complete supplied lists. Levite allocations of thirteen, ten, thirteen and twelve total forty-eight; district subtotals retain their tribal scopes. Hebron city and surrounding open land are granted to priests while its fields and villages remain Caleb's property: different property scopes, not an omitted grant. One half is one unit of half, never one-and-a-half. The thousands of Israel in chapter 22 are plural social units, not a supplied exact population census. Joshua's age is 110; Joseph's burial parcel cost one hundred silver pieces, not invented modern coins.
