# KJV historical continuation77 — households, descendants and named groups

## Collective ontology without invented first-person speech
The fifty-five verses of1Chronicles2 contain no source first-person I or we. House, family, people and named groups remain nominal constructions. Their collective reference does not authorize inserting plural-I or singular-we. The historical language retains those perspectives for actual speakers. Half of a named group is a group fraction, not a half-person. Father of a place-associated name can remain genealogical, eponymic or group-relational without imposing a modern distinction the source does not state.

## Namesake identity and inherited forms
Reuse the existing Jether-as-Amasas-father entry: it was already distinguished in2Samuel20. Keep Jether the Ishmeelite as printed here, without normalizing another passage's Ithra/Israelite wording. Ram in the royal lineage is the inherited personal Ram, not the male-sheep entry; Ram son of Jerahmeel is separately identified. Obed in Ephlals line differs from Ruths Obed. Nathan, Jehu, Azariah, Shallum and Elishama each retain the local parentage rather than inheriting a prophet/king/official identity from a familiar name. Jesse's sons are distinguished from earlier census leaders, ark-householders and Saul's family. New semantic entries can reuse forms; vocabulary-entry growth is not the same as creating new phonological roots.

## Kinship rank and generation scope
Firstborn belongs to its stated possessor: Jerahmeel is Hezrons firstborn, while Ram is Jerahmeels. Jesse's second through seventh sons retain their printed ranks without importing a different sibling count. Broad closing sons-of-Jerahmeel language follows multiple generations; retain it instead of correcting every sons phrase to a modern generation-specific label. In2:18the abrupt her follows Azubah and Jerioth; the translation preserves that compression and does not invent a fully resolved marriage history.

## Women, unnamed daughters and household dependency
Shua's daughter remains unnamed; Shua is not automatically the womans own name. Tamar is the wife of Judahs son in the kinship expression. Ephrath and Ephratah in birth/firstborn relations receive personal rather than merely geographical senses. Abiah is Hezrons wife, not Samuels son. Ahlai is under children in2:31; no male gender is imposed before2:34states Sheshan has no sons but daughters. Jarha remains an Egyptian servant to whom Sheshan gives his daughter as wife. The household relation is not modernized into an invented egalitarian contract. Concubine remains the inherited historical status. Childless means without children, not specifically no sons or an invented medical diagnosis.

## Source direction, chronology and quantity
Hezron is sixty when he marries Machirs daughter; the sixty does not become her age. Jair has twenty-three cities, while the later taking statement names sixty. The supplied English says he took Geshur and Aram from them; retain this direction rather than silently correcting it from another edition. The death of Hezron precedes Abiahs birth statement as printed. Three sons of Judah from Shua's daughter, five sons of Judah overall, five sons of Zerah, three sons of Zeruiah and the two half-Manahethite group references all remain separately stated quantities.

## Father relations and city-associated names
Mesha, Mareshah, Hebron, Ziph, Tekoa, Jorkoam, Maon, Bethzur, Madmannah, Machbenah, Gibea, Kirjathjearim, Bethlehem and Bethgader enter parentage or father-of constructions. Translate the relationships without asserting that masonry literally reproduces, but also without inventing a precise founder or settlement date. Distinguish local lineage senses from earlier unrelated kings, people and places. The awkward coordination in2:42does not acquire an unprinted Mesha-to-Mareshah edge. Caleb son of Hezron and Caleb expressly called son of Hur in2:50remain source-designated references; the firstborn-of-Ephratah phrase is not reattached to force a unified chart.

## Families, occupations and frozen historical scope
Scribal families at Jabez preserve occupation, dwelling-place and ancestry as distinct relations. Jabez is locally the dwelling-place, not automatically a later named person. Source group names are not modern ethnic assertions. Keep the supplied Ataroth, house-of-Joab wording without silently replacing it with a different compound name. Gazez appears in two adjacent parentage statements; retain both without forcing identity or manufacturing a correction. The preceding54verses, all sealed historical editions and frozen Jonah stay unchanged. This is a continuation of the awkward historical register, not modernization.
