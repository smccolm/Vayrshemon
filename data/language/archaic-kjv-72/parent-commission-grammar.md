# KJV historical register 0.10-kjv.10 — Exodus commission and coerced collective

This historical continuation inherits the complete Genesis register 0.10-kjv.9. It adds Exodus 1–6 while the entire 31,102-verse Bible remains incomplete. The final deliverable remains one .vay. The sealed v1 Jonah is unchanged. All choices below are authored interpretations, not claims of independent ancient attestation or a clinical account of identity.

## Divine plural-I and the cited self-name

The deity's direct first-person self in Exodus uses `ipl` (rôun) and `mypl` possession. Moses' curiosity, fear, protest and commission use ordinary personal `i` (rô). Exodus 3:14 has `ipl be:pr that ipl be:pr`; the following reported message cites a whole name `kjv_i_am`, whose internal form is plural-I plus present be. In `kjv_i_am:n i:a ... send:perf`, the name is the sender and Moses is singular me. This quoted phrase is not permission to change all first persons in a passage to plural-I. There is no imposed number of divine persons; the later Trinity passages remain untranslated at this checkpoint.

## One people, one son, internally plural possessor

Divine `mypl/people` and `mypl/son` keep the possessor's internal plurality distinct from the possessed entity's grammatical number. Israel as one firstborn son in 4:22 is corporate descent imagery, not one physical body. The two messengers' reported us in 3:18 and 5:3 can be `wesg`, speaking for the worshipping whole; their actual two mouths and the instructions addressed to both are explicitly plural. Narration does not turn every crowd into singular-we.

## Competing office-self and the injured whole

Pharaoh's sovereign refusal in 5:2 and decree quoted in 5:10 use `ipl` for claimed office-self. This does not equate his nature with the deity or overwrite earlier personal dream reports. The workers' complaint uses `wesg` and `ourone` for shared plight; deferential third-person thy servants remains when supplied. Pharaoh's quotation of their petition retains the quoted collective self, while his charge of idleness is attributed to him. Repeated idle is intentional rhetoric. The English let switches sense: release/permit in the petition, hinder in 5:4.

## Mouths, delegated speech and bodily awkwardness

In 4:15 the deity accompanies thy mouth and his mouth and teaches you-plural. Aaron serves as Moses' mouth; Moses is in place of God to Aaron in 4:16. Role comparison does not grant Moses a divine first-person identity. Uncircumcised lips in 6:12 and 6:30 stays a bodily covenant metaphor rather than becoming a smooth word for ineloquence. Leprous retains the historical skin-description without retrospective modern diagnosis; dumb means unable to speak, not unintelligent. The inn scene's he/him/his feet in 4:24–26 stays unresolved instead of choosing an unstated target of the threatened killing.

## Agency, suffering and unharmonized divine claims

The deity hears, remembers, looks and attends in 2:24–25; the sequence is not modernized into timeless knowledge. The active hardening in 4:21 remains beside the demand for release. The claim of not being known by Jehovah in 6:3 remains beside prior Genesis naming rather than being rewritten as incomplete understanding. Moses' complaints in 5:22–23 retain their charge of harm and non-deliverance. Israel's failure to listen in 6:9 is attributed to anguish and bondage, not automatically moral stubbornness.

## Material senses and historical worship

The small rush-ark, sealing slime, pitch, infant-nursing, brick block of unspecified firing, stubble and imposed quota each have declared senses. They are not selected through superficial English spellings. `kjv_worship` is a religious homage verb distinct from the inherited modern social title. Four working Genesis verses (22:5; 24:26,48,52) are corrected to that sense in this v10 copy; earlier edition files and frozen Jonah are preserved. Those four target changes are listed explicitly in the revision ledger.

## Names, genealogies and exact quantities

Names preserve local source spelling and relationships: Pallu differs from earlier Phallu; Gershon differs from Gershom; Zichri differs from Zithri. Hebron the person and Korah son of Izhar have separate referent entries from the earlier locality and Edomite. Jochebed remains Amram's father's sister; no later prohibition is used to repair the source. The named order Aaron/Moses and Moses/Aaron is retained. Source ages 137, 133 and 137 remain exact values represented through the established counting system. No new numeral base, suffix paradigm or exclamation glyph is introduced.

## Reader-facing gloss and preservation boundary

The compiler now displays `mypl` as `my(pl.self.)` and `ourone` as `our(sg.whole.)` in construction glosses. This changes the explanatory labels, not their target spellings, Maxima or grammar. The working Genesis copy is recompiled and remeasured because even explanatory text can change page wrapping; old saved versions remain intact. Other inherited morphology, fonts, sizes, exact leading, paint and template geometry remain unchanged. Every new Exodus verse is an executable compiler case with a literal back-reading and translator note. Compilation, quantity checks and layout verification do not constitute independent literary certification.
