# Historical KJV 0.10-kjv.65 — private loss, foreign healing and counterfeit speech

This continuation adds all71 verses of2Kings4–5, retaining the preceding70verses and every earlier corrected target. The whole-Bible authored coverage becomes9,723 of31,102;21,379 remain. This historical branch is permanently excluded from modernization. Frozen Jonah is unchanged.

## Personal I and the royal letter

The widow, Shunammite mother, prophet, Naaman and Gehazi speak personal I in practical requests, distress, expectation, testimony or self-interest. Elisha's question about helping the widow(4:2) and his admission that the LORD concealed her distress(4:27) do not become divine plural-I. His accompanying-heart claim in5:26 is retained as speech in that scene, not expanded into universal omniscience. The Syrian king's dispatch and written message(5:5–6) use royal plural-I and its matching possession. The receiving king's am-I-God question(5:7) uses personal incapacity and alarm. This is a deliberate contrast between an institutional sender and a vulnerable recipient, rather than a uniform rule for every royal utterance.

## Contextual duals and reference resets

The Shunammite speaks to her husband about us-two and their chamber proposal(4:9–10). Elisha's us-two in4:13 covers himself and the attendant. The journey pair in4:31 is prophet and mother; the pair behind the shut door in4:33 is prophet and child. The same dual form changes referents with the scene. Two actual carriers take Naaman's gift ahead of Gehazi and are dismissed(5:23–24); plural goods are distinct from the two human carriers. No singular-we is artificially introduced where the source presents actual pairs or individual voices. Human one remains suitable for the unnamed gatherer and intermediary(4:39;5:4); the donkey in4:22 takes cardinal one_num.

## Speech within speech and counterfeit authority

Gehazi's my-master-sent-me report in5:22 is a lie he tells Naaman. The inner to-me of the fabricated message is the purported master's personal I, distinct from Gehazi's outer me. The target preserves the quotation boundary and the editorial note labels the fabrication; it does not change Elisha's actual refusal into a request. The repeated as-the-LORD-lives formula belongs both to Elisha's refusal and Gehazi's private pursuit. Identical oath language does not establish identical sincerity. Naaman's imagined hand-moving cure in5:11 remains an expectation, distinct from the messenger's actual washing instruction and the later narrated immersion.

## Grief, repetition and grammatical exclamation

My-head twice(4:19) and the alarmed man-of-God address(4:40) take the inherited exclamatory form without an unsupported exclamation glyph. The captive girl's wish in5:3 combines optative and exclamatory marking. Repeated summonses, questions, formulaic life-time language, oaths and reassurance remain repeated where the source deliberately repeats them. Natural variation is not a license to erase these motifs. It-is-well in4:23,26 is the mother's response, not a narrator reversal of the child's death. The failed staff procedure, no voice or hearing, repeated dead-state, warming flesh and seven sneezes all remain in sequence. No diagnosis, modern resuscitation theory or hidden successful intermediate event is supplied.

## Material categories and unassimilated worship

Leprosy remains the inherited historical category rather than a single modern disease. Clean after washing is bodily/ritual condition, not moral goodness. The captive little maid remains captive and little; the foreign commander's deliverance is credited to the LORD by the narrator without changing Syria into Israel. Naaman's earth-load request, vow, and request for pardon when supporting his master in Rimmon's worship-house retain their material and political awkwardness. Physical leaning is not trust. Elisha's go-in-peace stays brief; no unprinted doctrinal authorization or condemnation is added. The hereditary consequence against Gehazi and his descendants remains unsmoothed.

## Sense discipline and eight lexical additions

Six proper entries identify Gehazi, Baalshalisha, Naaman the Syrian captain, Abana, Pharpar, and Rimmon the deity. Namesakes reuse inherited forms with explicit distinct local identities. Wild-vine is climbing-plant, and wild-gourd is wild-fruit; these are source-linked functional terms, not botanical identifications. They do not overwrite frozen Jonah's shade-plant. The food addition in4:41 is flour, not a whole meal. Grain ears are not organs or imported maize. Rest is remainder, lighted-down is descent, and some-thing is an object rather than the degree adverb somewhat. Handmaid can be deferential self-designation without an invented slavery status; the creditor's intended bondmen in4:1 explicitly are unfree servants. No new pronoun or inflectional paradigm is added.

## Counts and objects

The widow has two sons. The Shunammite requests one human attendant and one donkey, with the generic-human versus cardinal distinction retained. Seven sneezes are separate from seven commanded and seven performed washings. Twenty barley loaves serve a hundred men. Naaman brings ten silver talents, six thousand gold pieces and ten clothing changes. Gehazi falsely requests one silver talent and two garment changes for two supposed arrivals; Naaman gives two talents in two bags with two changes carried by two servants. Repeated counts refer to the same objects and are not added into new totals. The later list of fields, vineyards, livestock and servants is a rhetorical acquisition list, not an assertion that all those goods were physically carried home.
