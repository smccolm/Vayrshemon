## Ordinary human dual versus civic singular-we

In14:1 Barnabas and Paul travel together as a physical dual. In14:15 they explicitly answer the deifying crowd as we-two,men sharing human passions. No divine plural-I is imposed on their ordinary human claim. The crowd's us in14:11 is its own receiving singular-we.14:17 broadens us-rain/our-hearts to the nourished human community;14:22 gathers the disciple community into the singular-we that must enter through tribulation. The same grammatical form can have different membership at a new discourse boundary.

Jupiter and Mercurius remain the crowd's supplied identifications of Barnabas and Paul. These are not narrator judgments,and the Greek pantheon names are not substituted for the supplied forms. The apostles' rending clothes and barely restraining sacrifice preserve misunderstanding rather than turning the episode into a smooth conversion story. Paul is supposed dead in14:19;the translation does not certify a narrated death and resurrection absent from that wording.

## Council judgment and the Spirit alongside us

Peter's among-us and grace-saved-we in15:7–11 use the communal singular-we,while my-mouth remains personal possession. Both halves of neither our-fathers nor we were able-to-bear have negative scope. The conclusion keeps we-saved-even-as-they,not a reversed comparison. The Pharisees raising the objection in15:5 are explicitly believing.

James's listen-to-me and my-judgment are personal. His proposal and the council letter use singular-we as a deliberating body.15:28 names the Holy Spirit and us alongside one another in the good-seemed judgment. It is not rewritten into a direct Spirit I-statement,an arithmetic count of persons,or an identity equation. The letter explicitly disavows the earlier circumcision-and-law command;its four restraints remain distinct. Strangled food and blood are not collapsed.15:34 remains present and retains Silas's staying beside the preceding plural release.

## A real split and a new travelling voice

Paul's let-us-go proposal in15:36 is an actual dual addressed to Barnabas. Our-brothers is the wider community's singular possession. Bringing John Mark with those two would add a third;it does not make the group of three a dual. The sharp dispute and separation remain. Barnabas/Mark and Paul/Silas become different pairs. Unity morphology does not remove narrated disagreement.

The narrator is left unnamed;no Luke identification is inserted into the target. Lydia's personal me/my-house contrasts with this travel group. In16:17 the narrated us and the girl's spoken us occupy separate discourse contexts. ## Prison, household and competing civic claims

The arrested Paul/Silas pair uses physical dual object marking through the imprisonment.16:28's we-all-here broadens to the present prisoners and supports Paul's intervention against the keeper's intended self-killing.16:30's what-must-I-do is the keeper's personal-I.16:37's we-Romans is the arrested pair's dual claim,contrasting with the accusers' civic singular-we/our-city in16:20–21. Both receiving and observing unlawful customs are negated. Public beating,uncondemned status and secret proposed release all remain.

The keeper's belief,household hearing,washing stripes,baptism and food are preserved in order. No infant ages or household membership list is invented. The source's spirit-possession category and attempted self-killing are narrative content,not diagnoses or instructions. Foot-stocks are restraints;fast means securely,and stripes are blow injuries rather than colour marks. The city officials' fear is not expanded into an unstated legal penalty.
