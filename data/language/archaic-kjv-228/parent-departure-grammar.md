## The Spirit's plural-self and the travelling dual

In13:2 the Holy Spirit speaks `ipl` both in separate-for-me and I-have-called. This is the historical plural-I of the speaking self,not a claim that the commissioning group or the Spirit consists of a numerically counted three. Barnabas and Saul are the explicitly named two recipients and receive physical dual object marking. The wider group lays hands on and sends those two;it is not itself turned into a dual.13:4 continues that pair until13:5 mentions John as assistant,after which bare travellers and company are not mechanically forced into two-person morphology.

The named missionary pair speaks as `we:du` in13:32 and13:46 and receives the command as us-two in13:47. This is an editorial discourse decision grounded in the named Paul/Barnabas pairing,not a universal rule that all apostolic we is dual. In13:33 the us who are the fathers' children is the wider ancestral community and therefore `wesg`. The reader can hear one mission's two mouths broaden into one inherited people.

## Peter's I and the assembled we

Peter's narrated prayer,vision,hearing,refusal,recollection and inability to withstand God remain personal-I in11:5–17. The six accompanying brothers in11:12 join the delegation and Peter in an assembled singular-we entering the house. This is not a two-person group. The earlier source's unspecified companions are not rewritten to carry the later explicit six.

In12:11 Peter returns to himself and knows his rescue personally. The angel's follow-me in12:8 is also personal-I: an angelic role is not enough to impose the divine plural-self. The gathering's accusation that Rhoda is mad and its claim that Peter's angel is at the gate remain attributed claims;neither is supplied as diagnosis or narrator explanation.
