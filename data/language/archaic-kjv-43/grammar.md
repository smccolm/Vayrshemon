## A political singular-we can speak from both sides of an argument

Philistine communities ask what to do with the ark and what offering to return. Bethshemesh asks where the dangerous holy presence can go from us. Israel confesses, asks for rescue, demands a king and voices the king's expected duties. Each uses singular-we for a public body speaking as a whole, with ourone where collective possession is expressed. This is an authored Vayrshemon discourse distinction, not a claim that the English or Hebrew pronoun has singular morphology.

The dissenters in10:27 also ask how Saul will save us. Their singular-we appeals to the same polity while contesting its ruler. It does not mean the whole polity agrees with them. Collective reference and unanimous consent are different matters. Nor does singular-we force every participant into a single biological organism.
