# Possession, memory and competing I-voices · 0.10-kjv.27

Working historical continuation, Deuteronomy 7–12. No new inflectional endings, no modernization, no change to the earlier corrected targets or frozen Jonah. The whole Bible remains incomplete. These are authored constructions and translator self-review, not independent literary certification.

## The people's inward I

In 7:17 the community says inwardly that the nations are greater than “I”; in 8:17 it credits “my power” with wealth; in 9:4 it imagines “my righteousness” earned possession. These use `ipl` and `mypl`, because Israel is speaking its constituent plurality as one self. This can be fearful, boastful or self-justifying: plural-I does not mark correctness, divinity, or privilege. The claim of merit is explicitly rejected. Individual Moses' prayer and eyewitness narration keep `i`/`my`.

## Ordinary I and distributive we

The generic eater's “I will eat flesh” in 12:20 and the imitative worshipper's “I will do likewise” in 12:30 remain individual-I. In 12:8, “what we do here, each according to his own eyes” uses plural-we to foreground differing individual practices. It does not turn that set into a corporate singular-we promise. Body terms and possessives may stay singular within an addressed whole when the source addresses it as one person.

## Divine voice inside Moses' address

7:4's sudden “following me” is kept as a divine first-person intrusion, not changed to “him.” The rain/grass promises in 11:14–15 use divine plural-I inside an otherwise Mosaic address. The instructional “I command/set before you” belongs to Moses. These unmarked changes of quotation level are deliberately uncomfortable; the wiki explains them, but the reading text does not insert invented speaker labels. Divine I remains numerically uncounted.

## Whose people, whose inheritance

In 9:12 the deity tells Moses “thy people, whom thou broughtest out.” Moses' intercession answers “thy people, thine inheritance,” returning ownership to the deity. The threatened replacement nation from Moses is retained but not treated as accepted. A quoted foreign accusation of inability and hatred in 9:28 remains a feared report within Moses' prayer, not the narrator's assertion. `wesg` in that prayer is the people brought out as one whole.

## Two tablets and incompatible memories

Keep paired forty-day/forty-night statements, two tablets and two hands. Do not add the repeated recollections into a new fasting total. Aaron's death and burial at Mosera in 10:6 stays beside the earlier Numbers account; Beeroth/Jaakan, Gudgodah and Jotbath keep their supplied names. No harmonizing itinerary or replacement location is inserted. Moses making the chest remains first person in this account. The heart-foreskin and stiff-neck metaphors remain bodily and alien rather than becoming generic good behaviour.

## Ordinary meat and restricted offerings

Deuteronomy 12 permits ordinary slaughter and meat in local gates, including for ritually clean and unclean people, compared to roebuck/hart. Sacred tithes, firstlings, vowed gifts and offerings remain restricted to the chosen place. Distance permits local ordinary slaughter; it does not erase the sacred restriction. Ordinary blood goes onto the earth, sacrificial blood onto the altar. The declaration “blood is life” remains explicit. The city is not named where the source does not name it.

## Keeping different kinds of rightness apart

Righteousness, uprightness of heart, moral right, and a right-hand direction have separate lexical senses. This continuation adds the straight-heart calque `kjv_uprightness`. The claim that land comes through one's righteousness is denied even though obedience and righteousness have appeared together earlier. Judicial “reward” in 10:17 is the new `kjv_bribe`, not a prohibition on all recompense. Fatherless and widow receive judgment; the stranger receives food/clothing and an explicit love command. These stay beside exclusionary conquest rhetoric without being reduced to one simplified ethic.

## Threat, restraint and material abundance

Source destruction commands, no-pity clauses, name erasure and the child-burning accusation remain attributed and explicit. Gradual expulsion to prevent wild-animal increase stays beside commands for rapid destruction. Egyptian diseases are threatened against haters, not translated into universal healing. Rain-dependence is contrasted with Egyptian foot-watering without inventing irrigation machinery. Manna, clothing, swelling feet, scorpions, flint, crops, metal-bearing hills and house contents all retain their material specificity. These are source claims, not explanatory endorsements.

## Historical lexical additions and source variants

Scorpion is claw-sting-beast; chasten is teach-punish; hart is male-deer. Roebuck remains an explicit source-label borrowing without forced modern species identification. Ebal mountain is a separate referent from Ebal person with the same form. Girgashites keeps this source form beside earlier Girgasite. Gerizim and Gilgal preserve local relations. No new modern geography, ethnic identity, exact insect species, or ritual dimensions are silently supplied.
