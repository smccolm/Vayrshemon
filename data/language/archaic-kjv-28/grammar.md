## Collective I, corporate we and the intimate dual

The people imagining a king over itself in 17:14 uses `ipl`: a many-person self speaks an English singular I. The remembered request not to hear the terrifying voice in 18:16 likewise uses `ipl` and `mypl`. In 18:21 the community seeking a common test says `wesg`: we as one covenant whole. Neither is a free substitute for the other. Moses' personal like-me in 18:15 and God-addressed-to-me in 18:17 keep `i`. The servant's personal refusal to leave in 15:16 also keeps `i`. The close relative/friend's private invitation in 13:6 uses actual pair-we `we:du`, while public invitations to a group in 13:2,13 use `wepl`.

## Speech authority and embedded possessors

In 18:18–20 divine `ipl` raises a distinct human prophet and puts `mypl/word:pl` in that person's mouth. The words and name remain the divine speaker's possessions; the prophet does not become the divine self. This is a discourse choice, not a declaration that the text names three persons. The unmarked I-not-commanded in Moses' legal address at 17:3 remains instructional singular-I; its lack of explicit quotation boundary is documented rather than repaired with an invented speaker label. The register leaves awkward transitions visible.

## Two different tests of prophetic speech

Chapter 13 explicitly allows a sign to happen and still forbids following the proposed other gods. Chapter 18 identifies an unfulfilled prediction as speech not given by LORD. These are not converted into the false rule that every fulfilled sign proves authority. Investigation, truth, certainty, speaker, content and outcome remain separately expressed. No modern diagnosis is inserted for spirit-consulting or prophecy. The source categories describe its own prohibited practices without certifying their efficacy.

## Witnesses, courts and the king below the law

17:6 keeps two or three witnesses and explicitly excludes execution on one witness alone. Witnesses act first in the prescribed punishment. Blood/blood, plea/plea and stroke/stroke remain three awkward paired judicial categories; `kjv_plea` names an adjudicated claim and `strike:ev` the nominal act of striking. The person judging is `judge:pr person`, not the abstract judgment. Directions use `kjv_right_side` and `left`, distinct from moral righteousness and a right of entitlement. The king writes and reads a law-copy, must not lift his heart above his brothers, and is restricted in horses, wives, silver and gold. His corporate people's desire is not his personal I.

## Release with uncomfortable exclusions

`kjv_creditor` is lend-person; `kjv_exact_debt` is debt-demand, not precise exactness. Neighbour/brother debts are released while exaction from a foreigner remains allowed in 15:3. The no-poor conditional in 15:4 stands beside poor-never-ceases in 15:11. Anticipating the release year does not excuse refusal to lend. Six years of service, seventh-year freedom, sufficient departure provisions and permanent marked service by the stated choice remain together. Male and female cases are retained. No modern free-contract assumptions are added. Double worth in 15:18 remains a comparison, without an invented wage formula.
