# Titus: households, communal memory and firm admonition

Historical checkpoint 0.10-kjv.257 adds the 46 verses of Titus to the ordered 0.10-kjv.256 parent. This archaic branch stays permanently outside modernization. It adds five contextual common entries and three proper entries; earlier forms, paradigms, targets and seals remain unchanged. Draft authorship is not native verification or independent literary certification.

## Singular-we and separate agents

Communal `wesg` carries instruction and redemption in 2:12/14, former shared wrongdoing in 3:3, mercy/washing/renewal and inheritance in 3:5–7, belonging in 3:14 and loved community in 3:15. `ourone` accompanies the shared Saviour relation. The collective pronoun can have distributive plural heirs; this does not invent one legal inheritor. Personal `i` is the entrusted writer, the one who left Titus, the one making a request or planning a visit, and the one whose companions greet him. There is no imposed plural-I or divine physical dual in Titus. Third-person God, Christ, himself and Holy Spirit remain distinct as the supplied syntax presents them; coordinated titles in 2:13 are not turned into a numerical solution to the Trinity.

`they:du` in 3:13 refers to the explicitly named Zenas and Apollos. Artemas **or** Tychicus in 3:12 is an alternative and does not license dual. The final `you all` is plural while the immediate greeting addresses singular thou.

## Age, office and the historical household

The elders appointed in every city are the established formal-role `kjv_elder`; aged men and women in chapter 2 use age plus person. Overseer, steward and household retain distinct senses. One-wife husband remains literal in 1:6 without an added rule about widowhood or office eligibility. Faithful children is not silently settled as either loyal or already converted. Riot uses the dissipation/revelry sense, not automatically a political disturbance.

The women's home-keeping and obedience restrictions, the servant/master relation and the source's negative ethnic quotation are preserved as historically attributed claims. They are neither euphemized into modern employment nor presented as the translator's own advice. The quoted **idle bellies** keeps slow bellies' lazy-glutton image; 1:13's endorsement remains the letter's claim, separate from this translation commentary.

## Contextual lexical extensions

`kjv_sound_integrity_titus` shares whole for integrity of faith/love/patience, distinct from audible sound and the narrower sound-teaching entry. `kjv_purloin_titus` is entrust-steal, taking from entrusted goods. `kjv_regeneration_titus` preserves again-birth in the washing context while leaving Matthew 19:28's renewal setting untouched. `kjv_heretick_titus` is divide-person in the first/second admonition sequence, not a later denominational or diagnostic label. `kjv_subvert_titus` extends cast-down to the household/person corrupted by the stated teaching or conduct without requiring physical building demolition. Names Artemas, Nicopolis and Zenas record only their local roles; no modern location or name etymology is asserted.

Peculiar retains special possession, not eccentricity. Kindness is benevolence rather than species-kind. Grave/gravity uses dignity, not tomb or weight. Pattern in 2:7 is an example to follow, explicitly licensed here. Temperance and soberness concern self-command, not only alcohol and not clinical mental health. Affirm constantly in 3:8 is confident assertion; careful is heedful, profitable beneficial, and sometimes in 3:3 means formerly. A source count of first and second admonitions remains [1,2]; `one of themselves` in 1:12 is indefinite-person selection, not a further doctrinal numeral.

## KJV wording and unresolved claims

Preserve grace **mercy** and peace in 1:4, good **men** in 1:8, **sincerity** in 2:7, and the plural **you** in 2:8. Grace appears to **all people** in 2:11. The negation in 3:5 rejects salvation by performed righteous works; it does not claim no works were done. Washing/regeneration and Holy Spirit renewing are retained without an invented sacramental technique. God pours through Christ in 3:6; no added first-person divine declaration resolves that relation. The named messengers, intended winter stay and law-skilled Zenas remain travel details rather than completed events.
