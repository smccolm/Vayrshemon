# Historical KJV 0.10-kjv.33 · Trespass, treaty and conquest

Joshua 7–12 adds 178 individually authored verses, taking Joshua to 303 and the incomplete whole Bible to 6203/31102. There are 77 lexical additions (65 proper-name identities and 12 common senses); no changed affix, pronoun paradigm, earlier target or frozen Jonah. This remains deliberately archaic and permanently outside modernization. Source English is exact; notes are translator self-review, not independent certification.

## Divine plural-self and individual confession

The LORD's first-person commands and promises use `ipl` and `mypl` (7:11–12,8:1,18,10:8,11:6). The deity is internally plural in the authored language while a bodily hand, a covenant or an addressed Joshua may remain singular. This is the user's conceptual extension, not a claim that the English explicitly marks a particular internal number. No count of three is inserted.

Joshua's lament and orders keep personal `i`: what can I say, I command, bring to me. Achan's confession likewise remains personal I (7:20–21). Shared consequences do not turn every human offender into plural-I. The collective accusation Israel has sinned and the individual theft remain in tension. The narrowing sequence tribe → family → household → man is retained without inventing an unmentioned selection apparatus.

## Corporate regret and competing singular-we communities

Joshua speaks as singular-we for Israel's threatened life and name (7:7,9). His wish that the people had remained across Jordan uses optative exclamation, not a newly asserted divine motive. In 8:5, I and the people with me starts with an individual commander; the shared decoy movement uses singular-we. The following quoted enemy assessment also uses singular-we, for a different community. Quotation frames and named speakers, rather than an ethnic privilege in the pronoun, determine reference.

Exclamation is morphological (`:opt:excl` or a finite exclamatory question); no unsupported Thimfora exclamation glyph is introduced. Person, grammatical number, bodily number and moral responsibility remain separate dimensions.

## Emissaries, oath-takers and treaty-whole

Gibeonite emissaries claim plural-we came from afar (9:6). They represent singular-we when asking for a treaty and describing their servant polity. Their hearing, houses, provisions, clothes and endangered lives are plural-witness possessions (`of wepl ...`). In 9:11, our-one elders send instructions to us-plural emissaries, whose embedded message says we-one are your servants. This nested shift is deliberate and recorded.

Israel's princes likewise use plural-we for having personally sworn the oath and being unable to touch the protected people (9:19–20). The threatened wrath affects singular-we, the community. The allied kings use plural-we as a coalition (10:4), while the Gibeonite request for rescue again speaks for one polity (10:6). Singular-we does not imply friendship, innocence or equal power.

## Fabricated evidence and constrained survival

The narrative marks the emissary performance and its material signs as deception. Their claimed journey, hot bread and newly filled bottles remain within their speech; the narrator's discovery of local residence is not retroactively substituted into it. Patched footwear uses the existing fabric-patch noun analytically. Bottles remain portable liquid containers, without asserting an unprinted material. Mouldy is a new food-rot adjective, not a crop-mildew substitution.

The oath protects life while Joshua imposes lasting bondage and wood/water labor. The text preserves both rescue and coercion. `kjv_league` is an oath-bind treaty, distinct from the inherited league of distance. `kjv_ambassador`, `kjv_provisions` and `kjv_hew` have explicit senses rather than accidental reuse of unrelated English homonyms.

## Objects, names and local identity

The gold wedge is a shape by weight, not an invented currency denomination. Spear is a named pole weapon without a modern technical reconstruction. The neutral dead-body term avoids adding contempt when the source says carcase. Dissemble is conceal-deceive; discomfit is defeat-confuse; slack qualifies effort rather than literal detachment of a hand. Hough is retained as the source disabling injury to horses, without procedural elaboration or an added assertion of killing them.

Cities Libnah, Tirzah, Hepher and Shimron and king Jobab receive separate semantic identities from earlier stations or persons while retaining their name forms. Goshen in this campaign is not forced into the Egyptian locality. Debir the king and Debir the city are distinct keys. Source variants Ai/Hai, Chinneroth/Chinnereth, Bethjeshimoth/Bethjesimoth, Salcah/Salchah and people-name variants remain visible; no modern geographical certainty is claimed. Jasher is a cited title only, not imported apocryphal contents.

## Agency, violence and literal celestial address

The source attributes the discomfiture, hail and heart-hardening to the LORD; those agents are not silently reassigned to humans or impersonal weather. Sun and moon receive literal imperatives, followed by their stated stillness. The target does not substitute an Earth-rotation explanation. Divine hearing of a man's voice remains as stated, even beside divine sovereignty.

Achan's family/property inclusion and the him/them shift in 7:25 are retained. The five kings are slain and then hung (10:26); the order is not changed into hanging as an asserted cause of death. Total destruction, surviving exceptions and coerced service are translated tersely without approval, euphemism or extra gore. The text remains philosophically uncomfortable in the target.

## Unsmoothed chronology and quantifiers

The thirty-thousand force (8:3) and later about-five-thousand force (8:12) both remain. Gilgal returns at 10:15 and 10:43 surround the intervening Makkedah camp statement without editorial reordering. Consumed all and surviving remainder in 10:20, the later Hebron king after an earlier king's death, whole-land/rest claims and future remaining territory are not repaired by unprinted explanations.

The source's twelve thousand men and women followed by all men of Ai stays visibly awkward. Only Hazor is burned among the still-standing cities (11:13), while the Anakim remnant is explicitly located outside the stated cleared territory (11:22). At one time in 10:42 and long war in 11:18 remain different source perspectives.

## Quantities, inventory and repeated form

Alternative recommended forces, approximate casualties, distinct silver/gold weights, halves, ordinals and repeated five-kings counts keep their scopes. Sand-like multitude is a simile, not an estimated census. Chapter 12 preserves each repeated one, every city and its qualifying relation, and the explicit thirty-one total. The thirty earlier one-counts plus Tirzah's one match that total. Half Gilead and half Manasseh are not interchangeable.

Repetition required by the source remains; natural variation does not authorize deleting an inventory or smoothing ritual/political formulae. Novel vocabulary is drawn from established semantic roots or explicitly authored name forms, with each entry's provenance recorded.
