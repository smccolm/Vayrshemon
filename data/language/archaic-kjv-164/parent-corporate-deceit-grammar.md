# KJV 0.10-kjv.7 — endangered selves, corporate deceit and contested descent

This historical continuation inherits the pinned v6 register. Genesis 33–38 adds 189 authored verses. Together Genesis 1–38 and unchanged frozen Jonah account for 1,198 of 31,102 verses; 29,904 remain beyond this checkpoint. The requested single complete Bible is not finished. New entries are authored historical constructions, not claims of archaeological attestation. Modern 0.10 and the sealed Jonah text remain unchanged.

## 1. I being few: the threatened self includes a household

Genesis 34:30 extends the prior two-band construction. Jacob first says his sons have troubled me: ordinary singular `i`, a personal complaint. At I being few in number, the speaker becomes `ipl`, the one Jacob-identity apprehending his vulnerable household as its own plurality. The threatened gathering, killing and destruction then target that plural self. The final I and my house unpacks the reference into ordinary I plus the household. This gives the number switch an explicit source trigger rather than making every emotional speaker internally plural.

This use is human and source-scoped. It is neither a divinity claim nor a clinical description. The user's desired metaphysical discomfort comes from one grammatical self encompassing multiple people, not from attributing a psychiatric condition. The ordinary I in reconciliation, dream reporting, grief and pregnancy remains personal where the text gives no corresponding plural-self perspective.

## 2. Singular-we can promise unity falsely

In Genesis 34 the parties initially remain separate: give daughters to us-several, take ours, live with us-several. The brothers then speak as one kin-house, `wesg`, claiming our-one sister or daughter and a shared rule. Their future one people construction anticipates a merged whole, but the narrator has already called the answer deceitful. Singular-we marks the asserted corporate perspective; it does not validate the promise or imply moral approval.

Hamor's civic sales pitch also uses singular-we: one city considering residence, marriage and acquired property. The source rhetorical question about possessing the newcomers' livestock remains a question inside that pitch, not a completed transfer of ownership. The plural bodily population, every male, remains explicit despite the collective decision voice. Our daughter in the brothers' ultimatum is retained even though the narrator calls Dinah their sister: corporate kin-language can shift the claimed relation without changing her biological parentage.

## 3. One deed, several observers

Genesis 37:7 uses plural-we for the separate sheaf-binders in Joseph's dream. His brothers' threatened us under a ruler is plural-we too, preserving distinguishable subordinates. In 37:20, the proposed killing and agreed false account use singular-we: one conspiracy speaking and acting. Its concluding we shall see uses plural-we for the subsequent individual observers. Reuben's let us not kill addresses the proposed common deed as one action while refusing it; his dissent is not erased by collective grammar.

Judah's proposed sale and the later we have found cover-story again use singular-we. Our-one brother contrasts with thy son in the deceptive presentation to Jacob. In 38:23, lest we be shamed uses plural-we for Judah and Hirah, separately exposed participants; the next clauses assign I sent and thou didst not find to different people. These switches are intentional discourse choices, not alternate spellings selected to reduce repetition randomly.

## 4. Divine speech keeps explicit reference forms

Genesis 35:1 has the deity direct Jacob to make an altar unto God who appeared. It stays a third-person designation in divine speech; explicit source reference is not forcibly rewritten into plural-I. In 35:11–12 the direct I am God Almighty and I gave / will give use `ipl`. The title is singular while the self-reference is internally plural, preserving the deliberately uneasy identity system.

The strange gods surrendered by the household are ordinary plural `kjv_god:pl`, neither divine plural-I nor named Trinitarian persons. Jacob's household let us go uses singular-we, while I will make the altar immediately returns to his personal singular-I. The source's later Trinitarian passages remain future translation work, not coverage claimed by anticipation.

## 5. Genealogy does not require editorial harmonization

The register retains repeated names, title lists and competing affiliations. Genesis 35 repeats Jacob's renaming and then still uses Jacob; Benjamin's birth near Ephrath sits awkwardly beside the blanket born in Padanaram summary. Genesis 36 preserves daughter-of-Zibeon wording for Anah and later male he / father wording; both Korah placements remain. The sons labels can encompass descendants through intermediate parents. Mother's name, household affiliation and physical birth are kept distinguishable.

Proper forms are not unique identity keys. Adah the wife of Esau receives a separate source-scoped entry sharing Adah's earlier form. Bela the king, Jobab the king, Uz of Dishan and Rehoboth by the river likewise avoid equating unrelated bearers. Timna, Timnah and Timnath; Dishon and Dishan; Zepho and Shepho; Zerah and Zarah remain written distinctions. These entries add referent records without pretending each homonymous form is a new surface word. Duke gets a source chief sense without importing the existing European peerage hierarchy. Mules stays the supplied KJV reading, not a harmonized hot-springs alternative.

## 6. Pain, death and material acts need their own senses

The new childbirth-labour and birth-helper terms distinguish travail from generic work and midwifery from an imposed modern profession. Rachel's soul departing retains its metaphysical wording; Isaac's gave up the ghost uses the established release-breath construction. The difference remains philosophically awkward rather than modernized into a single clinical description. Loins is the body-region/descent metaphor, not an anatomical diagnosis.

Physical rending is cut-pull, separate from the inherited eye-tear homonym. Dipping a coat in blood uses an explicit liquid-immersion sense. Balm, myrrh and spices remain separate cargo categories; no medicinal efficacy is asserted. A sheaf is a tied grain bundle, not a single seed. The supplied many-colored coat is not replaced with a different scholarly rendering. Temporary wording errors found during review—marry as a mild oath, generic conceive, and draw as drawing—were replaced with the established wed, pregnancy-conceive and pull senses before compilation of the checkpoint.

## 7. Tamar: embodied I, lineage, allegation and evidence

The daughter-in-law / father-in-law terms use analytic son's-wife and husband's-father chains. Seed-line can belong to the dead brother as a descent institution while the living man supplies bodily paternity; Onan's withholding remains this specific narrated act, not a general sexual rule inserted by the translator. The source sexual euphemisms went in and knew are retained and explained in the verse notes. Pregnancy takes the dedicated `kjv_conceive` sense, avoiding a word for mental conception.

Harlot as a role or perception is distinguished from the insulting comparison used by Dinah's brothers. Judah's belief, the local men's denial and the later accusation remain attributed statements. Tamar's I am with child is personal singular-I; it asserts her embodied position rather than turning her into a household abstraction. Signet, bracelets and staff preserve three evidence categories. The signet is not assumed to be a ring; bracelets are not replaced with cords. More righteous uses the ethical adjective, not merely. Judah's admission stays comparative and causal: he withheld Shelah. The narrative's violence and coercion are neither softened nor endorsed.

## 8. Cry, quantity and archival cadence

Reuben's The child is not and the midwife's How hast thou broken forth carry native `:excl`; the target never inserts the unsupported exclamation glyph. The birth sequence distinguishes the first emerging hand, its scarlet thread, the withdrawn hand, and the brother who is delivered before the marked child. Pharez and Zarah follow the source naming order.

Checks separately verify four hundred men, seven bows, one day of overdriving, a hundred money-pieces, third day and two attackers, twelve sons, Isaac's 180 years, Joseph's seventeen years, eleven stars, twenty silver-pieces and approximately three months. Unta's numeral base does not change these quantities. Repeated conjunctions, genealogical titles, awkward comparison and reported confidence remain where they carry the archaic register. The exact source column is preserved.
