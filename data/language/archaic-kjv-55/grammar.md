# KJV historical register 0.10-kjv.55 — contested succession and personal liability

This continuation adds99 individually authored verses,1Kings1–2. The whole Bible has8,865 of31,102 authored verses;22,237 remain. The deliverable remains ONE complete Bible in ONE `.vay`, not these chapters as a completed delivery. Parent0.10-kjv.54, all earlier corrected targets and frozen Jonah remain unchanged. This historical edition is permanently excluded from modernization. These notes are translator self-review, not independent literary certification.

Fourteen lexical records are added: eight proper-name/identity records and six common senses. No new inflectional ending or possessive paradigm is introduced. Natural variation comes from discourse-sensitive reference, inherited constructions and explicit historical senses, not random pronoun alternation.

## 1. Claiming a royal plural-self is not the same as possessing legitimate authority

Adonijah's **I will be king** in1:5 takes `ipl`: he performs the identity of ruler and ruling body before recognized accession. The narrator has not certified the claim. In2:15 his retrospective **the kingdom was mine / that I should reign** likewise speaks from that claimed plural-self. Immediately surrounding petitions use personal `i`, especially1:51 and2:14,16–17. A claimant can invoke a body politic in one clause and plead for his own bodily survival in another.

David's succession oath and appointment in1:13,17,24,30,35 use `ipl` and `mypl` inside the appropriate quotation. The questioned authorization for Adonijah in1:24 stays a question. A quoted royal pronoun does not turn the question into a fact. David's summons to his physical presence in1:28,32 remain personal; the same applies to his own mule and son in1:33.

## 2. Personal eyes, shared allegiance and the boundary of a divine quotation

In1:48 **my throne** is `mypl/throne`, the continuing royal office; **mine eyes** is `my/eye:pl`, the aged person's seeing body. The distinction is intentionally local within one sentence. Personal soul-rescue in1:29 remains `my/soul`, not an institution's rescue. The dying **I go the way of all the earth** in2:2 is personal `i`.

Shared **our lord** in1:11,43,47 uses `ourone`, the singular-whole royal household/polity relation. It is neither a number of kings nor an obligatory label for every collection of people. The named pair Joab and Abiathar in1:7 is actual `they:du`. Abner and Amasa in2:5,32 are actual dual victims; their blood in2:33 is explicitly **of they:du**. A pair is not dissolved into singular-we merely because it acts or suffers together.

In2:4 David's outer **concerning me** is personal; the LORD's quoted **before me** is divine `ipl`. The if-clause governing descendants' conduct remains intact. No Trinity person-count or later doctrine is inserted into this promise. The user's plural-I preference is applied at an evidenced divine voice boundary, not sprayed across every first-person form.

## 3. Nested quotations reset the speaker

Bathsheba reports an oath, Nathan asks about a possible different oath, David restates and enacts his oath, and Jonathan reports the ceremony. Repetition is evidential structure, not a defect to remove for stylistic variation. Each quoted `ipl` belongs to the king inside that quotation; it does not make Bathsheba or Nathan a royal plural-self.

In2:30 Joab's **I will die here** is personal `i`; Benaiah's following **he answered me** is another personal centre. In2:42 royal `ipl` frames Solomon's accusation while the nested **the word I have heard is good** returns to Shimei's personal `i`. This is a local speaker-reset rule suitable for later historical and prophetic dialogue.

## 4. Promises and violent contradictions stay visible

David's recollection in2:8 retains his oath not to kill Shimei with the sword. His next instruction nevertheless calls for Shimei's grey head to descend with blood. Neither is removed, modernized into a consistent legal theory, or excused by an added narrator clause. The royal injury in2:5 is `ipl`; the personally remembered flight and assistance in2:7–8 stay `i`.

Solomon's **I will not say thee nay** to his mother in2:20 is a personal filial promise. His angry refusal and kingdom accusation in2:22 remain beside it. His self-curse in2:23 uses personal `i`, the bearer of the threatened curse; the dynastic establishment and execution decision in2:24 use `ipl`. Withholding Abiathar's execution is explicitly **at this time**, not an unconditional lifetime pardon.

Solomon's claim that executing Joab removes innocent blood from himself and his father's house in2:31 uses royal `ipl`, with personal father-kinship retained. The statement that David did not know the earlier killings remains Solomon's asserted exculpation. The translation preserves attribution rather than independently endorsing the king's innocence, divine authorization or predictions of perpetual peace.

## 5. Frailty, sexuality and embodied court acts remain awkward

David obtains no heat despite clothes. The servants propose a young virgin to cherish him and lie in his bosom. The text then explicitly says he **knew her not**. The translation preserves the historical euphemism and negation, with a semantic note; it invents neither intercourse nor consent nor a medical diagnosis. Later requests that Abishag be **given as wife** remain objectifying as supplied. They do not become a statement of her wishes.

`kjv_obeisance` is bow-honour, court bodily homage, distinct from asserting devotional worship of a king. David's bed-bow in1:47 is on the bed, not silently moved to a throne. Solomon rises and bows to his mother before seating her at his spatial right. The act does not independently establish co-regency. The personal-eye/royal-throne distinction preserves both bodily and institutional relations.

## 6. Historical senses are explicit, not accidental homographs

`kjv_resound` is sound-return: the city's **rang again** in1:45 does not require a second coronation. `kjv_warn_solemn` shares the inherited warning form but records **protested** as a solemn warning speech act, not a public demonstration. `kjv_elder_sibling` is old-more, relative kin age, distinct from `kjv_elder`, a household/community office. `kjv_horseman` is horse-ride, a specifically mounted rider. `kjv_grip` is hold-strong for catching altar horns; it asserts a gesture, not automatic sanctuary immunity.

Inherited distinctions remain active: room is `kjv_place_site` when it means an office; hoar is aged `kjv_gray`, not hoarfrost; hosts is army rather than guests; ark is `kjv_ark_chest` rather than Noah's vessel; bodily right is `kjv_right_side`, not moral right; coasts are territorial boundaries rather than only shorelines. **Well** in2:18 is assent, not `kjv_well` water. Commands and judgments remain alongside statutes and testimony in the legal list. Acclamations use the language's exclamatory morphology, never an unsupported exclamation glyph.

## 7. Names carry local evidence without forced identity harmonization

Abishag, Shunammite, Rei and Zoheleth receive explicit source forms. Gihon in the accession procession receives a local identity record sharing the earlier form; it is not automatically the Genesis river. The unqualified court Shimei in1:8 is not silently equated with Gera's son or a Levitical namesake.

Jether as Amasa's father in2:5,32 is preserved beside the earlier supplied Ithra wording; no harmonized name is substituted. Maachah as Achish's father in2:39 remains beside the earlier supplied Maoch. The local records do not claim these are necessarily different historical persons merely because the source forms differ. Likewise1:6's **his mother bare him after Absalom** is retained without asserting that Haggith bore Absalom. Attribution and identity uncertainty are part of the versioned reference.

## 8. Quantities, restrictions and conditional refuge retain their limits

The fifty runners in1:5 are distinct from chariots and horsemen. David's forty-year total in2:11 remains seven years at Hebron plus thirty-three at Jerusalem; the translation does not import six months from an earlier account. Two commanders are two named victims. Three years and two fleeing servants in2:39 remain separate quantities.

Adonijah's altar refuge ends with conditional protection, not an unconditional pardon. Shimei's initial restriction in2:36 is broad, the next verse names Kidron, and his later journey is to Gath. The translation does not invent a Kidron crossing for that trip. Solomon's restated general prohibition stays in his speech. These distinctions matter to the argument without turning the translation into an added legal verdict.
