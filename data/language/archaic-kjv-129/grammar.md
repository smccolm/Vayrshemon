## 1. Human I, communal we and a physically paired invitation

Personal-I belongs to the poem's human speakers and quoted human speech. The chorus/shared promise in1:4,1:11,2:15,5:9,6:1and6:13uses singular-we,`wesg`. The family's collective deliberation8:8–9also uses singular-we,with`ourone`for their shared sister. These groups act as one discursive body without being one physical body. In7:11–13,the speaker specifically invites the beloved to accompany them;`we:du`marks the two physical participants. Shared household/garden possessions elsewhere use communal`ourone`without inferring an exact head count. No divine plural-I is introduced merely because later traditions read the poem allegorically.
