# Refuge, testimony and uneasy social boundaries · 0.10-kjv.29

Working continuation, Deuteronomy 19–24: 141 individually authored verses, retaining all prior corrected targets and frozen Jonah. This is an incomplete whole-Bible checkpoint. No new inflectional endings or change to the approved script/font treatment. Translator self-review is recorded separately from structural validation.

## Different groups speaking differently

The elders' hands and eyes in 21:7 belong to `wepl`: separate witnesses deny their own involvement or sight. This is not the corporate singular-we taking responsibility for every person's act. In 21:20 the father and mother speak as an actual pair, `of we:du son` and `of we:du voice`. The accusation is theirs. A married household is not automatically recast as a mystical plural-I when the source explicitly presents two speakers. The husband and father in 22:14–17 retain personal `i` and `my`; the father quotes the husband's I inside his own statement. Possessors follow the current quotation level.

## Protected killing versus ambush

19:4–6 requires killing without prior hatred, exemplified by an axe-head leaving its handle. `kjv_manslayer` does not prejudge murder. A long road and hot-hearted pursuer create danger even though the killer is not death-worthy. The ambush case in 19:11–13 includes prior hatred and intentional lethal attack; refuge does not shield that case. Three additional cities are besides the earlier three. Old boundary markers are protected separately. No-pity wording is kept attached to its stated case rather than generalized to all slayers.

## Testimony, intention and matched penalties

One witness cannot establish a charge; two or three can. Both disputants stand before authorities, who investigate before judging a witness false. The false witness receives what they intended to inflict, not necessarily harm already completed. Life/eye/tooth/hand/foot pairs remain exact. Parents and children cannot substitute for each other's capital liability in 24:16. That rule remains beside earlier rhetoric of inherited punishment; the translator does not silently consolidate them into a modern uniform legal doctrine.

## Unequal war rules and limited protections

The peace offer in 20:10 leads to tribute and service, not equality. Distant cities and inherited peoples' cities receive different stated treatment; no-breathing-survivor language remains explicit historical source speech. The source's six names are retained without modern ethnic identifications. House, vineyard, betrothal and fear exemptions are separate. Food trees may not be cut for siege while non-food trees may be used for siege banks. The supplied tree-is-human-life parenthesis is retained, without replacing it with another translation's interpretation.

## The unresolved death and the blood-bearing land

The nearest city is determined by measurement, not presumed guilt. The heifer has not worked or drawn in a yoke; the valley is unploughed and unsown. Eared is agricultural, not a hearing organ. Elders wash over the animal and testify; priests adjudicate. Blood is forgiven and blood-guilt removed. These literal metaphors remain awkward. Same-day burial of a hanged body prevents land defilement; no later execution device or unprinted ritual is inserted.

## Captivity, marriage and hostile evidence

The captive woman's month of mourning, subsequent marriage and prohibition on selling her if rejected retain their coercive context. No consent or romance is invented. Humbling is expressed causatively, `humble make`, rather than accidentally saying the captor himself became humble. In 22 the bride's alleged virginity tokens remain the source's claimed evidence, not medical certification. The city/field contrast, force, cry and absence of a rescuer retain the text's own troubling distinctions. The field victim has no death-worthy sin; the case is compared to a neighbour's murder. Monetary amounts, lifetime no-dismissal clauses, and penalties stay exact without added graphic scenes or endorsement.

## Labels whose history is not modernized away

Bastard is the source's derogatory birth-status label, not the translator's valuation of a child. The word `kjv_sodomite_label` preserves the supplied label without assigning a modern sexual orientation or silently choosing a disputed replacement. Dog-price stays dog-price. Testicular injury is anatomical; the privy member remains secret body-part, preserving the euphemism. The distinct exclusions for Ammon/Moab and eventual admission for Edom/Egypt remain separate. Tenth generation, third generation and forever are not harmonized into one eligibility rule.

## Asylum, debt and food access

An escaped servant is not returned, chooses a dwelling and must not be oppressed. That stands beside earlier bondage regulations. Interest is forbidden to brother and allowed to stranger in 23:20, while wage protection explicitly covers both brother and stranger in 24:14. A lender waits outside the debtor's house; poor-person clothing returns by sundown. Neither millstone can be collateral because it is the person's life. Forgotten sheaf, un-rebeaten olive branches and ungleaned grapes remain for stranger, fatherless and widow. Immediate hand-eating from another's crop is allowed, carrying off in a vessel or harvesting with a sickle is not.

## Ordinary tools and deliberately bounded new senses

Axe is wood-cut, its helve hand-wood; boundary marker is boundary-mark. A roof barrier prevents falling, distinct from a siege bank. Fingernail is bodily, not a fastening spike. `kjv_fine_penalty` is monetary punishment, not fine quality. The spade belongs to camp sanitation, not boating. `kjv_divorce_document` is a written object handed to the woman. The unspecified uncleanness charged in 24:1 receives a separate recorded sense sharing the impurity form; no disease, precise act or general ritual rule is invented. The new house-inauguration sense shares the earlier inauguration form without declaring every house an altar or irrevocable sacred property.
