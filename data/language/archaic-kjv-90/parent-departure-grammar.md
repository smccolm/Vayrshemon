## 1. A claimed whole is not an objective certificate

Abijah opens13:4with personal-I, identifying the individual who demands a hearing. In13:10–12he moves to `wesg` and `ourone`: his covenantal side claims to be one obedient body. The contrast with plural `you:pl` is deliberate, but it remains his accusatory speech. Singular-we does not certify that every member is faithful or that the opposing side is illegitimate. The resulting unity is asserted politically and ritually, not proven by pronoun morphology.

Asa's civic building speech14:7also uses singular-we for the common land and undertaking. His military plea14:11uses the same whole for a dependent army-community. The repeated we-have-sought is retained. Azariah's me15:2and Asa's transactional letter16:3remain personal-I. Rank alone does not cause plural-I; no divine first-person speech occurs in this tranche, so the established plural-self is not forced into third-person narration. Spirit15:1is not turned into an additional counted speaker. No dual is inferred merely from two named tribes or nations.

## 2. Rhetoric and genealogical seams remain exposed

Michaiah daughter of Uriel13:2is retained beside the prior Maachah daughter of Absalom. Maachah as Asa's mother15:16is not rewritten grandmother. Azariah son of Oded receives the Spirit15:1, yet15:8names Oded's prophecy: the source's shift remains. Iddo is now called prophet rather than the earlier seer without inventing another consulted manuscript. Hanani the seer has an explicit local identity; neither sameness nor difference from every earlier Hanani is silently asserted.

Rehoboam is described as young and tenderhearted13:7inside Abijah's speech despite the earlier accession age41. Both remain. Names identify locally stated referents, not a harmonized family tree or a modern map. Ephraim among captured cities13:19is a city, distinct from tribe and ancestor. Abelmaim16:4is not replaced with Abelbethmaachah from the parallel account.

## 4. Holiness can be violent without translator approval

The salt covenant13:5remains a material image, not an invented ritual explanation. Belial-children, no-gods, strange gods and abominable idols remain attributed polemical terms. The charge13:10–12is Abijah's, while later evaluations belong to the narrator. Furniture-table13:11uses the physical table key, not a writing-table root. Cultic groves remain distinct from ordinary woodland.

The death requirement for nonseekers15:13explicitly covers small and great, man and woman. The adjacent rejoicing is not editorial endorsement. Battle slaughter, city plunder, livestock seizure and royal oppression retain their violence. Translation does not repair these by euphemism or an invented moral justification.

## 5. A perfect heart can coexist with a contradictory story

High places removed in Judah14:3/5stand beside high places remaining in Israel15:17. Their local domains are retained, without making the apparent tension vanish. Asa's heart is called perfect all his days15:17, yet the seer16:9condemns his foolishness and announces wars. The LORD's running eyes keep their bodily strangeness. The source's evaluation is not converted into a logically consistent modern psychological profile.

The reliance rebuke16:7says Syria's army escaped Asa, even though he enlisted Syria against Israel. Syria remains the named escaped host. The treaty16:3uses the covenantal-league sense rather than the distance unit. The two treasuries and reused Ramah building materials remain concrete objects, preserving the political transaction and its cost.

## 6. Illness, aromatics and an unspecified burning

Asa's diseased feet16:12and his seeking physicians instead of the LORD remain the source's contrasting claim. This is not advice against medical treatment. Sleep-with-fathers16:13retains the death idiom. His prepared tombs, fragrant bed and aromatic compounders16:14remain distinct from the great burning made for him. The source does not state that his body is the object burned, so the translation does not insert cremation. `kjv_apothecary` keeps its historical aromatic-compounder sense rather than a modern professional license.
