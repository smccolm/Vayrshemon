## Personal complaints and communal appetites
The imagined stranger and eunuch56:3 use personal-I. The speaker fetching wine56:12 likewise volunteers with personal-I,but the drinking clique then uses singular-we and our-one self. This marks a group presenting itself as one appetite,not a judgment that communal selfhood belongs only to virtuous people. No physical plural-we is needed in this section,and no arbitrary dual is introduced.
