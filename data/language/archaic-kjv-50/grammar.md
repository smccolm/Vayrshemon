## 1. Plural divine self, personal petitioner, collective army

The LORD's covenant speech uses `ipl` and `mypl` through 7:5–15. The speaking self walks with Israel, brings it from Egypt, commands its tribes, appoints its ruler and establishes a dynasty. These are distributed actions of the same covenant identity, not a count of separate gods. In 7:14 the deliberately uncomfortable construction is **I(pl.self) will be his father; he will be my(pl.self) son**. The source's immediately following **if he commits iniquity** remains. No later christological identification, incapacity to sin or Trinity person-count is inserted. The grammar can sustain the user's desired plurality-in-identity while the translation still preserves what this passage actually says.

David's fear, dancing, self-abasement and prayer retain personal `i` in chapter6 and 7:18. His own bodily performance does not become plural merely because he is a king. In 7:22 the heard testimony uses `wepl` with **ears of we(pl)**, preserving multiple witnesses. In 10:11 Joab's `ipl` means the commander acting with his particular force; in 10:12 `wesg` and `ourone` join the separated forces into one obligated people-body. These shifts follow referents and agency, not a global first-person substitution.

## 2. Quotation boundaries are grammatical boundaries

Nathan's initial approval in 7:3 precedes the nighttime oracle in 7:4; it is not retroactively presented as that oracle. The divine first person continues only inside its own speech. At 7:11 the source itself says **the LORD tells thee that he will make thee a house**, so the third-person `he` is retained within the larger first-person oracle. David's prayer resumes a different speaker. At 7:27 his quoted **I will build thee a house** switches back to divine `ipl`, then returns to **thy servant** as David.

At 9:11 Ziba first promises obedience. The supplied **said the king** then assigns **my table** to David. `mypl/table` appears after that explicit boundary, not in Ziba's mouth. At 10:2 David's present emissary action uses plural-self but the remembered recipient of Nahash's kindness is personal `i`. Such local shifts make the old register more lived-in without making it arbitrary.

## 3. House, seed, bowels and the old material metaphors

The same house root links David's cedar dwelling, the tent proposed for the ark, the house promised to David and the descendant's building for the divine name. Its dynasty use is documented as an extension of the concrete house image in this historical construction; the inherited lexical entry is not silently overwritten. This preserves the passage's repeated word rather than assigning unrelated modern terms to each occurrence.

The people are **planted**, the descendant comes from **bowels**, David will **sleep with fathers**, the name is **made great**, and prayer is **found in the heart**. These material expressions remain conspicuous. The difficult **is this the manner of man?** in 7:19 stays a question. In 7:23 the supplied shifts among himself, you, thy and thou are retained rather than repaired into smooth modern prose. The nations' **gods(pl)** remain lexical plurality, distinct from the grammatical plural divine self.

## 4. Uzzah, Michal and discomfort that belongs to the source

The oxen **shake it** in 6:6; the translation does not substitute an explicit stumbling claim. Uzzah takes hold, God strikes him for his error, and David is **displeased**, then afraid. The discomfort is neither excused away nor intensified with invented motives. The ark's three months with Obededom bless his entire household. Six paces belong to the procession, not the number of sacrifices.

Michal's sarcastic question in 6:20 uses the grammatical exclamatory form instead of an unavailable exclamation-mark glyph. Uncovering shamelessly is her accusation. David's answer preserves the humiliating **more vile** and **base in my own sight**, alongside prospective honor from the handmaids. The closing childlessness statement retains therefore and until death without inventing a physiological cause or an unreported marital arrangement.

## 5. Instrument and official vocabularies retain their distinctions

Seven common entries are added: `kjv_fir`, `kjv_cymbal`, `kjv_flagon`, `kjv_sheepcote`, `kjv_record_keeper`, `kjv_dedicate` and `kjv_scribe`. The fir label makes no precise modern botanical identification. Cymbal is a struck plate instrument distinct from timbrel, cornet, harp and psaltery. The supplied fir-wood wording is retained even where its scope over the instrument list feels awkward. Flagon means the source's wine vessel without a new volume. The KJV allotment of bread, flesh and wine is not changed to a different textual reading about raisin cakes.

Recorder as an official cannot use the existing recorder-flute entry. The new record-person and write-person compounds keep recorder and scribe distinguishable in 8:16–17. The existing `kjv_stripe_blow` already covers bodily chastisement, so no duplicate stripe entry was coined; the contrasting visual stripe remains a separate sense. `kjv_dedicate` marks sacred setting apart, distinct from generic giving or vowing. Existing `kjv_hough`, `kjv_please_satisfy`, `kjv_uncover`, `kjv_ark_chest`, `wagon` and `chariot` are reused in their appropriate senses.

## 6. Named identity is not spelling alone

Nathan the prophet is tracked separately from David's son Nathan while sharing the same written form. Rehob as Hadadezer's father is distinct from Rehob as a locality. Machir son of Ammiel at Lodebar and that Ammiel each receive identity records separate from their earlier namesakes. Maacah in the king/polity passage is distinguished from David's wife; the KJV's king Maacah construction is not overresolved into a new asserted personal name.

The supplied **Ahimelech son of Abiathar** in 8:17 remains, even though an earlier narrative gives Abiathar as Ahimelech's son. A separate record retains the local statement without silently reversing it or inventing an explanation. Hadadezer and Hadarezer retain their supplied forms; variant spelling alone is not a claim of two kings. Zoba is retained beside Zobah, and Micha beside earlier Micah. Abinadab in chapter6 remains the ark-householder, not Saul's son or Jesse's son.

## 7. Quantity and violence are neither sanitized nor harmonized

The audit checks thirty thousand chosen men, three months and six paces; two measured lines for death against one full line to keep alive; one thousand chariots, seven hundred horsemen, twenty thousand footmen and horses reserved for one hundred chariots; twenty-two thousand and eighteen thousand casualties; Ziba's fifteen sons and twenty servants; half-beards; hired forces of twenty thousand, one thousand and twelve thousand; and men of seven hundred chariots with forty thousand horsemen.

The additive/multiplicative constructions explicitly produce 22,000 and 18,000 rather than 20,002 or 10,008. **Men of700 chariots** is not rewritten as700 men. Enough horses for100 chariots is not claimed to be exactly100 horses. The source's700 horsemen is not changed to7000, its horsemen are not replaced with footmen, and **Syrians** in the valley of salt is not changed to Edomites. The Moab line selection remains bodily and brutal without manufacturing an exact individual casualty percentage.

## 8. Kindness, disability and attributed suspicion

Mephibosheth's reception retains the dead-dog self-description and the continuous royal table. Land labor and table privilege remain side by side rather than becoming a single simplified food arrangement. Saul **thy father** keeps the source's ancestral kinship usage even after Jonathan's fatherhood is explicitly stated. Mephibosheth remains lame in both feet at the conclusion; honor does not imply an invented cure.

David's condolence mission is described before Ammon's princes accuse it of spying. Their accusation stays attributed, not narrator-certified. Shaving half the beards and cutting garments to the buttocks remain explicit humiliations. The stench metaphor is retained. Joab's closing wish leaves the LORD to do what seems good; an optative avoids inserting a promise of victory where the speaker has not made one.
