## 1. Acting-I, personal-I, kin-body-we and the observing pair

Plural-I is a perspective on distributed agency, not a requirement attached to rank.2:1 uses `ipl` for David relocating the band whose wives and households follow.2:6 uses it for his effective public promise of reciprocity.2:7 retains personal-I as the individual anointed by Judah. The alternation makes a leader both a person and the acting center of a body.

Abner's offended defense in3:8 is personal-I. His undertaking to transfer political power in3:9 and his negotiating hand in3:12 are plural-self and its possessive. At3:21 he personally rises and goes, but his plural-self gathers Israel through his following. The switch occurs within one speech rather than being fixed for a character forever.

At4:10 David says I slew the messenger, although1:15 explicitly had him order another man to do the killing. `ipl` expresses that command-mediated ownership without rewriting the earlier execution.4:11 extends public enforcement while dual-you addresses the two brothers actually before him. This is a concrete application of the user's plural-I intention, not arbitrary replacement of a singular form.

At5:1 the many tribes declare we(sg.whole) are thy bone and thy flesh. Singular-we sustains a kin-body claim across5:2. At2:14 Abner invites a performance before us(dual), the two observing commanders; this is a genuine pair rather than the whole armies compressed into two.3:28 preserves personal-I AND my kingdom as separately coordinated terms because the source makes that distinction in David's disavowal.

## 2. Divine agency without a fabricated head count

Abner quotes the LORD's promise in3:18 using plural-I and its people/servant possessives: divine agency acts through David and Israel. At5:19 David's plural-I asks about his army, and the LORD's plural-I answers with a promise. Quotation boundaries distinguish those referents. They do not fuse God and David into one grammatical individual.

5:20 continues David's plural-self in his enemies and before me, linking his claim to his force.5:23 changes the prior go-up order to do-not-go-up and a circuit behind the enemy.5:24 retains a sound of going in the treetops and the LORD going ahead. Neither an exact number of divine persons nor a named supernatural troop is inserted.

This establishes context-sensitive precedent for later divine identity passages while leaving their actual translation to their own text. It does not claim the present military narratives already state the Trinity.

## 3. Reported death and command-mediated responsibility

1Samuel31 narrates Saul's death with a sword and an armor-bearer.2Samuel1 presents an Amalekite's first-person claim to have killed Saul after finding him on his spear. The second account remains a report; it is not edited into a missing final stage of the first account. David asks how the man knows, then condemns him by his own testimony.

1:7–10 contains personal-I belonging to the messenger, with quoted Saul speaking personally inside the report.1:16 repeats the messenger's alleged I-slew-the-anointed as the incriminating quotation. It must not become David claiming to have killed Saul. David's later I-slew-the-messenger at4:10, by contrast, owns an act performed through his commanded agent.

The brothers bringing Ishbosheth's head claim divine vengeance at4:8. Their speech is not narrator-certified approval. David rejects it and commands their execution. Preserve both the attributed claim and the violent punishment without invented endorsement or physical elaboration.

## 4. Lament allows deliberate repetition

The fallen-mighty refrain at1:19,25,27 repeats because it is a lament. Natural variation does not justify substituting new imagery merely to avoid recurrence. The existing exclamatory form carries the force without an unsupported Thimfora exclamation glyph. Explicit Maxima mapping remains in force.

Saul and Jonathan are praised together as lovely, swift and strong despite earlier conflicts. This is the lament's voice, not a retrospective deletion of those conflicts. David's personal grief for Jonathan and the love-surpassing-women comparison at1:26 retain their intensity. The line about anointing at1:21 keeps the supplied he-reference beside Saul's shield. The bow instruction and Jasher citation at1:18 remain the supplied wording, without silently substituting a proposed alternative title for the poem.

## 5. Competing houses, claims and public innocence

Judah's anointing at2:4 is distinct from all-Israel anointing at5:3. Ishbosheth's forty-year age and two-year reign remain beside David's seven-year-six-month Hebron interval. The translator does not invent a chronological bridge to remove their mismatch.

Abner's accusation of treachery by Joab at3:25 stays Joab's claim. David's lack of knowledge of the recall is narrated at3:26. David's own disavowal at3:28 and the people's understanding at3:37 remain separate evidential layers.3:30 names both Joab and Abishai as killers while3:27 names Joab as the direct assailant. ## 8. Quantities and chronology without corrective arithmetic
