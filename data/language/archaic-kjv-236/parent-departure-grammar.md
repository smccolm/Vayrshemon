## One human body, a divided speaking self

The personal I of 7:1 and 7:7–13 introduces the argument and the retrospective encounter with law. The community's questions and knowledge use singular-whole we. In 7:14–24 the passage explicitly separates what the speaker wills, approves, hates and does, then names sin as an indwelling actor. That sustained division supports a scoped human plural-self. It is not triggered merely by distress, grief, deceit or an emotional outburst.

The plural-self I and its my-plural possessive refer to the same human through the passage. Man, inward man, flesh, mind and body keep their supplied singular nouns; bodily members remain plural. Wretched man that I-plural am is intentionally uncomfortable. There is no added claim of multiple literal humans, named personalities, a medical condition or a divine identity. This follows the earlier historical principle that one speaking identity can foreground several agencies or constituents, while ordinary personal statements remain personal.

The first clause of 7:25 returns to personal I for thanksgiving through Jesus Christ our-one Lord. Its second clause explicitly resumes I-plural myself in the mind/flesh service contrast. The shift is recorded rather than silently treating all first-person statements alike. The reflexive plural-self marks co-reference within the same speaker, not a new interlocutor. The personal me of 8:2, personal assessments of 8:18/38 and sorrowing personal I of 9:1–3 confirm that the chapter-7 choice is locally motivated. Sorrow alone in 9:2 does not generate plural-self.

## Law, command and desire

The marriage analogy preserves both branches: living husband and released bond at his death. Dead-to-law through Christ's body, marriage to the raised other and fruit-bearing remain joined images. The source's that-being-dead-wherein-held construction in 7:6 remains rather than importing a different reading about we having died.

Motions of sins means stirring passions, not spatial movements. Concupiscence shares the established lust form with a recorded contextual sense covering desire in the commandment argument. Once in 7:9 means formerly here, not an assertion of exactly one occurrence. Allow in 7:15 means approve, not permit. These differences matter because the argument distinguishes knowledge, approval, willing and acting.

The law remains holy and the command holy, righteous and good. Good becoming death is questioned and emphatically rejected before the sin-working-through-good explanation. The repeated not-doing-the-willed and doing-the-hated clauses are retained; lexical variation would obscure the very division the plural-self makes visible. God forbid and the wretched-man cry use existing exclamatory morphology without unsupported punctuation glyphs.

## Spirit, our spirit and creation

The complete KJV walk-not-after-flesh clause in 8:1 remains. The source's me in 8:2 remains personal rather than being changed to you. In 8:3 the Son comes in the likeness of sinful flesh and for sin; neither relation is omitted, and the translation does not assert that the Son committed sin. Body dead because sin and Spirit life because righteousness in 8:10 keeps the predicate life, not a substitution of merely alive.

God's Spirit, Christ's Spirit, Spirit itself and our-one spirit retain their separate supplied relations. The collective we can possess plural hearts or weaknesses while our body in 8:23 remains singular. Singular-whole possession does not require literally sharing an organ. The source itself in 8:16/26 is preserved rather than modernized to a different pronoun. Abba and Father both remain in the cry.

Creature in 8:19–21 is the created order collectively; the individual creature in 8:39 retains the ordinary creature term. Creation's unwilling subjection, its bondage of decay, its groaning and childbirth labour remain metaphors of the created order. The subject verb now explicitly means under-bring, not the inherited subject-as-topic noun. Mind as a verb means directing attention or concern, distinguished from the faculty noun. No modern cosmological account is inserted.

The Spirit's intercession is for-asking, an explicitly recorded advocacy relation. Unutterable groans remain unutterable; no invented prayer words or audible language replace them. The heart-searcher knows the Spirit's mind, and Christ also intercedes at God's spatial right hand. These relations remain distinct without counting divine persons or collapsing the community into a divine speaker.

## Sequence, negation and scope

Foreknowing and before-appointing are distinct verbs in 8:29–30, followed by calling, justifying and glorifying. The specified purpose is conformity to the Son's image. No later systematic theory is added to settle the scope of the sequence. All things work together for good to God-lovers and the called according to purpose in 8:28 retains its subject and recipient conditions; it is not changed to a different textual formulation.

The separation catalogue keeps tribulation, distress, persecution, famine, nakedness, peril and sword. The later catalogue retains death, life, angels, ruling authorities, powers, present things, coming things, height, depth and other creature. Principalities are ruling powers, not a principal sum of money. The final negative governs the continued list. No invented arithmetic turns the catalogue into a count of divine components.

## Quoted divine self and uncomfortable election

Divine I in 9:9/13/15/17/25/33 uses plural-self; divine my in 9:17/25/26 uses its corresponding possessive. Sarah, Jacob, Esau, Pharaoh and the people addressed remain their source referents. The hypothetical pot's me in 9:20 is personal singular, not the divine plural-self simply because it occurs inside a quotation. The communal we of 9:14/24/29/30 remains singular-whole.

The source's could-wish-accursed-from-Christ in 9:3 is retained as wishing, not an actual accomplished curse. The six listed relations of 9:4 include plural covenants and giving of law. The who-over-all-God-blessed construction in 9:5 remains as supplied, without silently choosing a different punctuation or rewriting it into a doctrinal paraphrase.

Not all Israel of Israel and not all Abraham-seed children retain their repeated terms. Seed is descendant-line, not plant seed. The one father in 9:10 is explicit. Before-birth and neither-good-nor-evil in 9:11 remain with choosing-purpose; election is nominal choosing rather than a civic ballot. Loved Jacob and hated Esau are not softened. Mercy and compassion repeat separately in 9:15 rather than being flattened into a single benevolence term.

Wrath-vessels fitted for destruction in 9:22 keeps an unspecified fitter. Mercy-vessels he prepared beforehand in 9:23 has its explicit agent. The asymmetry is not repaired by inserting the same actor into both clauses. The one vessel from the same lump in 9:21 is counted; another is left relative. In the Osee quotation, her beloved retains feminine reference alongside the people clause. The full finish-and-cut-short wording of 9:28 remains. Stumbling offence in 9:33 receives a distinct nominal sense rather than the earlier moral-transgression meaning of offence.
