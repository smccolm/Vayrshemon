## Individual crisis and the community's singular self

Psalm73's envy, apparent futility, painful questioning and later reconsideration belong to an individual speaker. Ordinary-I remains even when the testimony is used publicly. Psalm77 similarly retains the individual crying voice, running sore, insomnia, inability to speak and inward conversation. None is a clinical diagnosis. By contrast74:1's rejected congregation speaks as `wesg`, while74:9distinguishes physical plural observers (`wepl`) from their community's shared signs (`ourone`) and lack of a prophet among the common whole. The corporate myKing in74:12uses `mypl`; it does not assert multiple kings. Psalm78:3–4uses singular-we for inherited memory and the duty to pass it on. The ancestors and children remain plural people, not one biological individual. Psalms79and80retain one community's humiliation, appeal and promise through `wesg` and `ourone`. These decisions follow discourse and reference, not random prefix variation.

## Three controlled lexical additions and contextual senses

The new common entries are synagogue (`gather+house`), wine-dregs (`wine+remainder`) and steadfast (`stand+strong`). Synagogue retains the source category without a silent chronological repair. Wine-dregs identifies sediment rather than ordinary unused wine. Steadfast names firm commitment rather than the inherited stable building sense. There are no new proper names, inflectional endings, Maxima pairs or compiler rules. Most work is careful reuse: bands are bindings, bow is the weapon, lie is false speech, right-hand is a side, desert is dry land rather than earned merit, meat is food unless the source specifies flesh, prevent is come-before rather than obstruct, quicken is make-alive rather than accelerate, line is measuring cord, and firstborn is passive birth order. Illumination light occurs only in74:16,77:18and78:14. Reins are kidneys; a given hand is not always a generic power substitute. These distinctions keep intentional historical awkwardness separate from homograph mistakes.

## Quantities and the limits of numerical interpretation

Psalm79:12has sevenfold repayment: exact multiplier7,not seven recipients. Psalm78:51's firstborn identifies birth order1,not one slain person. Psalm76:7's once-anger means onset and is expressed as becoming angry, not an asserted single occurrence. Generic one in73:20/25,75:7and78:65and HolyOne in78:41are not cardinal counts. Cherubim is plural but unnumbered. The three supplied tribal names in80:2are retained as names without inventing an explicit counting sentence. Great tear-measure,heaps,feathers,dust and sea-sand abundance remain unmeasured. No divine-person total is inferred from any of these expressions.
