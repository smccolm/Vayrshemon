## Opposed communities can each say singular-we

The approaching builders in4:2 use `wesg` for their claimed worshipping body. In4:3 the Israelite leadership also uses `wesg`,but draws an exclusive boundary:you have nothing with us;we together ourselves will build. `ourone/god` belongs to that second claim. Shared grammar expresses each speaker's claim to corporate identity;it neither endorses both claims nor merges their referents. As-one-man in3:1 remains a simile with cardinal `one_num`,not an assertion of one physical body. Do not replace the explicit opposition with an invented conciliatory interpretation.

## Imperial plural-self and the court-body

Cyrus's commissioned self in1:2 uses `ipl`,consistent with the Chronicles transition. Artaxerxes's reply uses singular-we for the court that received the letter and plural-I for the crown before whom it was read in4:18. The royal command in4:19/21 retains `ipl`. These are foreign royal voices,not divine speech. In4:14 ordinary `wepl` identifies multiple people materially supported by the palace;`wesg` then identifies their coordinated official petition. The shift has a discourse reason and is not ornamental variation. No first-person clause is assigned a theological person-count.
