# The remembered voice · 0.10-kjv.26

This is a working continuation of the deliberately alien historical Bible register. It adds Deuteronomy 1–6 and repairs nineteen earlier lexical-sense selections in a new edition. It does not modernize the archaic language, change frozen Jonah, or claim the entire Bible is finished. Older editions remain intact. Source claims and commands belong to the quoted speakers; the dictionary does not endorse them.

## One whole across generations

`wesg` and `ourone` construe Israel as one covenant-bearing, travelling whole. A father can tell a later child “we were Pharaoh's slaves” without asserting that every later member was individually present. Deuteronomy 5:3's emphatic “not our fathers, but us” remains beside this continuing identity. The grammatical whole does not erase the source's temporal tension. Plural predicate nouns remain possible: a whole can have been composed of many slaves. Numerals remain independent of pronoun number.

## The envoy's I and the hearers' we

Moses normally uses individual `i` for recollection, mediation, prayer and denied entry. In the embassy's collective journey petition (2:27–29), `ipl`/`mypl` voices the many people as a speaking I. It changes to `wesg` in the shared land-grant clause. This is not a divine-only marker. In 5:24 and 5:26, `wepl` foregrounds separate witnesses who heard and saw; singular-we resumes for the collective promise to hear and do. Context, not random variation, licenses these shifts.

## Plural-I and one LORD

Divine direct speech uses plural-I, but 6:4 still says one LORD. This is the author's requested internally plural self, not an inserted count of three, a diagnostic label, or an automatic proof of a later doctrine. “None besides him,” “face to face,” and “no likeness, only voice” remain explicit. The historical language bears these awkward source predicates without supplying a philosophical repair.

## Hear, speak, hear again

Nested discourse can pass from narrator to Moses to people to deity within one verse. Keep the recipient of each speech verb explicit, preserve individual Moses-I outside the quote and divine plural-I inside it, and keep the people as quoted participants. The fire witnesses say they survived and then fear that further hearing will kill them. Do not harmonize the second assertion away. Face-to-face speech and a mediating Moses also coexist.

## Remembered commands need not be identical

The retrospective Decalogue keeps its own wording. “Keep the sabbath” and the Egyptian servitude/deliverance rationale are not replaced with the Exodus wording or creation rationale. Ox, ass, livestock, servants and stranger all remain within the rest rule; servants resting as the owner does is an explicit purpose. Wife-desire and property-coveting remain distinct verbs and lists. The source's listing of people as property is retained without editorial approval.

## Wish without an exclamation glyph

The thousandfold blessing in 1:11 and the longing for an obedient heart in 5:29 use optative plus `:excl` expressive morphology and an ordinary sentence boundary. The typeface's lack of an exclamation mark is respected. A divine wish is still a wish in the source; it is not silently recast as an accomplished decree.

## Names, territory and quantities

Keep eleven journey days, the fortieth year/eleventh month/first day, and the thirty-eight-year interval distinct. Sixty cities means sixty. Og's nine-by-four human-cubit measurements belong to a bedstead, not an invented bodily height. Half Gilead, half Manasseh and half a valley have different referents. The three refuge cities retain individual names, locations and affiliations. Sion in 4:48 is explicitly Hermon; Laban in 1:1 is a place, not the family figure. Emims, Horims, Zamzummims and the named successors remain attributed historical labels, not modern ethnic identifications.

## Correcting senses in an unfinished edition

The inherited courtesy `please` was incorrectly selected for delight/satisfaction in Genesis 16:6, 20:15, 28:8, 33:10, 34:18, 38:10, 45:16; Exodus 21:8; Numbers 23:27, 24:1. `kjv_please_satisfy` now gives the verbal delight sense. Nine Numbers references used unadorned `plain` for flat terrain: 22:1, 26:3, 26:63, 31:12, 33:48–50, 35:1, 36:13. These use `kjv_plain_land`. Only these nineteen inherited targets change. Neither repair changes the historical morphology, and earlier snapshots retain their old text. The completed whole Bible, not each unfinished checkpoint, is the final freeze boundary. Jonah's already frozen edition remains exempt.

## Restraint, exclusion and return

The protected lands of Esau, Moab and Ammon are not absorbed into a universal conquest command. Sihon and Og's destruction, including women and children, stays explicit. Moses is forbidden entry even as the people receive it. Land-loss threats and conditional return promises both remain. Historical categories such as heathen and the fire/jealousy predicates are marked as source discourse. No modern moral justification, biological explanation, or devotional smoothing is added.
