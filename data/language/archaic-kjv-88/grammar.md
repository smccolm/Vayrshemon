## 1. A ruler can speak personally or through an apparatus

The visiting queen uses personal-I9:5–6for what she heard, disbelieved and saw. Rank does not automatically force plural-self. Rehoboam's scheduling me10:5/12and his inquiry to advisers10:6/9also remain personal. In10:11and10:14the threatened escalation of the people's yoke is `ipl`: the ruler speaks as the coercive apparatus that can inflict it. The first occurrence is a proposed script; the second is the delivered threat. Matching forms preserve the connection. This political plural-I is not divine identity, a diagnosis of the speaker, or a count of human bodies.

The divine reply11:4and12:5/7uses the established plural-self. `mypl/service`12:8contrasts service to that self with service to kingdoms. The translation leaves that disturbing comparison between forms of subjection exposed, rather than replacing it with a comfortable freedom-versus-slavery explanation.

## 2. Singular-we can reject as well as unite

The petitioners10:4speak as one burdened political community: `ourone/yoke`, `wesg:a` and `wesg`. The king-and-council decision body uses `wesg`10:9for the proposed answer, then quotes a different singular-we, the petitioning people. This nested shift does not make council and people the same speaker. Explicit quotation context carries the change.

In10:16the seceding community says `wesg` has no share or inheritance in David's house, then disperses as each man to his tents. Singular-whole identity therefore supports collective refusal and division, not only harmonious worship. Narrator they and plural recipients remain plural. No actual dual is inferred merely because Judah and Benjamin, or two political factions, are named together.

## 3. An intolerable boast remains bodily

The little-finger/loins comparison10:10keeps its stated body parts and the inherited thicker sense. It is not rewritten into stronger administration. Citations of Nathan, Ahijah, Shemaiah and Iddo remain cited source records; no lost text is fabricated or claimed as consulted. Until-this-day is the source narrator's present, not2026.
