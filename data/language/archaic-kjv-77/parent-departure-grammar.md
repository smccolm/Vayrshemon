# KJV historical continuation76 — compressed origins and competing name forms

## No invented collective speaker
The fifty-four verses of1Chronicles1 contain no first-person speech. Bare genealogical lists are not utterances by a plural-I ancestor or a singular-we nation. Preserve personal-I, plural-I self and singular-we whole as available grammatical perspectives without inserting one where there is no source speaker. The language's collective ontology is expressed when the discourse warrants it, not by globally replacing plural nouns with a pronoun.

## Compressed lineage, continuation and explicit identity
Opening Adam-to-Noah lists and the later Shem-to-Abram list remain terse, unpredicated name sequences. Do not add a father/son edge between every adjacent name: Noah, Shem, Ham and Japheth would make that particularly wrong. Sons of X introduces an actual source relationship; continuing verse lists inherit the pending beget construction without adding missing actions.1:27explicitly equates Abram and Abraham, so it receives an identity predicate. Royal succession is in the predecessor's place, not an inferred father-to-son descent.

## Preserve supplied variant forms
Enosh, Kenan, Jered, Henoch, Ashchenaz, Sabta, Sabtecha, Caphthorim, Girgashite, Nebaioth, Abida, Zephi, Ezar, Homam, Alian, Shephi, Zavan, Jakan, Pai and Aliah are retained. Ezar in1:38and Ezer in1:42remain visibly different supplied forms. The final Hadad/Pai is not normalized to the earlier Genesis Hadar/Pau. Variant-form entries are authored encodings with source relationships, not invented etymologies or claims of modern linguistic reconstruction.

## Distinguish namesakes and kinds of referent
Put is a name rather than the placement verb. Tarshish and Havilah use the inherited genealogical-person senses rather than geographical entries. Shelah son of Arphaxad is distinct from Judah's son. Amram of Dishon's line is distinct from the Exodus genealogy. Aiah son of Zibeon is distinct from Rizpah's father. Hadad in Ishmael's list, Hadad son of Bedad and Hadad associated with Pai are separately identified. Jobab the Edomite king differs from Joktan's son and the king of Madon. The dictionary may reuse an established form across distinct semantic entries; identical spelling does not establish identical people.

## Birth, descendants and counted selection
Beget carries the stated paternal engendering; active born means give birth, with passive born for being born. Keturah's status remains concubine rather than an assimilated modern relationship. The source's sons-of-Keturah statement follows a grandson list; preserve the broader genealogical use without adding a corrective generation. Two sons and the name of one in1:19use a cardinal pair and one_num selection, not generic-human one. Firstborn is a compound relation and does not add an independent counted person.

## Keep unresolved list and chronology tensions
Timna is retained within the sons-list in1:36and as Lotan's sister in1:39without silently replacing a relation from another passage. The Casluhim parenthesis stays attached as printed. Meshech at the end of Shem's list is not corrected to Mash, and no Aram subgroup is added. Earth divided remains the source naming explanation for Peleg, without geological invention. Edomite kings precede Israelite kings in the source's relative chronology; no absolute calendar is supplied. Duke uses the inherited historical chief/head sense, without European peerage. Repeated titles and abrupt lists remain intentionally stiff.
