# KJV historical continuation79 — sanctuary ancestry, service and allotted towns

## A genealogy does not create a first-person speaker
This chapter contains no source I or we speech. The singers, priests, families and tribal groups remain nominal collectives. Plural-I and singular-we remain available for actual discourse perspectives, but are not inserted into an ancestral list simply because many persons form a whole. No pronoun paradigm changes in this continuation.

## Repeated names, repeated lists and supplied differences
The opening Gershon and subsequent Gershom forms are retained; Gershom son of Levi is distinguished from Moses son. The priestly chain contains repeated Amariah, Ahitub, Zadok and Azariah names at different positions; those positions are not deduplicated. The resumed Aaron-to-Ahimaaz chain reuses the same earlier entries rather than inventing new bearers for a repeated passage. Amminadab in6:22 remains beside Izhar in6:38. Vashni remains the printed firstborn of Samuel, while the later singer ancestry retains Joel and the supplied Shemuel spelling. Eliel/Toah and earlier Elihu/Tohu forms are not silently harmonized. The two Assir positions and recurring Elkanah positions remain visible; no unprinted missing generations or biographies are supplied.

## Women and broad descendant headings
Miriam appears under Amrams children, while Aarons list is explicitly sons. Neither category is flattened into male offspring throughout. Sons can introduce a compressed ancestry chain, a family aggregate or descendants across generations according to the source construction. Local father relations distinguish Meraris Mahli from the Mahli expressly called son of Mushi in the later singer ancestry. These are semantic identity records, not evidence that every shared spelling has a new phonological root.

## Worship labour and appointed order
Waited on their office means attended to assigned duty, not delayed until something happened. The common term kjv_service_order, service-order, denotes an assigned arrangement of duties; it does not invent a detailed calendar, shift rota or rank system. Singing before the congregation-tabernacle, continued service after Solomons temple, broad Levite service and Aarons priestly offering duties remain distinct. The two altars, most-holy-place work and inherited ritual atonement category are preserved. Burnt offering is a burnt form of the established deity-offering noun, not an ordinary gift or an added animal species.

## Spatial hands and sacred rest
Asaph at the right hand and Merarite singers at the left hand denote placement beside the other singers. The right-side lexical sense is used, not the generic entitlement noun or right-conduct sense. Hand in this positional expression does not become a literal platform on which people stand. The arks rest is retained as the source condition preceding Davids appointment, without adding an unstated relocation date or collapsing it into a generic remainder.

## Allocated settlements and outlying land
Lot is the inherited allocation-token concept. Coasts are territories, castles are represented by the inherited fortified-enclosure term without medieval architectural detail, and suburbs are the outlying fields belonging to the cities. Hebron city, its associated lands assigned to Caleb and the priestly grant are distinguished as the text states. Aner is a city rather than Abrahams ally, Debir a city rather than the king or inner shrine, and Ashtaroth a place rather than the goddess. Bileam, Hilen, Alemeth, Jokmeam, Anem, Mashal, Hukok and Jahzah retain supplied forms. Ramoth in Issachar differs from the explicitly Gilead-qualified Ramoth. A location is not moved to another tribe merely to agree with a similar spelling elsewhere.

## Totals, restatements and incomplete enumerations
The Aaronite total of thirteen cities remains thirteen even though the immediate enumeration does not supply thirteen names; no names from another witness are inserted. The remaining Kohath allocation is ten cities. Its half-tribe, namely half-Manasseh restatement repeats one referent rather than giving two independent grants. Gershoms thirteen and Meraris twelve retain their aggregate scopes. The half-tribe references later in the list are group fractions, not half-people. The other side Jordan phrase remains alongside its explicit east-side description.
