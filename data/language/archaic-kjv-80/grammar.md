# KJV historical continuation80 — households, displaced kin and counted strength

## No first-person identity invented in a list
These eighty verses contain no source first-person utterance. Descendant groups remain nominal groups. Neither plural-I nor singular-we is inserted merely because a household includes many people. The inherited contrast remains available for actual speakers and collective self-presentation; all pronoun forms and possessive paradigms are unchanged.

## Sons headings and chains have different structures
A sons heading can introduce a list, a lineage chain, descendants across generations or a group including an expressly named sister. Retain the local construction rather than turn every adjacent name into a father-son edge. Repeated Tahath positions in7:20 remain distinct, and the mixed Rephah/Resheph/Telah/Tahan construction remains mixed. Repeated Gera in8:3/5 stays repeated without an invented death, replacement child or copying explanation. A repeated form is not automatically a new native root: the dictionary distinguishes referents when local relationships require it, and reports entry growth separately from surface-form growth.

## Household women retain agency and uncertain references
The unnamed she in7:14 remains unnamed. The difficult Machir/sister/whose-sister/second wording in7:15 retains its compression; no missing brother or additional generation is supplied. Maachah and Hammoleketh bear their named children. Sherah explicitly builds lower and upper Bethhoron and Uzzensherah: a female builder is not paraphrased into an unnamed male household achievement. Shua is a sister. Shaharaim's sending-away clause stays distinct from a modern legal divorce claim, and Hushim and Hodesh retain their separate maternal lists.

## Loss, naming and unexpanded raid perspective
The Gath killing episode retains its compressed they/their references and locally born perpetrators. It does not add a resolved raiding chronology. Ephraim's mourning and his brothers' comfort remain a narrative interruption within ancestry. Beriah's name remains borrowed while the attached evil-with-the-house explanation is translated separately. Misfortune affecting the house is not rewritten as the newborn's moral guilt, and the native name is not given a fabricated etymology.

## Distinct namesakes and supplied forms
Puah is Issachar's son, not the midwife. Anathoth, Tharshish, Bezer, Arad, Elam and Alemeth are personal names in their local lists rather than automatically towns or territories. Ahaz is a descendant, not the king. Athaliah belongs to Jeroham's household rather than being replaced by the queen. Non/Jehoshuah, Shamer/Shomer, Helem/Hotham, Imna/Imnah, Eliah, Malchishua, Eshbaal, Meribbaal and Micah retain the supplied forms. The father of Gibeon in8:29 remains unnamed; a name from the next chapter is not inserted. The supplied Ner-to-Kish chain is retained without harmonizing another genealogy.

## Place, displacement and selected warriors
Possessions and dwellings retain their relation to named towns and dependent settlements. Eastward Naaran, westward Gezer and the printed Gaza boundary are not geographically corrected from another source. Over against denotes opposite placement, not hostility. Removal to Manahath and driving out Gath's inhabitants remain explicit displacement. Choice warriors are selected persons, not the abstract act of choosing; archers are bow-shooters. The inherited noun for possession denotes holding, not a new material species of object.

## Preservation and evidence limits
All eighty verses have authored analyses, back-readings and construction notes. Numeric, lexical, source-order and native layout checks accompany translator self-review; no independent literary certification is claimed. The entire Bible remains incomplete; these are internal checkpoints within the single complete-Bible deliverable.
