# KJV historical continuation78 — descent records, petitions and inherited claims

## Personal petition and narrated identity
The mother's explanation at1Chronicles4:9 and Jabez's request at4:10 use personal I/me/my. The narrator's interrupting I say at5:3 also uses personal I. None speaks as a composite deity or corporate polity in these clauses; plural-I and singular-we are therefore not mechanically inserted. The historical register retains plural-I for a speaker presenting multiple selves as one speaking subject and singular-we for a whole presenting itself as a unit. A genealogical family is a nominal collective, not an unspoken first-person claim. This continuation introduces no new first-person endings or possessive aliases.

## Wishes, exclamatory scope and fulfilled requests
Jabez's Oh that request uses the inherited optative on bless, widen, be-with and keep. This is a desired divine action rather than the speaker granting permission. The final negative sorrow-causation clause bears the established exclamatory form; Thimfora receives no unsupported exclamation mark. The negative belongs to evil causing sorrow, not to blessing or the request itself. The narrative separately says the request was granted. At5:20 the deity being intreated means responding favourably to the appeal, rendered as granting the request; it is not reduced to someone merely addressing the deity. Neither passage is rewritten into a universal prosperity promise.

## Descent record, birthright and political precedence
The new common entry kjv_genealogy is father-record: a recorded reckoning of descent or family ancestry. It is distinct from the broader inherited generations heading and from a drawn line. In5:1–2 Reuben is firstborn, Joseph's sons receive birthright, Judah prevails and supplies the chief ruler, and genealogy is not reckoned by birthright. These are separate relations, not synonyms to be consolidated into one favoured son's status. The source's parenthetical explanation and the narrator's I-say resumption remain intelligible without inventing a new family-law system.

## Names, repeated forms and unresolved ancestry
New semantic entries distinguish the local bearer of a name; entry counts are not counts of newly invented sounds. Existing Elishama-son-of-David and Zerah-in-Simeon entries are reused. Daniel, Shimea, Bathshua, Ammiel, Abia, Jeconiah, Jecamiah, Reaia, Nephish, Senir and Tilgathpilneser retain the supplied forms rather than being normalized to another account. Assir remains the printed name rather than being replaced by a captive interpretation. Zerubbabel is expressly in Pedaiah's sons list here. The compressed Zedekiah his-son sequence is not rewritten into an uncle relation. The repeated Elishama and Eliphelet in David's list remain repeated without inventing deaths or replacement children. The source's five named sons followed by six in3:22 is retained, not repaired.

## Broad sons, women and craft households
Sons can head a broad lineage or a list later including an expressly identified sister. Shelomith and Tamar retain their stated sister relationships. The sudden she bare in4:17 and the difficult wife/mother clauses in4:18–19 remain compressed rather than receiving invented antecedents. Father-of-place/group stays relational; it does not assert literal stone procreation or a historically dated founding. Ashur's two wives, the maternal birth groups, fine-linen workers, pot-makers and the Charashim craftsman explanation retain their household and occupational distinctions. The inherited agent construction on pot yields pot-maker in the explicit occupational context, without a new inflectional rule.

## Terrain, occupation and source violence
Fat pasture is productive grazing, using fruitful with the pasture noun; it is not animal fat. Coast in Jabez's request is the inherited territorial sense. Rooms and steads in settlement clauses are places occupied, not indoor bedrooms. Suburbs are outlying fields, not modern commuter districts. Ham ancestry is retained as the source's claim without a modern racial equation. The killings, destruction of dwellings, occupation and captured human inventory remain explicit. The war-of-God and cultic-whoring language are attributed source concepts, not translator endorsements or invented additional sexual events.

The chapter3 ranks remain ordinals, while six Hebron-born sons, seven years and six months, thirty-three Jerusalem years, four sons, nine names and the later five/six/three/seven counts remain separate quantities. Chapter4 retains two wives, sixteen sons and six daughters, five cities and five hundred men. Chapter5's half-tribe fractions are not half-people. Until David's reign, the separate Jotham/Jeroboam dating clauses, and unto-this-day perspectives are not silently converted to a modern chronology.
