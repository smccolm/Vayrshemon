## Personal-I, singular-we and representative identity

Personal `i` carries thanksgiving, the appeal for Onesimus, intended retention, consent, repayment, joy, writing and hoped-for release. `wesg` in verse 7 preserves the supplied **we have** great joy; it does not silently become another reading's I had. `ourone` marks the common brother, beloved, fellow-soldier, Father and Lord relations. The pronoun choice varies with the discourse role, not a mechanical replacement of every first-person form.

Receiving Onesimus **as myself**, and calling him **mine own bowels**, are strong representative/affective identification. They do not on their own require physical fusion, a plural speaking agent or an unmarked plural-I; the appeal depends on the writer, addressee and entrusted person remaining distinguishable. No physical dual is imposed on these relations. Verse 22 moves from singular lodging request to the group's plural prayers and plural recipients. Verse 25 preserves plural your with singular spirit rather than flattening that contrast.

## Embodied and shared-work lexicon

`kjv_bowels_affection_philemon` shares the inherited bodily-interior form and explicitly licenses the affectionate inward-parts metaphor in verses 7,12,20. The translation keeps its alien bodily awkwardness; it neither reduces the image to a bland modern feeling nor claims detached intestines or digestive illness. `kjv_partner_philemon` is share-person, participation/fellowship rather than spouse or a specified business contract. Fellowlabourer, fellowsoldier and fellowprisoner are transparent share-plus-role compounds; their precise tasks, army membership, cell arrangement or charges are not invented.

Philemon, Apphia and the supplied Lucas spelling receive contextual entries. Timothy, Archippus, Onesimus, Epaphras, Marcus, Aristarchus and Demas reuse the ordered inherited names. Apphia is **beloved**, not an inserted sister or wife. Lucas remains beside Luke; Marcus is not rewritten as John Mark. Demas's criticism in another letter does not contaminate this greeting. The useful/unuseful contrast is kept without fabricating a native etymology for the adapted Onesimus name.

## Consent, debt and the status of Onesimus

Convenient in verse 8 means fitting. Mind in verse 14 means consent. Count in verse 17 is reckon/regard, while account in verse 18 is responsibility for a conditional debt. The text does not supply a stolen sum, confirmed offence, exact route or date. The writer's handwritten repayment promise is future, not an already settled account. The rhetorical refusal to mention the addressee's own self-debt remains visible rather than being sanitized into a neutral transaction.

Perhaps in verse 15 is retained. Temporary departure and forever receiving preserve the source contrast without inventing a definite escape story or providential certainty. **Above a servant, a brother beloved** remains a relational appeal. Neither this phrase nor the anticipated **more than I say** is filled out with an unprinted legal emancipation declaration. Nor is the historical unequal household relation modernized into freely negotiated employment.

## Source wording and tense

Verse 6 preserves **in you**, plural, and faith-sharing rather than telecommunications. Verse 9 preserves **aged**, not an alternative ambassador reading. Verse 12 retains **thou therefore receive him** and sent-again aspect. Verse 20 keeps **in the Lord** twice. Verse 22 is confidence about being given back, not a completed release. The supplied final Lord Jesus Christ and your spirit remain intact.
