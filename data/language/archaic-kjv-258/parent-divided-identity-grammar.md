# KJV 0.10-kjv.6 — contested selves, household descent and divided identity

This continuation inherits the pinned 0.10-kjv.5 historical package. Genesis 27–32 contributes 233 new authored verses. Genesis 1–32 and unchanged frozen Jonah total 1,009 of 31,102 verses: the requested whole Bible remains incomplete. Earlier files and modern 0.10 remain untouched. Historical additions are not claims of attested ancient forms.

## 1. A human I can encompass a plurality

Genesis 32:10 deliberately contrasts three clauses. The unworthy speaker and the person who crossed Jordan with his staff use ordinary singular I. Now I am become two bands uses `ipl`: one Jacob-identity now includes the divided household he has just organized. This source-grounded human application prevents plural-I from becoming merely a substitute spelling for God. It represents a scoped perspective, not a clinical condition, several independent speakers or a claim that Jacob is divine.

In Genesis 27:19 Jacob's I am Esau stays ordinary I: he is one human making a false identity assertion. Esau's later true I am thy firstborn uses the same ordinary first person. Grammar distinguishes speaker perspective; it does not automatically certify truth. The user's identity system should make meaningful tensions legible, not turn every deceptive or emotionally strained utterance into plural-I.

## 2. Dream voices and quoted promises keep their boundaries

Genesis 28:13–15 and 31:3 use plural-I for the divine self-identification and presence promise. Jacob's adjacent I knew it not in 28:16 remains ordinary singular. His conditional vow stays conditional and includes bread, clothing, return and a tenth; it is not smoothed into a modern doctrinal profession.

In 31:11 Jacob reports a messenger speaking within a dream. His Here am I is ordinary I; the messenger's I have seen and I am the God of Bethel in 31:12–13 carry the commissioned plural-I. The narrator's messenger frame, the direct God self-identification and the vow-to-me relation remain simultaneously visible. They are not flattened into a named Father/Son assignment. Genesis 32:9 and 32:12 quote divine promises inside Jacob's human prayer, so the deictic center changes only within those quotations.

## 3. Unknown identity remains unknown where the source withholds it

The wrestler in Genesis 32 is introduced as a man. Let me go and my name are locally personal singular, unlike the explicit divine identification at Bethel. Jacob later says he saw God face to face; that is retained as his claim without retroactively inserting a revealed name into the earlier refusal. Israel is introduced at the naming event, and later source uses of Jacob remain Jacob. Peniel and Penuel retain the supplied spelling difference.

Fear of Isaac receives an explicit revered-object/designation sense sharing the fear root. The oath is not reduced to the emotion of a frightened Isaac. Abraham's God, Nahor's God and their father's God in 31:53 remain a series of ancestral designations, not an invented Trinity list or a silently settled identity equation. Laban's stolen gods are ordinary plural deities or images; they are not the internally plural speaker represented by `ipl`.

## 4. Corporate we must not erase separate parties

The well workers' we-one in 29:8 presents a shared watering procedure as one operating arrangement, while their origin and knowledge reports use plural-we. Laban's our-one country and we-one will give in 29:26–27 express a norm-bearing household/community claim. Rachel and Leah's common father-house can use our-one while their own losses and inheritance claims remain plural-we in 31:14–16.

The covenant of Laban and Jacob explicitly says I and thou. Its us remains plural, as do absent one from another and the parties awaiting judgment. Cooperation alone does not make singular-we appropriate. The several human messengers of Genesis 32 keep ordinary plural-we in their location reports, even when carrying a script that contains Jacob's singular I.

## 5. Blessing, accusation and bargaining have speakers

Isaac's blessing wishes use the inherited optative; they are not new divine first-person promises. His confirmation of the blessing after discovering deception remains. Rebekah's proposed assumption of a curse is her speech, not narrator certification of transferable liability. Jacob's attribution of the disguised food to God remains inside his deception.

Laban's claims of ownership, his promised musical farewell, the women's accusation of sale, Leah's hired-thee transaction and the mothers' interpretations of their children are kept as their claims. The narrator does not silently endorse or correct them. Threatened killing, dependent-person transfers and household dominance stay explicit without added graphic details or invented consent. The source's philosophical awkwardness is preserved through these relations, not through careless lexical substitution.

## 6. Named births and mother-relative counts

Leah's three sons in 29:34, fifth in 30:17 and sixth in 30:19–20 refer to her own births. Bilhah and Zilpah each have a second son in their own sequences. Dinah remains a named daughter. Ceased bearing is not made permanent. The seven years, completed wedding week and additional seven years remain different time spans; Rachel is given after the week, not withheld for the second full service term.

The twenty service years in 31:41 are fourteen plus six; two daughters and ten wage changes are separate counts. Genesis 32 retains four hundred men, the sex/species-separated livestock gift, two wives, two handmaids and eleven sons. Thirty milk-camels with their young does not invent an independently stated thirty offspring. The tenth in 28:22 is one of ten equal parts, not a twelfth because Unta uses base twelve.

## 7. Keep lexical categories as fine as the source requires

Savoury food, hunted game, goat-kids and bread stay distinct. Quiver is an arrow container; plain/tender eyes and smooth skin are not moral judgments or diagnoses. Tender-eyed remains deliberately underspecified. Mandrakes retain their source plant name without a promise of actual fertility. Speckled, spotted, ring-banded and grisled remain separate livestock markings. Poplar, hazel and chestnut retain KJV names rather than unrequested botanical substitutions.

Stole away is secret departure, while stole images is actual theft. Camel furniture is riding equipment. Dry heat by day and frost at night are bodily hardships. Halted upon the thigh means limped, not stopped. Hollow, joint and shrunk sinew preserve the source's limited anatomy without an invented modern diagnosis. Pile as textile nap is not used for a heap of stones, and count as a noble title is not used for counting descendants.

## 8. Native exclamation, opacity and textual fidelity

Esau's bless-me cries in 27:34/38, Rachel's urgent request in 30:1 and Jacob's dreadful-place exclamation in 28:17 use the native exclamative ending, not an unsupported Thimfora exclamation mark. Source English retains its own punctuation. Not every intense sentence receives an exclamation; discourse and the source wording determine placement.

Two different names for the witness heap, repeated face/hand/body expressions, uncertain pronoun antecedents and apparently conflicting claims remain visible with notes. Awkwardness is deliberate, but sources, quantities, negation scope and speaker changes still require checks. The four columns use the approved fonts, Maxima, sizes, leading, paint and paragraph alignment. Long relations may use analytical of rather than smaller type. Source-bound self-review is documented; no independent literary certification is claimed.
