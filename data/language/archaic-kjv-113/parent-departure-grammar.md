# KJV historical register 0.10-kjv.112 — Psalms 49–60

This continues sealed 0.10-kjv.111, adding 171 authored verses and bringing Psalms to 880 verses through Psalm60. Earlier targets and seals remain unchanged. This is still an incomplete Bible; every historical KJV edition is permanently excluded from modernization and consolidation.

## Divine plural-I with explicit changes of speaker

Psalm50 has an extended divine `ipl` voice, including objects and possessions rather than merely initial subjects. Gather-to-me, covenant-with-me, offerings-before-me, creatures-belonging-to-me and glorify-me all retain the same internally multiple self. The declared my-people, my-statutes, my-covenant and my-words use `mypl`. The narrative third-person interjection in50:6 remains distinct, as does God-said in50:16. The hypothetical hungry-I in50:12 remains under if/would, and the flesh-and-blood consumption question in50:13 remains a question. No meal is asserted to have happened. The deity's rejection of the hearer's thou-thoughtest-I-was-like-thee in50:21 is not turned into a new doctrine about the speaker's number of persons. Psalm60:6–8 similarly uses plural-I after the explicit God-hath-spoken boundary, with `mypl` for head, lawgiver, washpot and shoe. Ordinary-I resumes with the human leader's who-will-bring-me questions in60:9; the translation does not make God ask who will lead God into Edom.

## Singular-we, physical plural-we and the intimate dual

Psalm50:3 opens with `ourone/god` before divine speech begins. Psalm59:11's our-shield expresses common allegiance, even while the praying speaker refers to my people. Psalm60:1 distinguishes a community cast off (`wesg`) from scattered physical members (`wepl`) in the same verse. The common appeal returns to `wesg`; collective abandonment in60:10 and the shared request for help in60:11 likewise remain singular-we. Bodily drinking in60:3 and fighting in60:12 use plural-we, while the enemies opposed to the common people in60:12 use `ourone`. Armies remain plural in60:10. Psalm55:14 deliberately uses the inherited dual `we:du` for the immediately established I-and-thou pair who took counsel and walked to God's house. Intimacy alone does not dissolve two walking bodies into singular-we. These choices follow the Origins pronoun distinctions by discourse role; they are not random variation or clinical labels.

## Individual confession and unresolved religious relationships

Psalm51 keeps a personal singular-I penitent. Against-thee-only, shaped-in-iniquity, mother-conceived-me-in-sin and bones-thou-hast-broken remain the speaker's difficult claims. They are neither endorsed as independently verified facts nor repaired with a doctrine of inherited guilt or an added victim narrative. Thy-holy-spirit in51:11 retains possession without inventing another independently speaking person or specifying a cardinal number of divine persons. Thy-free-spirit in51:12 is rendered as willing spirit: the word concerns willing or generous support, not zero price. The possessive remains thy, not a silent substitution of my. The rejection of sacrifice in51:16 stands beside acceptance of righteous, burnt and whole-burnt sacrifice in51:19. The tension is preserved rather than harmonized. Psalm55's covenant-breaker remains unnamed; no later gospel figure is inserted into the friend's betrayal.

## Source repetitions and differences between parallel passages

Natural variation must not delete repetition that belongs to the source. The doubled mercy plea in57:1, doubled fixed-heart in57:7, repeated glory refrain in57:5/11 and doubled destruction in59:13 are intentional. Psalm56:4 says flesh whereas56:11 says man;56:10 keeps both God and LORD. Psalm53 is compared withPsalm14 without being copied wholesale: God replaces LORD in the inquiry, iniquity differs from works, gone-back differs from turned-aside, and53:5 has fear where no fear was plus scattered besieger bones. The unmarked my-people in53:4 stays an ordinary possessive, as in14:4, instead of an invented divine speaker tag. Psalm59:9 has his strength,59:17my strength. Psalm60:8 preserves Philistia's commanded triumph-because-of-me rather than substituting an I-will-triumph-over-Philistia reading from another passage. The source English is retained exactly in the fourth column.

## Conditional, optative and reported constructions

If and would jointly keep the divine hunger in50:12 hypothetical. Else-I-would-give in51:16 marks the counterfactual sacrifice response. Psalm55's wished-for wings, wilderness retreat and flight are not narrated events. The retrospective unreal alternatives in55:12 use perfect infinitives with can/would; past-perfect alone would not express those alternatives. Psalm58's imprecations remain optative, including the snail and untimely-birth comparisons. The frightening quick-descent in55:15 means alive, not rapidly. Psalm59:6 reports the dog-like circuit in the present, whereas59:14 reprises it as a wish. Questions, negations and source changes of addressee are retained, including59:13's tension between enemies no-longer-being and knowing divine rule. Vocatives and expressive wishes use the existing `:excl` construction rather than an unsupported Thimfora exclamation glyph.

## Lexical extensions and contextual senses

Four common entries are added: brutish (beast-fool), bloodguilt (blood-guilt), belch (mouth-pour), and washpot (wash-pot). Brutish records the source's animal-like lack of understanding without a modern diagnosis. Bloodguilt concerns liability for bloodshed, not a medical blood condition. Belch retains the coarse bodily image of hostile speech; it does not invent vomiting. Washpot retains the lowly vessel comparison for Moab without adding a ritual process. Philistia is an authored proper-name adaptation for the supplied territorial address, with no modern geographic equation. Existing roots carry most other senses: conversation is behaviour, frame-deceit is shape, tell-wanderings is count, mark-steps is observe, prevent-me is go-before-me, grudge-for-food is grumble, consume-enemies is destroy, clear-in-judging is innocent, and bodily soundness is not audible sound. The Selah psalm marker is kept separate from the identically written Edomite place. Lying in52:3and59:12 is false speech, while57:4and59:3use reclining/ambush posture. The shield in59:1 is a protective verb;59:11uses the separate armour noun. No inflectional endings, Maxima pairs or compiler rules are changed.

## Quantities, source discomfort and analytical possessives

Psalm50:10's thousand counts hills, not cattle or divine persons. Psalm53:3's not-one is a negative universal; generic one in49:16,50:21,53:3and58:8does not become a headcount. Half in55:23 is half of their days, without an invented number of years. Counting wanderings in56:8 and measuring territory in60:6 provide no numerical total. The dual of55:14 follows the explicit pair, not a guessed army size. The archaic register keeps the melting snail, pot-feeling-thorns, wine-of-astonishment, tears-in-bottle, bones and vengeance-blood images without comforting modern explanations. In57:1, refuge-for-myself expresses the possessive analytically, avoiding an overlong inflected word without shrinking the approved font. This is a grammatical alternative within the historical register, not a change to the page standard.
