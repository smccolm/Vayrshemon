## Plural-self in the meal,prayer,abandonment and presence

The unnamed woman's act is upon me-pl;the bread is my-pl body and the new-testament blood is my-pl blood. These supplied identifications remain without a modernizing sacramental explanation. The covenant sense of testament is distinguished from the inherited ordinary will/testament word and from a later bound collection of writings.

In the prayer,the plural-self addresses my-pl Father and opposes not-as-I-pl-will to as-thou-wilt. The second prayer retains its changed conditional exception and thy-will-be-done;the third explicitly repeats the words. Plural-I does not erase these different grammatical subjects or relieve the philosophical discomfort. The high priest personally adjures Jesus but asks him to tell us-one,shifting to the council's shared self. The council's witnesses question and the mockers' prophesy-to-us-one also use singular-we. The people's blood-on-us-one/our-one-children declaration remains attributed to this narrative scene. It is not an assertion of hereditary guilt or a translator claim about modern Jews. The angel uses personal-I in28:5,7;Jesus's plural-self returns in28:10. A supernatural messenger is not automatically divine plural-I. This distinction is part of discourse grammar,not a blanket replacement rule.
