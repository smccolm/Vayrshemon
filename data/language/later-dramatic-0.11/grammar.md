# Later dramatic Vayrshemon 0.11

## 1. What the chronology means

The frozen Bible is the historical literary witness. The two plays use a later dramatic standard. Project creation dates are not fictional language dates: 0.10 was assembled before the Bible, and 0.11 reconciles that work with the subsequently developed historical witness. This is authored language history, not a claim of archaeological attestation. The awkward Biblical translation is a particular foreign-text register, not a specimen of every ancient speaker's ordinary conversation.

The Bible is never rewritten by this work. Historical forms can survive in ritual, quotation and deliberate archaism. New translations start from 0.11 and record their own versioned additions. They must not silently start from either an earlier play compiler or the frozen Biblical dictionary.

## 2. Inherited grammar and later developments

The ordinary tense, aspect, voice, case and mood inventory remains conservative. There is no invented wholesale sound shift and no random replacement of suffixes. Later change is visible in wordhood, reference and register: familiar compounds can become single inflecting words; case marking becomes less obligatory for an established, recoverable topic; dialogue permits carefully licensed omission rather than repeating a full formal introduction at every turn.

The inherited thing-class prefix remains productive in explicit classification, unfamiliar referents and marked ritual diction. It is not mechanically attached to every noun in a later conversation. Preserve the actual numeral values, negation, modal strength and participant roles. A source page break cannot reset reference.

The specific lexicalizations are listed in lexical-decisions.json. For example, historical **hefak gur** becomes ordinary **hefakgur** (war), and **pom sōlum** becomes **pomsōlum** (scholar). Old spaced phrases remain available as deliberate formal constructions. These are bounded conventional words, not a rule stripping spaces from arbitrary text. Long descriptive names can remain phrases. Outer inflection belongs to the final head of such a phrase; ordinary free phrases should instead be authored with explicit constituent analyses when internal roles matter.

## 3. Four first-person perspectives

**I(sg.)** is one speaking self; **I(pl.)** is internal plurality within a speaking identity; **we(sg.)** is a group or office construed as one acting whole; **we(pl.)** is several individuated participants. Later speech inherits all four possibilities. The plural-I construction remains marked and available; it is not compulsory for gods, magical characters, emotional conflict or actors playing a part.

The King can invoke his office with singular-we, then return to personal I. This alternation conveys a change of stance. It does not authorize changing an English personal *me* into royal *us*. The 0.11 review restores four such personal references in All's Well. Bertram's formal response on accepting military command uses office-oriented singular-we, while *my strength* remains personal; this is explicitly an interpretive decision.

Generic observations about several people, paired individuals and an audience usually use plural-we unless the passage presents one corporate agency. **ourpl/**, **ourspl** and **ourselvespl** keep possessive and reflexive number consistent. **ourone/** explicitly selects collective possession. The older **our/** remains collective for compatibility, not a catch-all English-our translation. Spell out the intended interpretation in an analysis rather than relying on the surface ambiguity of English *we*.

## 4. Dialogue economy and its limits

Unmarked established subjects and recoverable same-subject continuations are ordinary later usage. A local nominative can be omitted when case or discourse still uniquely identifies the roles. An overt subject returns after a competing participant or meaningful contrast; it must never disappear merely to reduce repeated shapes.

The paired Hermia/Helena *more I hate/love, more he follows/hates me* exchange licenses first-person subject ellipsis in its first half: each speaker supplies the deictic centre of her own antiphonal turn. The second half retains **I(acc.)** and overt **he**, keeping the role reversal explicit. This is a narrow conversational development from the older overt-subject preference, not permission to erase all turn-initial pronouns. Parallel syntax is deliberately preserved because it carries the joke and emotional contrast.

The independent examples and source-linked revision ledger document this permission. Mechanical compilation does not decide whether a referent is recoverable. In particular Helen's *he desires some private speech* retains **he** after her own *I have spoken*: the subject changes there.

## 5. Native vocabulary and editorial repairs

Common words use native stems and transparent native derivations. Person and place names may retain adapted source sounds. Source-culture ranks, coins, measures, animals and objects retain their distinctions: translating them natively does not turn a groat into a penny or a thousand into a different quantity.

Some 0.10 forms were unjustified imitations of source words. Their replacement is an editorial correction, not evidence that the fictional language passed through an English-like phase. Native corrections developed in the historical project are adopted where applicable, and additional dramatic terms receive explicit native derivations or deliberately authored roots. The frozen Bible is not silently repaired where it still contains a problematic older form.

The **marry** key is an assent-oath, now a native emphatic assent. It is not the verb **wed**. Every inflected use mistakenly expressing marriage through the oath is repaired in the plays; passive and causative order is preserved. The native ratification **kom rē’ne**, approximately *so may it be*, replaces the sound *amen* while retaining repeated assent where the source repeats it.

## 6. Wordplay and social voice

Quince's confused *paramour/paragon* pair receives the deliberately authored native pair **yalangz/farangz**, illicit-beloved versus admired-exemplar. Both share the love stem; the first is a conventional colloquial contraction of forbidden-love, the second a courtly good-love epithet. Flute corrects that same pair. This preserves a plausible native verbal mistake and its explanation; it does not claim to reproduce English sounds exactly or change every use of paragon into this epithet.

Bottom's pretentious address keeps an inflated native title. Correcting every comic malapropism into polished language would destroy his voice. The dolphin/dauphin and married/marred sound plays do not transfer intact; their literal claims remain and the commentary records the loss. Do not pretend a footnote recreates a spoken joke.

The Countess's *mortal* in All's Well 0024 remains a narrow, explicitly unresolved literary reading, retained as **mortal_legacy**. It is an editorial exception, not productive later vocabulary. Metre, every pun and independent literary certification remain outside the structural validation claim.

## 7. Dictionary scopes and reproducible continuation

The package distinguishes **later-standard**, **later-literary**, **historical-reference** and **editorial-exception** entries. Historical-only vocabulary remains searchable and traceable to the frozen witness; its availability is not automatic approval for ordinary later speech. Promote it consciously, recording semantic or register change and provenance. Stable semantic keys keep histories distinct from preferred spellings.

The Forge branch descends from the pinned consolidated 0.10 head. It does not clone the huge Bible ancestry or modernize its commits. The new snapshot contains dictionary records, a current grammar wiki, decisions, source-linked play tests and focused grammatical contrasts. Original source text, all 1,970 units, Maxima and the four-column reading structure remain traceable. Save/reopen, coverage, paragraph alignment and folio tests validate the edition's construction; they do not certify literary equivalence.


# Inherited grammar, with explicit 0.11 precedence

The following 0.10 rules remain in force except where the preceding later-language decisions supersede them. In particular 0.11 controls pronoun choice, the narrow turn-initial antiphonal ellipsis, dictionary scope and current working paths. Historical version references in this inherited section identify their original context.

# Vayrshemon 0.10 — current dramatic reference

This reference governs new composition from the consolidated All’s Well (0.8) and Midsummer (0.9) work. Those editions remain immutable. Earlier wiki articles document the development of the language; where they conflict with this reference, this version takes precedence for new work. This is an authored working baseline, not a claim that a speech community has attested the proposed grammar. Archive evidence, editorial decisions and unresolved literary readings remain distinct.

## 1. Clause order and reference

Neutral transitive clauses prefer object–subject–verb. A continuing topic can precede its object; a focused object can precede the subject. Case, discourse and punctuation must keep participant roles recoverable. This permits variation with a reason, not arbitrary permutations. Lexical subjects can take nominative -läy; fronted objects and nominal predicates use accusative -get. Nominative may be absent on an established, unambiguous subject. Pronouns are ordinarily unmarked as subjects. Restore the subject after a speaker change, topic shift, competing referent or contrast. Never let a page break decide who did an action.

Titles, vocatives, headings, dictionary citation forms and answer fragments may be bare. Lists can share a predicate. Balanced proverbial or antithetical clauses may omit a recoverable copula. An unfinished sentence stays unfinished. A source-unit split used for pagination is not a new sentence.

## 2. Nouns, modifiers, case and possession

The thing-class prefix remains available for classification, contrast and emphatic introduction. A known lexical noun does not require it on every appearance. This is the 0.8 dramatic extension of earlier restricted omission; do not apply old prefix-required tests to new dramatic prose without their historical context.

Count plural -un and dual -et precede case. A collective or generic class need not be pluralized; mass nouns receive count marking only for portions or kinds. A light descriptive attribute may precede its head. A heavier descriptive modifier follows with ’mū, or uses an explicit relative. Qualifying stems precede heads in compounds. One head receives one outer case: nominative, accusative, dative, genitive, source, location, goal or means. Modifier/adverb conversion is a separate category.

Bound possessors precede a head with ’; nested possessors scope inward. The final head bears the outer case. Free genitive ’i or linker i replaces an opaque chain. Use each:dat of you, not you:gen:dat: the latter attaches competing cases to one head. Spatial adpositions remain available alongside ’tha (source), ’ni (location), ’ra (goal) and ’rin (means). An external agent, an instrument and a companion are different relations, even when English uses with/by for several of them.

## 3. People and social register

Keep I singular, collective/institutional we and individuated plural we distinct. A ruler’s institutional we is not evidence of several bodies. Formal/plural you and familiar singular you reflect intimacy, public formality, rebuke or rank in the scene; they are not assigned mechanically from speaker rank. He, she, animate they and inanimate it retain the inherited proposed paradigm.

Generic vem denotes a person or a human anaphor (someone, one who). Cardinal neksus denotes one counted member, including a selected person. A measured mile, one grape, one in ten, one by one and one shared cry require quantity. Nonhuman elliptical one can use the cardinal with its recoverable noun. This distinction is now applied to both plays. Do not blindly replace every English one.

## 4. Tense, aspect and voice

The finite pattern is root + voice/derivation + tense/aspect. Present ow, past iz and future at combine with simple ya, progressive am or perfect fu where declared in morphology.json. A perfect relates an earlier event to a reference state; it is not a stylistic synonym for past. The reduced generic ya expresses dispositions or maxims, not necessarily an event happening now. A source will may express desire or intention rather than future tense.

Passive im, causative ar, reflexive ir and reciprocal il precede the clause ending. Ordered combinations preserve scope: passive then causative means causing something to be undergone; causative then passive means undergoing a caused event. Do not sort the suffixes alphabetically or equate these constructions. A passive need not supply an agent absent from the source. Commands, imposed marriage, consent, promises and contractual conditions remain semantically distinct.

Infinitive ’en marks a complement; a perfect infinitive is licensed for anteriority, as in having amended one’s faults. It retains the inherited perf:inf spelling; it does not locate an independent present-tense event. Other finite-tense-plus-infinitive stacks are not licensed by this baseline. Event nominalization ef’ and zero conversion remain available, with the grammatical role determined by the construction.

## 5. Attendant actions and subject tracking

The converb ’es has a same-subject interpretation in an attendant-action chain. Different-subject ’esta requires an explicit or locally unambiguous background subject. Titania sleeping while the clowns enter is not the clowns sleeping as they enter. The suffix itself identifies neither person nor gender. An overt while-clause is an ordinary alternative. Both converbs follow voice and exclude an independent finite tense. An infinitive complement is not an attendant action.

These are discourse rules. The compiler checks incompatible endings; it cannot infer the correct referent from Shakespeare. The editor must record participants in the source-aligned analysis and review ambiguous pronouns.

## 6. Mood, modality and negation

Imperative e directs an action. Optative ’ne expresses a wish, including inherited tenseless wishes. Ritual jussive ’rai follows a finite form and presents a state as imposed by a ritual utterance. It describes the speaker’s performative intention, not guaranteed success. Preserve the distinction in spells and their reversals.

Possibility/permission, ability, necessity and defeasible expectation remain distinct. A free modal precedes its scope; an unstressed bound modal can follow the finite predicate. Do not duplicate a free modal with its bound equivalent. Irrealis ’ve expresses a hypothetical/counterfactual interpretation and may follow a modal.

Negative ma before a constituent is narrow in scope. Predicate negation before a necessity ending means necessity of not acting: must not slumber. Negating need means absence of obligation: need not slumber. Final ma can deny a completed assertion or ability. Keep these scopes explicit. Lest already encodes avoidance; adding a negative can reverse it. A negative quantifier is not automatically a redundant second negation.

Thimfora has no exclamation mark. Marked expressive force uses the new clause-final enclitic **’hā** (encoded **’hÅ**, gloss **excl.**). It attaches to the final host after that host’s case, tense, mood or reportative ending; its scope is the completed utterance. Thus an emphatic statement, urgent command, intense wish and expressive ritual utterance remain different constructions: pr:excl, imp:excl, opt:excl and pr:juss:excl. It can also follow a nominal fragment or vocative without inventing a missing verb. Close with the ordinary sentence separator, not a borrowed exclamation glyph.

The enclitic marks the speaker’s heightened affect or emphatic delivery. It does not specify anger versus joy, increase factual certainty, impose a command by itself, or change negation scope. An unmarked command remains a command; an emotionally charged event does not automatically require expressive grammar. Existing authored exclamations are migrated with their original command/wish/statement distinctions; the English retains its punctuation. In new writing, choose the marked form for discourse reasons, not for every sentence. This is a 0.10 authored construction, not an archival discovery.

## 7. Claims, appearance, subordination and comparison

Reportative ’vë follows a finite predicate and attributes the proposition to a report. Apparent ’sëm marks a perceived or seeming proposition; it asserts neither certainty nor necessarily deception. Keep a materially relevant reporter visible. Arbitrary stacking of these particles is not licensed; express a reporting clause plus an apparent proposition when both matter.

Use explicit content and relative boundaries where roles could be confused. A resumptive participant or overt relation can identify a relative’s role. An initial framing subordinator or a clearly punctuated clause-final subordinator is licensed for temporal, causal, conditional and concessive clauses. Open if and counterfactual asif differ. Venki introduces imagined resemblance; irrealis belongs on the imagined predicate. Ordinary role/comparison uses the inherited comparison construction.

Polar questions, interrogative families, interrupted dialogue and quoted assertions keep their inherited readings. Quoted soldiers’ feigned speech is marked as such and is not assigned fictitious dictionary meanings.

## 8. Lexicon, variation and ambiguity

Use the canonical semantic keys in lexicon.json. Each key has one current form, meaning, provenance and stable semantic identifier. Distinct meanings may share a headword. Explicit historical forms are readable alternatives for old editions, not fallback defaults for new work. The package resolves the old twelve competing TSV declarations once; future file ordering cannot choose a word’s meaning.

Bar as a rigid object remains zaril; bar_obstruct restores boundary+close for preventing action. Mortal now uses die+able, distinct from dead. Existing deadly serves lethal danger. All’s Well 0024 retains its ambiguous historical mortal wording under mortal_legacy: this audit does not pretend to settle that literary reading. Do not generalize this exception to new prose.

Related polysemy, nominal/verbal conversion, conventional names and contextual homophones remain. Ten unrelated homophone groups are explicitly listed for contextual review (for example bone/noise and parent/leg). They have separate semantic keys. Similar sound alone is not proof of an error; equally, dictionary grouping is not proof of shared meaning. Expand an ambiguous phrase with an existing descriptive constituent or propose a reviewed lexical distinction when a new passage requires it.

Choose variants for reference, scope and register. Courtly address, fairy refrains, hesitant craftsmen, paired rebuttals, parallel more…more constructions and a character’s deliberately wrong words may repeat. Do not optimize diversity by random synonym replacement. Frequency and repetition reports are diagnostics, not a substitute for a readable scene. Rare reportative and different-subject forms remain proposals with limited examples, not evidence of a complete dialect history.

## 9. Quantity and the Unta evidence

The directly checked names for values zero through twelve are fōlem, neksus, thātus, venik, bārum, jensis, ōdēum, pärsus, krizum, deksin, eshnä, yamen, ämēnō. Note bārum’s macron and pärsus’s diaeresis. The package preserves exact Thimfora symbol identities through the approved Roman mapping.

New composition uses those names. Earlier tatus, tren, ketren, pent, sept and dükal remain historical readings in versioned records. Declared productive compounds follow their updated components. Ordinal second, third and fourth use the corresponding cardinal plus ’t; lexical first/neks and once/veneks remain conventional inherited exceptions. Cardinal two and ordinal second are no longer one form.

Source quantities retain their mathematical value. Decimal hundred and thousand remain explicit source-culture quantities; they do not become 144 or 1728. Ten-fourteen/fifty-type conventional decimal compounds retain the declared additive or multiplicative interpretation; new source quantities should be recorded with an exact value. Written Unta 10 means twelve, while the word eshnä denotes decimal ten. Act and scene labels retain their original displayed values. This consolidation does not change page-number glyphs or the formatter. The chart’s higher numeral readings need further systematic treatment; do not extrapolate unverified spoken forms from its highest powers.

## 10. Orthography, gloss and testing

Use the approved Maxima/Minima identity map at real sentence starts and proper names. Never Unicode-uppercase or normalize legacy font slots. A continuing sentence split across source units remains a continuing sentence. Thimfora and Transliteration 7 receive identical slots and case. The gloss follows target order and abbreviates grammatical roles; the English remains the supplied source, not a reverse translation of the gloss.

Canonical compiler checks cover declared vocabulary, compatible case/number/tense, clause-form exclusivity, voice order and particle placement. The minimal pairs include valid constructions and intentional invalid contrasts. Neither successful compilation nor a Forge page test proves literary equivalence. The two full ledgers preserve every source unit, explicit edits, lexical migration and paragraph starts. The old editions and their original test results remain historical evidence. New translation work begins by loading the pinned 0.10 package and branching the consolidated Forge project, then records its own additions and review decisions.
