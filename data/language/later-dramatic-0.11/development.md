# Later dramatic Vayrshemon 0.11

## 1. What the chronology means

The frozen Bible is the historical literary witness. The two plays use a later dramatic standard. Project creation dates are not fictional language dates: 0.10 was assembled before the Bible, and 0.11 reconciles that work with the subsequently developed historical witness. This is authored language history, not a claim of archaeological attestation. The awkward Biblical translation is a particular foreign-text register, not a specimen of every ancient speaker's ordinary conversation.

The Bible is never rewritten by this work. Historical forms can survive in ritual, quotation and deliberate archaism. New translations start from 0.11 and record their own versioned additions. They must not silently start from either an earlier play compiler or the frozen Biblical dictionary.

## 2. Inherited grammar and later developments

The ordinary tense, aspect, voice, case and mood inventory remains conservative. There is no invented wholesale sound shift and no random replacement of suffixes. Later change is visible in wordhood, reference and register: familiar compounds can become single inflecting words; case marking becomes less obligatory for an established, recoverable topic; dialogue permits carefully licensed omission rather than repeating a full formal introduction at every turn.

The inherited thing-class prefix remains productive in explicit classification, unfamiliar referents and marked ritual diction. It is not mechanically attached to every noun in a later conversation. Preserve the actual numeral values, negation, modal strength and participant roles. A source page break cannot reset reference.

The specific lexicalizations are listed in lexical-decisions.json. For example, historical **hefak gur** becomes ordinary **hefakgur** (war), and **pom sōlum** becomes **pomsōlum** (scholar). Old spaced phrases remain available as deliberate formal constructions. These are bounded conventional words, not a rule stripping spaces from arbitrary text. Long descriptive names can remain phrases. Outer inflection belongs to the final head of such a phrase; ordinary free phrases should instead be authored with explicit constituent analyses when internal roles matter.

## 3. Four first-person perspectives

**I(sg.)** is one speaking self; **I(pl.)** is internal plurality within a speaking identity; **we(sg.)** is a group or office construed as one acting whole; **we(pl.)** is several individuated participants. Later speech inherits all four possibilities. The plural-I construction remains marked and available; it is not compulsory for gods, magical characters, emotional conflict or actors playing a part.

The King can invoke his office with singular-we, then return to personal I. This alternation conveys a change of stance. It does not authorize changing an English personal *me* into royal *us*. The 0.11 review restores four such personal references in All's Well. Bertram's formal response on accepting military command uses office-oriented singular-we, while *my strength* remains personal; this is explicitly an interpretive decision.

Generic observations about several people, paired individuals and an audience usually use plural-we unless the passage presents one corporate agency. **ourpl/**, **ourspl** and **ourselvespl** keep possessive and reflexive number consistent. **ourone/** explicitly selects collective possession. The older **our/** remains collective for compatibility, not a catch-all English-our translation. Spell out the intended interpretation in an analysis rather than relying on the surface ambiguity of English *we*.

## 4. Dialogue economy and its limits

Unmarked established subjects and recoverable same-subject continuations are ordinary later usage. A local nominative can be omitted when case or discourse still uniquely identifies the roles. An overt subject returns after a competing participant or meaningful contrast; it must never disappear merely to reduce repeated shapes.

The paired Hermia/Helena *more I hate/love, more he follows/hates me* exchange licenses first-person subject ellipsis in its first half: each speaker supplies the deictic centre of her own antiphonal turn. The second half retains **I(acc.)** and overt **he**, keeping the role reversal explicit. This is a narrow conversational development from the older overt-subject preference, not permission to erase all turn-initial pronouns. Parallel syntax is deliberately preserved because it carries the joke and emotional contrast.

The independent examples and source-linked revision ledger document this permission. Mechanical compilation does not decide whether a referent is recoverable. In particular Helen's *he desires some private speech* retains **he** after her own *I have spoken*: the subject changes there.

## 5. Native vocabulary and editorial repairs

Common words use native stems and transparent native derivations. Person and place names may retain adapted source sounds. Source-culture ranks, coins, measures, animals and objects retain their distinctions: translating them natively does not turn a groat into a penny or a thousand into a different quantity.

Some 0.10 forms were unjustified imitations of source words. Their replacement is an editorial correction, not evidence that the fictional language passed through an English-like phase. Native corrections developed in the historical project are adopted where applicable, and additional dramatic terms receive explicit native derivations or deliberately authored roots. The frozen Bible is not silently repaired where it still contains a problematic older form.

The **marry** key is an assent-oath, now a native emphatic assent. It is not the verb **wed**. Every inflected use mistakenly expressing marriage through the oath is repaired in the plays; passive and causative order is preserved. The native ratification **kom rē’ne**, approximately *so may it be*, replaces the sound *amen* while retaining repeated assent where the source repeats it.

## 6. Wordplay and social voice

Quince's confused *paramour/paragon* pair receives the deliberately authored native pair **yalangz/farangz**, illicit-beloved versus admired-exemplar. Both share the love stem; the first is a conventional colloquial contraction of forbidden-love, the second a courtly good-love epithet. Flute corrects that same pair. This preserves a plausible native verbal mistake and its explanation; it does not claim to reproduce English sounds exactly or change every use of paragon into this epithet.

Bottom's pretentious address keeps an inflated native title. Correcting every comic malapropism into polished language would destroy his voice. The dolphin/dauphin and married/marred sound plays do not transfer intact; their literal claims remain and the commentary records the loss. Do not pretend a footnote recreates a spoken joke.

The Countess's *mortal* in All's Well 0024 remains a narrow, explicitly unresolved literary reading, retained as **mortal_legacy**. It is an editorial exception, not productive later vocabulary. Metre, every pun and independent literary certification remain outside the structural validation claim.

## 7. Dictionary scopes and reproducible continuation

The package distinguishes **later-standard**, **later-literary**, **historical-reference** and **editorial-exception** entries. Historical-only vocabulary remains searchable and traceable to the frozen witness; its availability is not automatic approval for ordinary later speech. Promote it consciously, recording semantic or register change and provenance. Stable semantic keys keep histories distinct from preferred spellings.

The Forge branch descends from the pinned consolidated 0.10 head. It does not clone the huge Bible ancestry or modernize its commits. The new snapshot contains dictionary records, a current grammar wiki, decisions, source-linked play tests and focused grammatical contrasts. Original source text, all 1,970 units, Maxima and the four-column reading structure remain traceable. Save/reopen, coverage, paragraph alignment and folio tests validate the edition's construction; they do not certify literary equivalence.
