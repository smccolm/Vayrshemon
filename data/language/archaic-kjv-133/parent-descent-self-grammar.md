# KJV 0.10-kjv.9 — descent-self, bereavement and the unfinished departure

This version inherits v8 unchanged and adds Genesis 45–50: 174 verses. All 1,533 supplied Genesis verses are now authored. With unchanged frozen Jonah, the working Bible contains 1,581 of 31,102 verses; 29,521 remain. This finishes one major section, not the requested whole Bible. The historical register remains permanently excluded from modernization. Newly authored senses and names are not evidence of ancient attestation.

## 1. Recognition is personal; divine accompaniment is plural-self

Joseph's I am Joseph in 45:3–4 is singular-I: the sold brother recognizes and names himself. His stated providential account preserves both ye sold me and not you sent me but God. Neither clause is rewritten to resolve the tension. Father to Pharaoh remains a metaphor of authority, not biological paternity or a named divine Father. Joseph's personal promises to feed his kin do not automatically become office-self speech.

In 46:2 Jacob answers here I am with singular-I. The deity's I am God and I will descend with thee in 46:3–4 use plural-I beside a singular deity title. The internal plurality remains conspicuous, without prematurely naming Trinitarian persons. Bring thee up again remains open to personal return, burial and descendants; the target does not silently settle it. Pharaoh's formal grants in 45:18 and royal livestock in 47:6 retain office-self plural-I and mypl established in v8.

## 2. Migration counts persons without forcing a tidy genealogy

The Genesis 46 list keeps the repeated sons formula, named women, dead sons, descendants and Egypt-born sons. Dan's sons has one listed name; Benjamin's ten named sons remain beside his recent little-lad description. Thirty-three, sixteen, fourteen and seven remain the stated branch totals, alongside sixty-six travelers excluding sons' wives and seventy for the house. These are distinct scopes, not a translator's license to add or remove people. Souls uses the historical life-self term, retaining the awkward count of selves.

Hanoch of Reuben, Zohar of Simeon, Elon of Zebulun and Hezron of Pharez receive source-scoped referent records. A shared written form does not prove identity with an earlier bearer. Job of Issachar is not equated with the later book's central figure or replaced by Jashub. Belah, Shaul, Ishuah and Isui retain supplied distinctions. Padan and Rameses remain the source forms without an imposed map reconciliation.

## 3. Hunger creates a claimed whole without erasing coercion

The Egyptian petition in 47:15–25 uses singular-we for one endangered population. Bodies, lands and each man's field remain plural or distributive. We and our land joins people and soil in a survival whole; the explicit both we and land can then unpack its members. The transfer remains coercive in its narrated circumstances: money is exhausted, livestock exchanged, then people and land bought for bread. Thankful speech is represented as speech, not translator endorsement.

Joseph's bargaining I can remain personal, but the formal I have bought you for Pharaoh in 47:23 uses the delegated office-self. He acts as the administrative authority while naming Pharaoh as beneficiary. Cities remains the supplied KJV relocation wording; priests retain their land exception. One fifth and four remaining shares preserve exact arithmetic despite Unta's base. Unto this day belongs to the narrator's timeframe, not a present legal claim about Egypt.

## 4. Adoption and inheritance introduce an ancestral estate-self

Genesis 48:5 uses mypl/sons for Jacob's incorporation of Ephraim and Manasseh into his lineage beside Reuben and Simeon. Joseph's they are my sons in 48:9 remains personal paternity. These possessives express different claims, not rival biological facts. Jacob's remembered arrival and Rachel's death use ordinary singular-I; the estate-self does not swallow the old man's embodied memory.

In 48:22 plural-I grants the additional portion, then singular-I claims the earlier taking with sword and bow. The conquest claim remains the speaker's even where the earlier narrative makes it awkward. In 49:7 plural-I divides and scatters descendants within Jacob and Israel: the speaking ancestor encompasses the lineage in which they will be distributed. This is a human corporate self, not a hidden quotation from God. The personal appeal for salvation in 49:18 and the bodily burial request return to singular-I.

## 5. Crossed hands and layered blessings preserve reference

Right-hand direction uses kjv_right_side, never the inherited right of moral entitlement. Ephraim is in Joseph's right toward Israel's left, Manasseh in his left toward Israel's right; Israel deliberately puts his right on Ephraim. Dim sight does not turn the crossing into an accidental mistake. Manasseh stays firstborn while his younger brother receives precedence. The old man's repeated I know remains personal insistence.

The source says he blessed Joseph while his hands rest on the sons; that representative blessing remains. God and the redeeming Angel stay parallel invocations without assigning named Trinitarian identities. My name named on them concerns lineage, not replacement of their given names. Singular thee within blessing them and plural you in the future return preserve the source's shifting address.

## 6. Poetic images are not modern explanatory prose

Genesis 49 retains direct-address to third-person shifts, human names with animal predicates, and young/old lion imagery. Cruelty's instruments in habitations, digging down a wall, two burdens, goodly words from the hind and red eyes with wine follow the supplied KJV rather than alternatives from other textual traditions. The lawgiver between feet and Shiloh remain deliberately unresolved; no Jesus substitution or invented doctrinal identification is inserted.

The poem keeps the grape-blood washing, fruitful bough by a water well, piled arms of hands strengthened by divine hands, shepherd and stone parenthesis, heaven/deep contrast, breasts and womb, and the head's crown as its top. New whelp, ass-colt, haven, tribute, tribe, delicacy, division and biting senses avoid misleading ordinary homonyms. Dan's snake biting a horse causes its rider to fall; Benjamin ravins like a wolf, not raven the bird. Native exclamatory morphology carries the urgent dismissal, relieved Enough and Joseph's hand-placement protest without unsupported punctuation.

## 7. Burial and remembrance remain material and archaic

Jacob gathers feet into bed, releases breath and is gathered to his people: bodily action and ancestral formula deliberately echo one another. Embalming is body-preservation, not revival; a coffin is a container for the dead, not a specified material or shape. Forty treatment days, seventy Egyptian mourning days and seven days at Atad remain separately scoped. The translator does not automatically add overlapping spans into one chronology.

The grave Jacob says he dug remains beside the purchased Machpelah cave. Rachel's roadside burial remains distinct from Leah's stated cave burial. Beyond Jordan preserves the narrator's viewpoint rather than repairing the route. Joseph's father's-house account keeps intermediaries, the oath, the return to Egypt and the procession's excluded children and livestock. The eventual transport of Joseph's bones remains future; Genesis ends with his coffin still in Egypt.

## 8. Refusing God's place, and continuing the whole Bible

The brothers' request for forgiveness uses singular-we as one guilty family. Their alleged dying-father instruction retains a nested singular-I, but its truth is not independently certified or declared fraudulent. Joseph's am I in God's place is a rhetorical singular-I question refusing the role; it must not be rendered as an affirmative divine plural-I. Their evil intention and God's good intention remain two attributed purposes in Joseph's account. Personal I promises food to them and their little ones.
