# Historical KJV 0.10-kjv.24 · descendants, claims and binding silence

Numbers 26–30 adds 175 authored verses to the pinned v23 historical branch. The Bible remains incomplete: 4,713 of 31,102 verses. Existing targets, modern v0.10 and frozen Jonah remain intact. These are translator decisions and self-review, not independent certification.

## One household speaking through five daughters

Mahlah, Noah, Hoglah, Milcah and Tirzah are five separately named petitioners. Their our father and give unto us in 27:3–4 use our-one and singular-we because the claim concerns one household inheritance and continuing name. This does not deny that five people stand there. Personal names, plural daughters and their plural obligations remain explicit outside the corporate request. The source does not give the sisters a plural-I speech, so none is invented. Divine first-person gifts and commands continue plural-I, while Moses remains an individual and the two accused leaders receive plural you in 27:14.

## Family names and homonymous people

The census uses a deliberate analytic family-of-ancestor construction for eponymous clans. It retains every listed group without manufacturing an unrelated root for every English -ite suffix. The English source column preserves the exact ethnonym spelling. Nemuel of Reuben and Nemuel of Simeon have distinct semantic entries with the same authored surface form. Female Noah, daughter Milcah, Asher's Sarah, Ephraim's Becher, Benjamin's Bela and Gilead/Shechem as persons are distinguished from earlier name-bearers and places. Sarah is not silently corrected to Serah. Korathites is preserved as a supplied family-name loan rather than changed to Korahites. Source genealogy differences are not reconciled by moving a son to a different father.

## Counting survivors without erasing exceptions

The twelve territorial totals add to 601,730. They are 1,820 below the earlier 603,550, but no invented single cause is asserted. The Levite 23,000 are males from one month upward and excluded from the territorial census of war-capable men twenty and upward. The surviving Korah children in 26:11 remain beside the swallowing and fire account. Caleb and Joshua are the named exceptions among previously enumerated men; this does not say that every earlier woman, child or Levite died. A census is a constrained count, not the language's universal definition of a people.

## Inheritance by proportions, lot and contingent kinship

Population-based shares and allocation by lot both remain in 26:53–56, without an invented procedure reconciling them. The daughters' claim in 27 is not turned into a modern general equality statement or dismissed by a blanket claim that women cannot inherit. The branching order is preserved: no son → daughter; no daughter → brothers; no brothers → father's brothers; no paternal brothers → nearest family kinsman. A new case/plea sense means a question submitted for judgment, avoiding the inherited verb cause and the physically enclosing case homograph.

## Partial succession and mediated divine authority

Moses is permitted to see but is told he will be gathered to his people. Joshua receives some of his honour, not all without qualification. Singular hand in 27:18 and plural hands in 27:23 are both preserved. Eleazar's Urim consultation remains a separate role. The text does not explain the apparatus or identify a modern divination technology. The sheep-without-shepherd simile retains four leadership movements: going out, going in, leading out and bringing in.

## The deity's food and exact repeating obligations

Plural-I speaks of my bread, my offering and sweet smell in 28:2. These bodily cult expressions are deliberately awkward in Vayrshemon rather than spiritualized into a smooth modern philosophy. Continual-rite is an explicit contextual sense of regular observance; it does not invent a continuously burning mechanism. Meat offering remains the established grain-gift term, while meat of the sacrifice in 28:24 is food. Strong wine is not silently distilled spirits. Several tenth means one separate tenth per animal, not an unspecified handful of tenths. Ephah is the measure sense, distinct from the personal name.

## Dates, fractions and additive ritual schedules

All repeated lists are fully translated, with the source's kid/goat and drink/drinks variation preserved. Daily, Sabbath, monthly and festival duties coexist where the text says besides. The seven-day feast descends through 13,12,11,10,9,8,7 bullocks: 70 total, alongside 14 rams and 98 lambs. The eighth day resets to one bullock, one ram and seven lambs; it does not continue to six bullocks. Thirds and quarters of a hin remain exact; flour rates are three tenths per bullock, two per ram and one per lamb. Where this passage does not name the flour unit, no unit or metric volume is inserted. Any work in 29:7 remains distinct from servile work elsewhere. Soul-affliction is not silently defined as a particular bodily practice.

## Binding silence and late annulment

The vow chapter retains its patriarchal household authority as attributed ancient law. It does not endorse that law for the reader or modernize it away. Silence means being silent, not peace as absence of war. A father or husband may disallow in the day of hearing; same-day silence confirms. Widowed and divorced women have vows that stand, explicitly distinct from the youthful daughter-in-father's-house condition. The repeated husband-house formulation is translated in full. Late annulment transfers iniquity to the husband in 30:15; the clause is not dropped to make the rules simpler. Annul-obligation is a bond-ending verb, distinct from physical void. Carrying iniquity uses burden-carry, never the birth verb.
