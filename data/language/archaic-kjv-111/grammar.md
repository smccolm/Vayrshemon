# KJV historical register 0.10-kjv.111 — Psalms 37–48

This continues the sealed 0.10-kjv.110 head, adding 198 authored verses and bringing Psalms to 709 verses through Psalm48. Earlier targets and seals remain unchanged. The Bible remains incomplete and this historical edition is permanently excluded from modernization and consolidation.

## A people speaking as I-plural

Psalm44 develops the corporate `ipl` voice without requiring that every plural-self be divine or royal. Its my-king in44:4 uses `mypl`; the bow-and-sword confession in44:6 and the personified face-shame in44:15 use `ipl` and `mypl`. The poem itself is communal, moving between we and a singular representative voice. These forms make the singular voice internally multiple: one speaking people, with members. They do not invent a named king, claim a diagnosis, or turn the human congregation into God. Personal aging and observation inPsalm37, bodily suffering inPsalm38, and the poet's promise in45:17 retain ordinary-I. Public testimony in40:9 does not become plural-I merely because a congregation hears it.

## Physical members and shared singular-we

Psalm44:1 distinguishes plural hearers with plural ears from the common people receiving ancestral testimony. The ancestry possessive is `ourone`, while the fathers remain plural. Fighters in44:5, retreaters in44:10, scattered food-sheep in44:11, broken people in44:19 and killed people in44:22 use `wepl`. Common rescue, praise, shame, fidelity and redemption use `wesg`. The shared heart in44:18 stands beside plural steps; the shared soul and belly in44:25 personify one suffering community. InPsalm47 the conquerors' feet are plural but inheritance and kingship are shared. Psalm48 similarly distinguishes plural hearing/seeing witnesses from common temple meditation. This is semantic variation controlled by discourse, not random alternation of pronoun prefixes. The construction gloss exposes each choice for future review.

## Divine declaration and unresolved royal address

The explicit divine self-declaration in46:10 uses `ipl` in all three assertions: I-am-God, I-will-be-exalted-among-the-heathen, and I-will-be-exalted-in-the-earth. The surrounding refuge chorus uses singular-we. The change in speaker remains visible. Psalm45 preserves O-God in the throne address and God-thy-God in the following anointing verse, without choosing a later theological identity or adding a cardinal number of divine persons. Its addressee remains second-person. Likewise the personal speaker of40:6 retains opened ears, not a body imported from a later quotation, and the unnamed trusted friend of41:9 is not retroactively given a later narrative name. These unresolved historical forms are intentional features of the edition.

## Refrains, temporal sequence and uncomfortable claims

The two refrains inPsalm42 are not mechanically identical:42:5 has help of his countenance, whereas42:11 has health of my countenance and my God. Psalm43:5 follows the latter. The repeated praise commands in47:6 and the exact return of the refuge refrain in46:7/11 are deliberate poetry, so they are retained. Psalm44's reversal from rescue to abandonment, its protest of fidelity, and its why-do-you-sleep demand remain unharmonized. Psalm37's elder says he has not seen righteous descendants begging; this is retained as the speaker's testimony, not independently certified as a universal social fact. Psalm39 retains no-more at the close and its stranger-with-you relation. Psalm41 keeps make-all-his-bed and the retaliatory purpose of being raised to repay opponents. Earlier religious formulas are not rewritten into a comfortable modern doctrine.

## Five common entries and two names

The new common entries record the bay tree, panting, innumerability, waterspouts and defensive bulwarks. Bay-tree is an authored source-name borrowing, distinct from the inherited brown horse colour. Panting uses a declared breath-fast compound and can describe the psalm's heart or thirsting hart without imposing a medical diagnosis. Innumerable is beyond-count and supplies no total. Waterspout retains the pouring-water image without choosing a modern vortex or a particular waterfall. Defensive-bulwark differs from the besieger's raised bank already present in the dictionary. Hermonites and Mizar are explicit source names; neither receives a modern location or invented native etymology. No compiler, inflectional ending or case-pair change is introduced.

## Contextual sense distinctions in reading and gloss

Conversation in37:14 is conduct, not dialogue. Mark means attentive observation in37:37 and48:13, not making a physical sign. Soundness inPsalm38 is health, while the trumpet's sound in47:5 is audible. Corrupt wounds use physical decay rather than moral corruption. Dumb and deaf are speech/hearing comparisons, not insults about intelligence. Halt in38:17 is limping, fast arrows are firmly fixed, fasting is abstention, and the source's sore can intensify an action or name a wound. Stroke and hand-blow in39:10 use striking, not the inherited air-blowing sense. Meat is food; right-hand direction differs from a right/just sceptre; dragons retain the historical creature entry without adding the inherited fantasy creature's wings. Shields in47:9 are defensive objects, not the verb to protect. Tell-towers in48:12 is counting; tell-to-descendants in48:13 is recounting. These distinctions prevent historical awkwardness from becoming accidental lexical nonsense.

## Quantities, identity and grammatical expression

These198verses introduce no explicit cardinal totals. Handbreadth in39:5 is a brevity comparison, not a numeric lifespan. The request for the measure of days does not supply an answer. More-than-hairs and innumerable evils remain uncounted. Counting towers is commanded without a stated total. The corporate body and plural-I do not imply an anatomical count or a number of divine persons. Questions, optatives, hypothetical though/if clauses and inherited neither/nor negative scope remain explicit. Expressive ah and vocative force use the existing grammatical exclamatory construction; no unsupported Thimfora exclamation mark is inserted. Passive-then-causative remembrance in45:17 deliberately means causing a name to be remembered.
