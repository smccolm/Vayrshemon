# Historical KJV 0.10-kjv.32 · Crossing, oath and the accursed city

Authored continuation through Joshua 6, 125 new verses. Whole Bible: 6025/31102 verses, 25077 untranslated. This is not the completed book. Genesis, Exodus, Leviticus, Numbers, Deuteronomy and previously frozen Jonah are inherited without target changes. Seven lexical entries are added; existing lexicon, pronouns, affixes, Maxima and font treatment remain unchanged. The register remains deliberately old, alien and excluded from modernization.

## Divine self and human succession

Direct LORD speech uses `ipl`/`mypl` in Joshua 1:2–9,3:7,5:9,6:2. Moses is my-plural servant, while Joshua is a single human successor whom the deity will make great. Repeated as-with-Moses/so-with-thee clauses retain both continuity and difference of agent. Perfect giving before future treading/conquest remains perfect, not silently changed into a promise with weaker time force.

Joshua’s own commands use personal `i` (6:10). The sword-bearing captain in 5:14 likewise says personal I: the source identifies a captain of the LORD’s army, not an explicit first-person naming of the LORD. Worship/prostration and holy ground remain, but the target does not decide an unstated identity. Internally plural divine identity is intentional; it is not a mechanical rule that makes every extraordinary speaker plural-I.

## City-whole, people-whole and witnesses

The public pledge in 1:16–17 is `wesg`, a people acting as one will. Rahab’s own knowledge/negotiation is personal I. The threatened city is singular-we at 2:9; the hearers and their melting hearts use `wepl` at 2:10–11. Her family’s lives are explicitly plural (`of wepl life:pl`,2:13). Individual human lives are not dissolved into one abstract household life.

The spies’ final report speaks for the invading people: `ourone/hand:pl` and `wesg` (2:24). Plural hands remain plural inside a single corporate identity. This is an authored discourse interpretation, documented rather than claimed as an inflection hidden in the English.

## Two guarantors and one shared oath

The actual two spies use `we:du` for their own life-pledge, disclosure conditions, oath liability and the descent by which Rahab lowered them (2:14,17–20). The future people’s arrival and reception of land use `wesg`. The shared singular life/head of the two guarantors remains source-singular; it is not automatically made two lives or two heads to match grammatical duality. Personal responsibility, corporate invasion and family rescue therefore remain distinguishable.

Blood on a head retains the source liability idiom. Secrecy and staying in the house are real conditions of the oath, not omitted qualifications. The scarlet line is rendered as a physical cord of thread, not the inherited drawn contour sense of line.

## Deception, occupation and rescue

Rahab’s occupation is the supplied harlot sense; no euphemism or invented sexual scene is added. Her claimed ignorance of the men’s origin and departure route remains quoted speech. The narrative immediately preserves their actual concealment under flax on the roof. The translation does not correct a character’s false statement into the narrator’s factual account.

The promised rescue includes kin and possessions as stated. The rescued group is first placed outside the camp (6:23), then Rahab is described as dwelling in Israel to this day (6:25). No unreported admission ceremony is invented to fill that interval.

## Water, memory and historical we

The ark is the covenant/testimony chest; bearing is carrying, never begetting. Water standing in a heap and the harvest overflow are retained without a naturalistic mechanism. City Adam has a distinct semantic identity from the person Adam; Zaretan is not replaced with a modern location.

Twelve stones carried out to the lodging/Gilgal and twelve stones set within the river remain two textual placements (4:3,8–9,20). They are not silently reduced to one set. Future children question their fathers and receive the repeated explanation. The we of the Red Sea/Jordan retrospection (4:23;5:1,6) is singular-we as a historical community across generations, not a false assertion that every hearer physically witnessed both crossings.

## Generation, food and naming

The second circumcision command is followed by the full source distinction between the departing generation and wilderness-born children (5:2–7). It is not represented as repeated surgery on the same bodies. Whole in 5:8 remains a recovery condition, without added clinical advice. Manna cessation, old grain, unleavened cakes and roasted/parched grain stay distinct; corn remains cereal grain rather than modern American maize.

The divine rolling-away of reproach and the name Gilgal stay juxtaposed. The authored proper-name spelling is not falsely presented as a Vayrshemon derivation from roll.

## Destructive sacred claim and exceptions

`kjv_accursed_devoted` is a marked cursed-apart adjective for accursed to the LORD in 6:17–18. It records the source’s destructive sacred reservation, not merely an insult or unlucky object. It relates to the previously documented irreversible devotion sense without erasing the different surface wording. Rahab and those in her house are explicit surviving exceptions. Silver, gold and metal vessels are reserved to the treasury; the city and its contents are burned.

The killings of men, women, young, old and named animals remain tersely translated as the source says; no euphemism, approval, or extra gore is appended. The rebuilding curse retains foundation in firstborn and gates in youngest son, without inventing a mechanism of death or an additional command to kill children. Its optative exclamation uses `:psv:opt:excl`, not an unsupported exclamation glyph.

## Ritual repetition and exact scope

Six days each have one circuit. Seven priests carry seven ram-horn trumpets, and the seventh day has seven circuits. The source’s repeated sevens and repeated command/performance descriptions remain instead of being summarized into a procedural list. Joshua’s shout cue is `:imp:excl`; silence before the cue remains explicit.

Two spies, three-day waits, about 2000 cubits and about 40000 combatants, half-Manasseh and ordinal calendar dates retain their respective scopes. Approximate counts are not exactified; cubits are not converted into an asserted metre measure. Firstborn/youngest are relations, not an invented number of sons.
