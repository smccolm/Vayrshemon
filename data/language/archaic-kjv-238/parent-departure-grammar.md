# Romans 13–16: conscience within the whole and the named greeting voices

These 97 drafted verses complete the 433-verse Romans draft. Projected authored coverage is 28,364 of 31,102, leaving 2,738. Drafts still require publication, native composition, tests and viewed proofs before joining verified coverage. Earlier targets and seals remain unchanged and excluded permanently from modernization. Translator self-review and structural checks are not independent literary certification. Completing this section is not completing the whole Bible.

## Authority and the uncomfortable source claims

The powers of 13:1–3 are ruling authorities, not merely physical ability. The source's God-ordination and good-work/evil-work assertions remain attributed to the letter. They do not become a translator guarantee that every actual ruler acts justly. Damnation in 13:2 records condemnation or judgment without adding an eternal torment scene or specifying an unprinted civil penalty. Minister is an attendant; carrying the sword is not bearing a child or the animal bear.

The conscience and wrath reasons for subjection both remain in 13:5. Tribute, fiscal customs, fear and honour are four separate obligations in 13:7. Custom is not habitual practice here. All five printed prohibitions in 13:9 remain: adultery, killing, stealing, false witness and coveting. The neighbour-love summary does not omit a command found in this source or insert another absent one.

Communal singular-we carries the nearer-salvation, casting-off and putting-on language. The time comparison supplies no calendar date. Armour of light remains a metaphor, not a new weapon inventory. Rioting beside drunkenness is excessive revelry, distinct from a necessarily political street riot. Chambering records sexual bedding rather than simply occupying a bedroom. The three paired conduct groups remain, without assigning a modern sexual identity or an unprinted act. Provision for flesh is preparation to fulfil its desires in the stated purpose clause, not an added prohibition on ordinary bodily sustenance.

## Receiving without erasing conscience

The weak-in-faith person is to be received without the specified doubtful disputes. The eater and non-eater receive reciprocal prohibitions: not despise and not judge. God receiving him remains the reason. The one who eats everything is an indefinite person, not a cardinal one. The singled-out one day in 14:5 remains numerical, while every day and every person's persuaded mind remain distributive.

The complete KJV non-regarding-day clause in 14:6 stays, with all its negations. Both eating and non-eating are related to the Lord and thanksgiving. None of us-one lives to self and each of us-one gives an account keep communal singular-we beside individual reflexives. Shared identity does not cancel personal accountability. The repeated live-to-Lord/die-to-Lord clauses remain deliberately repetitive.

Died, rose and revived all remain in 14:9. Christ's judgment seat in 14:10 is retained as supplied rather than replaced with another witness's God. The divine quoted I-live in 14:11 is plural-self, while the speaker's I-know in 14:14 is personal. The source's knee and tongue statements do not count divine persons.

Unclean in 14:14 is the food/ritual sense, not the unrelated moral-corruption modifier. Nothing unclean of itself remains beside unclean-to-the-person-who-reckons-it-so. Neither side is removed to make the passage tidier. Meat in 14:15/17/20 means food generally, while flesh in 14:21 remains specifically flesh. All things pure and eating with stumbling evil for that man retain their different scopes.

Edifying another is building up interpersonally, not construction of a literal building. Stumbling offence differs from mere insult or moral transgression in another context. Stumble, be offended and be made weak remain separate consequences in 14:21. Allow in 14:22 means approve. The doubting eater's condemnation in 14:23 is not expanded into an unprinted afterlife scene; the final whatever-not-of-faith-is-sin remains.

## Supporting the weak without flattening the voices

Strong we-one carries the weak persons' weaknesses in 15:1. These are not restricted to bodily disease. Neighbour-pleasing is directed toward good and edification rather than unrestricted flattery. The reproaches-fell-on-me citation in 15:3 retains the personal-I voice of the written psalm, consistent with Psalm69:9 and the earlier cited-zeal treatment. It is not silently recast as newly direct Jesus speech merely because the citation is applied to Christ.

The cited confession-among-Gentiles in 15:9 likewise keeps the inherited psalm's personal-I perspective. Direct divine speech elsewhere remains plural-self. This distinction follows discourse attribution rather than globally changing every religious quotation. One mind and one mouth in 15:6 retain both printed numerical images; God, Father and our-one Lord retain their supplied relations. Christ received us in 15:7 stays us, not a different witness's you.

The several citations preserve with-his-people, all-Gentiles/all-peoples, Jesse-root and Gentile trust without replacing one group with another. The letter's personal apostolic voice continues through the travel and prayer requests. Spirit-power is distinct from civic authority. Love-of-Spirit in 15:30 keeps the source possessive relation rather than assigning an unstated possessor/object interpretation.

## Planned travel remains planned

Jerusalem round to Illyricum is the stated proclamation extent. No modern border, mileage or itinerary is supplied. The proposed journey to Spain remains future. No completion of that journey is inferred. The request to be brought onward and to enjoy the recipients' company remains conditional.

Macedonia and Achaia contribute to the poor saints at Jerusalem, with no invented amount or currency. The debtor relation and spiritual/material service exchange remain; fleshly things in this setting means material support, not sexual misconduct. Sealing this fruit remains the supplied image rather than a newly specified wax seal or receipt. The complete fullness-of-blessing-of-the-gospel-of-Christ wording in 15:29 stays.

Both requests in 15:31 remain: release from the Judean unbelievers and acceptance of the Jerusalem service by the saints. The possibility of nonacceptance is not suppressed. Coming joyfully and being refreshed together remains a petition under God's will, not a narrated event.

## Pairs, households and a new I

Phebe's name, sisterhood, service at Cenchrea and help to many and the speaker remain explicit. The translation neither invents a later office hierarchy nor minimizes her work. Priscilla and Aquila are both helpers. Their neck-risk and their house are genuine human-dual references. Plural necks remain the pair's anatomy; this dual is unrelated to plural-self divinity.

Epaenetus is firstfruits of Achaia exactly as supplied, not silently changed to Asia. Mary who laboured on us is a distinct contextual identity, not automatically one of the earlier Marys. Andronicus and Junia are a genuine pair, kinsmen and fellow-prisoners, notable among apostles and in Christ before the speaker. Among is not silently changed to known by. Junia is not renamed, and an external biography or gender argument is not inserted into the verse.

Urbane is our-one helper while Stachys is my beloved. Aristobulus and Narcissus identify households; a greeting to their household members does not prove either named head is present or himself greeted. The in-the-Lord qualifier remains on the Narcissus-household recipients. Tryphena and Tryphosa form another labouring pair, while Persis's much labour has its distinct past description.

Rufus's mother and mine preserves the shared mother expression without asserting a biological siblinghood or merging this Rufus with Simon's son. Hermas and Hermes remain distinct names in the same list. Nereus's sister remains unnamed. No fixed number of accompanying saints or brothers is invented.

Mark the divisive people in 16:17 means take notice, not mark their skin. Beautiful speech deceiving the inexperienced in 16:18 differs from being guileless about evil in 16:19. Wisdom in good is not opposed to learning. The bruising of Satan remains the source image rather than a translator instruction to hurt someone.

Tertius explicitly introduces a new personal-I in 16:22: the person who wrote this letter gives his own greeting. That speaker change is not swallowed into the preceding narrator. Gaius's host role means provider of hospitality, not army or astronomical host. Erastus's chamberlain is a civic office, not household chambers and not evidence of castration. Quartus is a name, not an instruction to count a fourth person. The complete blessing in 16:24 and long closing doxology in 16:25–27 remain.

Existing Priscilla, Aquila, Cenchrea and Timotheus entries are reused with their stated relations. No inflectional ending or pronoun paradigm changes, and no ancient attestation is invented.
