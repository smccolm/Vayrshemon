# KJV historical register 0.10-kjv.18 — transferred wrongs, kin bodies and unequal law

This continuation inherits v17 and adds every Leviticus 16–20 verse: 144 new verses. Two earlier unfinished-draft verses, Leviticus 5:11 and 6:20, correct the same-spelled Ephah personal-name entry to the already existing ephah measurement entry. Their quantities and exact English are unchanged; older editions stay intact. Current authored coverage is 3,394 of 31,102 verses; 27,708 remain. The deliverable remains ONE whole Bible in ONE .vay. The completed historical Bible is never to be modernized. Translation and translator self-review are distinguished from independent literary certification.

## Divine plural-I, collective possession and sacred bodily presence

The deity's appearance in the cloud in 16:2 uses plural-I. The later blood declarations, repeated I-am-LORD formulas and threats against people use the same plural self. My face, name, sanctuary, statutes and judgments take my-plural-self; the source does not specify a numerical count of faces or separate actors, and none is invented. In 20:26 predicative mine means belonging to that plural self. The addressed people's plural you remains distinct from inherited singular-we, which is not inserted into clauses without a first-person group. The repeated self-identification formulas are source repetition intentionally retained in this archaic edition, not a reason to randomize divine identity. Later explicit Trinity passages are still untranslated.

## Two goats and the location of transferred wrongdoing

The two goats are presented together and assigned different lots. One is killed as the people's sin offering; the other is presented alive and sent into wilderness. Scapegoat names the source's sent-goat role, not an unprinted proper divine name or merely a modern falsely accused person. In 16:21 the text expressly puts confessed wrongs on its head, so their transfer and the goat's bearing them remain literal. No cliff, subsequent killing or unseen recipient is added. Aaron's bullock concerns himself and his household; the people's goat has a different represented group. Both hands are laid on the living goat, while seven finger sprinklings have their separately stated locations. Generic human one in 16:27 is not cardinal one animal.

## Access, clothes and distinct ritual recipients

Not at all times restricts entry rather than banning every entry. Incense cloud covers the mercyseat lest the priest die. The priest changes and leaves linen garments, washes, and performs later offerings. The living-goat releaser and carcass burner each wash before reentering camp. Holy place, tent, altar, priests, household and congregation are distinct recipients of atonement, preserving the source's alien application to both things and people. The seventh-month tenth-day affliction of souls is retained without adding an unprinted detailed fasting rule. Once each year is frequency, not a one-year duration. Anointing-agent and final he ambiguities remain where the source does not name an actor.

## Blood, liability and explicitly different consequences

Chapter 17 links flesh's life with blood and assigns blood to altar atonement. In-the-blood and blood-is-life formulations retain their different wording; no modern scientific explanation or purely symbolic replacement is added. Failure to bring specified slaughter to the sanctuary incurs imputed blood liability. Hunting edible game has its own pouring-and-covering instruction. Eating self-dead or beast-torn flesh leads to the stated washing/waiting rule; failure to wash incurs iniquity. These are not collapsed into the different sanction against deliberate blood eating. Cut off stays less procedurally specific than a verse that expressly commands death, stoning or burning.

## Kinship and shared bodily possession

Nakedness in chapters 18 and 20 receives a contextual sexual-access sense sharing the earlier bodily-exposure form. This does not retroactively turn every bare-body reference into sexual conduct. The source maps a wife's nakedness to her husband and descendants' nakedness to the addressed self. Those relational possessives remain instead of being replaced by a smooth modern list of relationship labels. Paternal and maternal links, home/elsewhere birth, lifetime conditions and the source's pronoun shifts are preserved. Daughter-in-law is an authored son-wife compound with the source's own explanatory gloss retained. Fountain of blood remains a bodily metaphor. No consent, age or diagnostic details absent from the supplied wording are invented.

## Inequitable cases and source-attributed prohibitions

The unfree woman in 19:20 is scourged in the supplied she clause while they are not killed because she is unfree; the following male participant brings the ram and receives stated forgiveness. That asymmetry is not silently rewritten as equal punishment or an unstated coercion case. Chapter 20's penalties are preserved as historical source text, without endorsement or procedural elaboration. Both he and they in 20:14 groups a man with two women; it is not a count of only two individuals. Childless and die childless remain different outcomes without an invented biological mechanism. The source's same-sex and animal-sex prohibitions retain their source predicates without added modern identity labels. Confusion is a stated boundary judgment, not a mental-health diagnosis. Whoring after a cult recipient is a documented devotional metaphor, not a modern claim about a religion.

## Provision, adjudication and material distinctions

Gleanings are crop remnants, distinct from the action glean or the whole harvest. Not gathering every grape leaves produce for poor people and strangers; it does not forbid every grape harvest. Impartial judgment toward poor and mighty coexists with material provision for the poor. Neighbour-love explicitly extends to the resident stranger in 19:34. Wages are owed payment and cannot be held overnight. Meteyard, weight, ephah and hin retain their separate source measurement domains without guessed metric conversions. Equitable just uses the inherited righteous sense, not the focus adverb merely. Molten gods use the metal-casting sense, not thrown gods. Fruit is described as uncircumcised for three years, holy in the fourth, edible in the fifth; the bodily metaphor is not replaced by unripe. Observe times has a contextual divination sense distinct from ordinary observation of laws.
