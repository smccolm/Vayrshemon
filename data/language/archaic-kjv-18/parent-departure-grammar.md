# KJV historical register 0.10-kjv.17 — creature rules, bodily boundaries and affected houses

This working continuation inherits v16 without changing its translated text and authors every Leviticus 11–15 verse: 204 new verses. Genesis, Exodus, Leviticus 1–10 and frozen Jonah remain preserved. Combined coverage is 3,250 of 31,102 verses; 27,852 remain. The deliverable remains ONE entire Bible in ONE .vay, permanently excluded from modernization after completion. These are authored developments and translator self-review, not independent literary certification.

## Plural divine self and the uncertain personal observer

Leviticus 11:44–45 uses plural-I for the speaker who is LORD, is holy and brings the people out of Egypt. The repeated holy-I clauses retain that number, while the addressees remain plural you and yourselves. In 14:34 the same plural-I gives the land and puts the plague in a house: explicit agency is not softened to mere permission. In 14:35 the owner reports it seems to me with ordinary personal I, preserving uncertainty. My tabernacle in 15:31 uses my-plural-self. No first-person singular-we is manufactured where the source supplies no first-person group; its earlier established collective-whole uses remain inherited. Plural-I is a user-authorized extension, not falsely attributed to a row missing from the original pronoun chart. Later explicit Trinity passages remain untranslated.

## Source creature categories and authored loans

Hoof division, cloven feet and cud chewing remain separate conjunctive predicates. Failed conditions distinguish camel/coney/hare from swine. The source assertions about cud and four-footed flying creepers are preserved without modern zoological correction. Fins differ from wings; aquatic scales differ from balance pans; paws differ from hooves. Rare names such as ossifrage, ospray, gier eagle and bald locust receive declared historical source-label loans. They are not presented as independently recovered ancient words or confidently identified modern species. Bat remains in its supplied list position. Animal mole has its own sense, distinct from the inherited skin mark. Existing pig, raven, owl, hawk, beetle, snail and fish senses are reused when appropriate.

## Contact, material and time as separate conditions

Living creature, carcass, touching, carrying and eating are not interchangeable. Carrying can require laundering where touching alone specifies waiting until evening. Water plus carcass contamination differs from clean water sources explicitly excepted in 11:36. Dry sowing seed remains clean where watered seed in the next verse becomes unclean; water alone without the carcass condition is not made a universal contaminant. Earthen vessels are broken while specified other objects are washed. Ovens and pot ranges are destroyed under their own rule. Ritual clean/unclean continues to differ from moral worth and from the holy/common axis established in v16.

## Birth, periods and unequal resources

Male-child birth has seven days and thirty-three days; female-child birth has two weeks and sixty-six days. Circumcision is on the eighth day. The unequal source durations are preserved without an invented rationale. Childbirth's first-year lamb plus a bird differs from its two-bird inability alternative. The later skin-cleansing inability rule still retains one lamb and a log of oil, reduces flour from three tenths to one tenth, and supplies birds for the remaining offerings. Named source log is not converted into a guessed modern volume. Sin-offering terminology is retained without inventing an intentional moral offence as the cause of childbirth or bodily discharge.

## Observation, declaration and healing

Source leprosy is a historical category spanning skin, textiles and houses, not a retroactive diagnosis of one modern illness. Rising, scab, bright spot, raw lesion flesh, healed boil and scall are distinct. Quick flesh is living tissue, not fast-moving flesh; raw lesion is not uncooked food. Seven-day observation can be repeated, and a prior clean declaration can be revisited after spreading. Whole white coverage can receive a clean verdict while exposed raw flesh reverses it. No black hair in 13:31 differs from no yellow hair later; spreading after cleansing can remove the need to look for yellow hair. Baldness alone is clean. The priest's uttered status judgment is distinct from physically healing the condition; the text explicitly says healed in selected places, and that difference stays visible.

## Textile and house cases

Warp and woof are separate thread systems; warp does not reuse the sense distort. Fretting means material-consuming rather than anxious. Unchanged color after washing can still be unclean even without spread. Rending out a dimmed patch differs from burning a recurrent condition. The supplied difficult bare-within-or-without wording is not replaced with an invented textile reconstruction. House contents are removed before inspection to avoid the stated uncleanness consequence. Affected stones, scraped dust and mortar are separately handled; recurrence after replacement and plastering can require demolition. The house itself is described as healed and receives atonement. The translation preserves this alien extension rather than moving its predicates to an unnamed resident.

## Ritual actors, boundaries and layered bodily application

The priest goes outside camp to inspect the person; the person is not prematurely admitted. Entry to camp then differs from entry to one's own tent. A second shaving includes eyebrows. One bird dies, the other is dipped and released; no symbolic transfer mechanism is added beyond the stated rite. Blood goes to the patient's right ear tip, right thumb and right great toe. Oil is held in the priest's own left palm and sprinkled by his right finger; the patient and priest must not exchange possessors accidentally. Oil then goes upon the blood, or upon the place of the blood where the source uses that wording. Residue goes to the patient's head. Remainder is the material sense, not the inherited verb rest at ease.
