# Vayrshemon vocabulary and thesaurus expansion

The dictionary answers “What can this word mean?” The thesaurus answers “Which word fits what I mean here?” Grow the two books together by reviewing **one distinction at a time**. Start with requesting, knowing and separating materials. All three have native vocabulary, relevant books and situations in which choosing a different word changes the meaning. The [pilot queue](Pilot%20queue.md) gives the first decisions to make. The [source catalog](Source%20catalog.md) gives the texts and data to consult.

## Starting point

The progressive dictionary records **14,902 headwords and 20,941 examples**. The first progressive thesaurus has **73 families, 205 illustrated senses and 203 indexed spellings**. A spelling can occur under more than one sense. The thesaurus register relates each member to a native form, a reading, dictionary rows, source witnesses and a page. Start there before deciding that an English word needs a new native form. ## Work in periodic batches

| Order | Work | What the batch should produce |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Read the native witnesses | For each first-round question, look up the dictionary readings and reopen the cited passage in its four-column edition. Search for additional forms, compounds and senses even when the English labels differ. | A short account of what the existing language already expresses and one concrete situation where a speaker still needs to choose. |
| 2. Test the contrast | Compare the historical English sense, the English thesaurus family and the relevant non-English entries. Also take at least one idea directly from a non-English resource, such as how Finnish specifies a kinship relation. | Candidate senses with source locators, part of speech, relation type and a pair of situations that distinguish them. |
| 3. Author the native reading | Choose an existing reading, extend a documented sense, use a transparent native phrase or compound, or propose a distinct root. Record the deciding evidence. Give every admitted member its own example. | Reviewed entries for the native dictionary and the appropriate thesaurus families. An existing phrase or an explicit decision to retain one broad native word can resolve a candidate. |
| 4. Integrate and proof | When the author calls the next vocabulary review, compile the approved batch with the existing book tools. Compare changed families with the prior edition, reopen the books and inspect the edited spreads and linked index. | An updated edition ledger, precise counts and a list of candidates still open for a future book. |
| 5. Extend the search | Work through the remaining subject queues below. In parallel with subject reviews, advance the historical English inventory one letter range at a time. Record the last page and headword checked. | Cumulative coverage by volume, letter range, domain and language, with gaps distinguishable from reviewed rejections. |

Between batches, append discoveries from new translations with their passage references. Leave publication to a periodic review chosen by the author. The unit of progress is a resolved native usage question. External headword totals measure the size of the search.

## Which vocabulary comes next

1. **Everyday verbs and social distinctions.** Requests for information or action, familiarity or knowledge of a fact, and the two sides of lending. These cross many books and affect ordinary dialogue.
2. **Trades, materials and navigation.** Straining, settling and pouring off a liquid in Boyle. Measurement, depth and units in Dampier. Compare what happens, what the worker does, what instrument is used and what result is obtained. These distinctions make technical prose easier to translate precisely.
3. **Law, custom and moral judgment.** Use Locke, Hobbes, the Bible, Shakespeare and the Prince alongside Latin and Hebrew sources. Compare a legal instrument, a right it creates, and the authority administering it. Record variation between verse, dialogue, ordinary prose and legal argument where witnesses support it.
4. **Concepts that English tends to bundle.** Begin with Finnish and Norwegian kin terms and classical Greek accounts of skill and experience. With each later batch, choose another domain from a non-English source before looking for an English equivalent. Give the native expression room to be broader, narrower or grammatically different.

### Beyond the translated library

Reserve a strand of each later review for things a Vayrshemon speaker might want to say even if none of the translated authors have said them. Start with **texture and touch** (a cloth that catches on the skin versus one that chafes it over time), **care** (help offered once versus responsibility for somebody’s daily care), and **movement and transport** (bearing a load versus arranging for it to reach a destination). Gather native-language descriptions from the source catalog, then try to describe two concrete scenes using the existing Vayrshemon lexicon. If a useful distinction warrants a new root, record its native formation and mark it as an **authored extension**. Credit the foreign comparison that suggested it and the original Vayrshemon illustration separately. Its first later appearance in a translated book can then be recorded as an attestation.

### How to move from source files to a reviewable inventory

Use one row per **source lemma and sense**. Keep the source spelling and stable locator alongside the normalized lookup key. For historical OCR, reconcile the printed page sequence with the scan, then compare sample headings at the beginning, middle and end of each range. Record missing leaves and ambiguous headings with their scan page. For structured lexicons, extract entry keys and sense relations directly. Separate obsolete spellings, cross-references, derivatives, compounds and headwords according to how the source marks them.

Compare these records against native semantic keys, dictionary forms, definitions and source passages. Give the editor three short lists for each subject: existing matches, possible new senses for existing words, and concepts still without an adequate expression. Keep multiple source senses attached to one native reading where appropriate. Measure a batch by the source ranges inspected, native senses reviewed, existing expressions recovered, new senses or roots accepted, and unresolved readings carried forward. This gives a future editor a place to resume even when most candidates require no new word.

## What to record for a decision

| Field | What to keep |
| --- | --- |
| Candidate | Stable editorial ID, domain, proposed relation and a short question in plain English. |
| External evidence | Language and variety or period, source and release, original lemma, homograph/POS, sense or page locator, relevant register and a link. Keep original script and diacritics alongside the search form. |
| Native evidence | Existing semantic key, exact native spelling, Transliteration 7, dictionary row and the work, passage and reading that support this sense. Distinguish the author’s later additions from historical attestations. |
| Meaning and use | Core meaning, what it implies, what it leaves open, typical arguments or complements, register, and its nearest neighbour. Label synonym, narrower term, opposite, converse or associated concept explicitly. |
| Example and decision | One original aligned example **per member per sense**, an English explanation of what would change if its neighbour were substituted, decision and rationale, and destination family or cross-reference. Retain rejected duplicates so the next batch can recognize them. |

Use the Prince’s **ouv / senvor / tavr** as a calibration. A pilot may use a chart once, apply a correction to a bearing, or adopt a standing practice for every future watch. The object alone cannot explain the difference. It lies in employing a means, putting it into operation, or taking it up as one’s practice. The published record also distinguishes **nerbyal**, selecting among alternatives. ## Edition checks

- Draft the example in native grammar and read it back against the English. Give the sentence enough context to establish its sense. Replacing a name, changing the tense or merely inserting the definition into a template does not establish a contrast. A polysemous member gets a separate illustration for each admitted sense.
- Preserve deliberate repetitions, grammatical endings and established capitalization. Keep proper names and citation fragments out of new common-word families. Retain the dictionary’s written number words under their ordinary alphabetical positions. The three sets of twelve place values have 36 combinations and currently yield 34 distinct place words. [Dictionary publication record](../../Saves/Vayrshemon%20Dictionary%20-%20Four-Column%20Progressive%20Edition/Working/publication.json)
- Use the established facing spread. Native script and Transliteration 7 occupy the left page. The construction gloss and idiomatic English occupy the right. Keep corresponding passages aligned. Preserve research references in the progressive apparatus and working records. Keep full references out of the dictionary print text in future print updates.
- Sort the native index by **every letter** in Thimfora order and follow each edited index link after reopening. Check glyphs, clitics, punctuation and page fit. Compare untouched passages with their previous readings.
