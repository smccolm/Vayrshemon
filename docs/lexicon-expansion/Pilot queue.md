# First vocabulary reviews

Start with the first three reviews. They test three different editorial outcomes: recognizing a sense already present under another key, deciding whether a broad native verb needs a second sense, and finding out whether a technical operation can be expressed with existing vocabulary. The English sentences below are original drafts for testing the distinctions. The native wording will need to carry the same distinction before any example goes into an edition. Page numbers refer to the current progressive thesaurus. ## 1. Asking for an answer or asking someone to act

**Decision to make.** Does the existing native verb cover an ordinary request for action? Page 39 gives **wÏō** for seeking an answer. The dictionary also has **wÏō** under `appeal_request` for an earnest appeal, witnessed in *Henry V* 00138a. **wÏōen** under `request` is a noun. A noun for “request” and a marked verb for “appeal” leave the neutral verb question open. Read comparable dialogue before proposing a new form.

| Reading to test | One example for that reading | What settles the choice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Seek information | From the bank she asked whether the ferry still ran on Sundays. | A spoken answer can satisfy her. |
| Request an action | Already aboard, she asked the ferryman to put her ashore. | The desired outcome is an action. |
| Appeal for intervention | With the last ferry under orders to leave, the nurse appealed to the harbourmaster to hold it for the injured passenger. | The speaker seeks an exception from someone able to intervene. |

**Actual source test.** The Spanish MCR data lists both *pedir* and *preguntar* at Princeton 3.0 `00784342-v`. It also lists *pedir* at `00752764-v` and *preguntar* at `00784727-v`. Those records show why a blind join would mislead in either direction. Read individual senses and compare what complement the native speaker uses. For the neutral action example, test the existing root with an action complement first. ## 2. Knowing a fact, knowing a place, recognizing it

Page 7 already distinguishes **hābėr** (know), **portimost** (recognize) and **tarŧh** (remember). The dictionary also records **hābėrfāw** for acquaintance. Compare French *savoir / connaître*, German *wissen / kennen* and Finnish *tietää / tuntea*. Ask which distinctions Vayrshemon conveys through the object or complement, and which merit a separately illustrated sense. Each source word can have other meanings. [French WOLF](https://almanach.inria.fr/software_and_resources/WOLF-fr.html) · [German OpenThesaurus](https://www.openthesaurus.de/about/download) · [Finnish dictionary](https://www.kielitoimistonsanakirja.fi/)

| Reading to test | One example for that reading |
| --- | --- |
| Know a fact | From the tide table she knew the harbour would be dry at noon, although she had never seen it. |
| Be familiar through experience | He knew that harbour well enough to find the landing in fog. |
| Recognize on meeting again | After twenty years away, she recognized the harbour by its two crooked towers. |
| Remember in its absence | Far inland, he still remembered the bell that rang across the harbour at dusk. |

Keep the last two senses anchored to their published members. For familiarity, search ordinary verbs in travel and dialogue before treating a noun for acquaintance as evidence for a verbal sense.

## 3. Strain, let settle, pour off

The dictionary has **strasyemsē** (`kjv_strain_filter`, Matthew 23:24) and **velgaktrivthren** (`boyle_sediment`, Boyle, Part V, 400.13). The latter names the material left behind. The existing thesaurus illustrates evaporating on page 7 and distilling on page 9. Look in Boyle’s four-column text for a passage in which a liquid is poured away after settling. Search older English phrasing as well as the modern headword *decant*. [Historical D volume](https://archive.org/download/oxforddictionaryv3p1unse_a5h6/oxforddictionaryv3p1unse_a5h6_djvu.txt) · [Boyle’s working edition](../../Saves/The%20Sceptical%20Chymist%20-%20Four-Column%20Progressive%20Edition/Working/)

| Operation | One example for that operation | Detail the native wording must convey |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Strain | She poured the cloudy liquid through linen, and the grit stayed in the cloth. | A barrier catches material. |
| Let settle | He left the jar on the shelf overnight until the chalk lay on the bottom. | Gravity separates material while the liquid stays in the vessel. |
| Pour off | Once the chalk had settled, she tipped the clear water into a cup and left the chalk in the jar. | The upper liquid is removed while the deposit stays behind. |

Try an ordinary native phrase for the third operation. Recommend a specialist verb only if actual usage or a recurring translation need warrants it. These three processes can share a technical comparison page while retaining their separate definitions.
