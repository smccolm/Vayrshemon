# Historical KJV correction 0.10-kjv.281: construct phrases and clause flow

The author explicitly requested this correction on 2026-11-05 after finding
stuttering particle chains in the compact Bible. It is a new historical edition,
not a migration to dramatic 0.11. Editions 278 and 280 remain historical witnesses.
The foreign conceptual calques, heavy reference, plural-self I and collective
singular we remain. These innovations are authored language design, not claims
of independently attested ancient usage. The inherited 280 package remains the
lexical parent; the current modern pin was verified and is not changed.

## 1. Construct-state ladders

Use the inherited genitive **’i** on successive possessive groups when the heads
are explicit. Each following head belongs to the preceding group. A bound
ancestor/child expression already expresses its inner possession and must not
receive another particle merely to count the English word “of.”

Numbers 10:15 now analyzes the nominal chain as
`kjv_issachar/child:pl:g kjv_tribe:g army`:
**Issachar's children → their tribe → its army**. The former three front-loaded
particles disappear because two relations are on the heads and the third is
already in the bound ancestor. The eight parallel census host formulae and nine
captain formulae receive the corresponding explicitly reviewed construction.

Numbers 3:30 marks the Kohath people, families, father and household as successive
genitives before “chief.” It retains every relation and participant. This is not
a rule for deleting any adjacent pair of words.

## 2. Second-position relational clitics

Analytical relations remain useful for heavy phrases, partitives, and nested
groups whose existing interpretation must be retained. Adjacent analytical
`of` operators coalesce and attach to the first phonological word following them:

| Authored analysis | Written clitic | Interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `of of X …` → `X:relpair …` | **’yë** (’yü) | Two inherited relational operators |
| `of of of X …` → `X:reltriple …` | **’yër** (’yür) | Three operators |
| `of of of of X …` → `X:relquad …` | **’yël** (’yül) | Four operators |

This is phrasal clitic placement: its host can be a modifier or a bound noun.
It is not an adjective attached only to that host. Read it by expanding the
operators at the phrase's left edge, then reading the unchanged constituent
sequence. The depth contrast preserves nesting instead of silently collapsing
two relationships into one. A partitive and a genitive can meet at that edge;
coalescence does not turn both into ownership. Existing scope is retained, not
guessed from the next noun's position. The correction ledger records every
expansion. The construct-state formulae above take precedence where their heads
were specifically reassessed.

The paradigm is an expressly authored historical contraction. It is not a claim
that replacing a repeated sound proves every inherited phrase semantically
perfect. Difficult source scope remains available in the English and analysis.
Do not apply it to emphatic lexical repetition or across punctuation.

## 3. Dependent pronouns preserve both participants

The bound dependent form **’em** distinguishes a pronoun governed by an explicit
preposition from an adjacent free pronoun. Thus `to he he speak:pa` becomes
`to he:dep he speak:pa`: **to him / he spoke**. The preposition remains, and neither
referent is elided or assumed identical. Number stays on the pronoun before the
dependent ending; plural I remains plural I and collective we remains singular.
This allomorph is restricted to personal pronouns, including inherited number
features. It does not add a new object, case role or gender. Use it at an actual
dependent/free collision, not after every preposition in the Bible.

## 4. Purpose, cause and dependent clause endings

The old analytical `to to X … V:inf` mixes purpose with direction/recipient.
Render **for / to X / V-infinitive**, using inherited `for` for the purpose frame
and retaining the recipient `to`. The clause must actually contain an infinitive.
Causal `for` immediately before benefactive/circumstantial `for` becomes
**because / for …**; both functions survive.

Head-final clauses can legitimately end with identical verbs in adjacent
dependent and matrix predicates. The new **’eth** marks the close of the
dependent finite clause: `love:pr:subend love:pr` means **[who loves …] / loves …**,
not a single intensified verb. It is also available for embedded statements and
locative clauses. It requires a finite predicate and an actual dependency; an
earlier relative word alone is not a general license for authors to invent one.
The 47 applications in this edition were checked against their source clauses.
Both tense and every predicate remain. “Holy, holy,” “verily, verily,” vocatives,
lamentation and source repetitions do not receive this ending automatically.

## 5. Clause flow without an English-shaped opening conjunction

Additive continuation has three realizations, chosen by discourse construction:

* **Unmarked continuation** (`:cont`, no visible suffix): after a completed
  sentence in the same narrative chapter, the next finite clause can continue
  through juxtaposition. No implication of “then,” “therefore,” or causation is
  added. Subjects remain explicit; this is not pronoun dropping.
* **Second-position additive ’ã** (’oi, `:add`): the first phonological word hosts
  clause-level addition when an overt connection remains appropriate, including
  incomplete preceding boundaries and non-narrative discourse. It is a phrasal
  clitic, not coordination between that one word and the next noun.
* **Independent ãÑ** (oich, `and`): retained inside lists and clauses, at chapter
  openings, for non-finite verse fragments, and for selected liturgical/acrostic
  refrains (Psalms 119, 136, 148 and 150).

The application is deterministic and structural. There is no random synonym
rotation, verse-number alternation, silent change to “but” or “therefore,” or
change of speaker. A verse is not presumed to be a sentence. The zero and
postpositive variants retain additive meaning in the authored analysis/gloss.
Proper names and sentence beginnings retain the original Maxima map. These
phrase clitics follow the complete host word, outside its lexical inflection and
expressive ending; they do not reorder voice, tense, negation or force.
