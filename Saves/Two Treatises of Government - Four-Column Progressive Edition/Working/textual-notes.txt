Book II, §5. The supplied Hooker quotation reads “other Men weak, being of one and the same nature.” The translation follows Hooker’s “we all” here. Delphine Soulard discusses the two readings in notes 67–68 of her study of the Christ’s College copy. The English column keeps “weak.”

Book II, §43. Where the transcript gives the fraction as “1/•000”, the parallel text reads one thousandth. The native columns use the Unta fraction for 1/1000.

Book II, §§20–22. The supplied file runs from §20, ending with Jephthah’s appeal to the supreme judge, directly to Chapter IV and §22. The edition follows that wording and numbering.

Book II, §§232–239. Barclay’s Latin quotations and Locke’s English renderings each have their own Vayrshemon passages. Where the Latin of §232 is unclear about the community’s right of defence, §233 supplies Locke’s reading. In §239 the transcription’s “servile Platterers” is rendered as flatterers.

Book I, contents and §§16–17. Citations of Genesis use the sealed Bible’s native title Pô. “In habit” in §§16–19 is a capacity someone is said to hold before exercising it. It is distinct from custom and from clothing.

Book I, §§25–27. The English transcription marks several Hebrew and Greek words with “〈 in non-Latin alphabet 〉”. Locke glosses these words in the surrounding sentences. The translation follows his distinctions among livestock, wild beasts and creeping things. The English retains his quotations, the missing-script notices and the verse citations as printed.

Book I, §73. The supplied transcription reads “Gen. 111. 16.” for the sentence addressed to Eve. The Hollis edition prints the chapter as Roman iii. The Vayrshemon reads Genesis 3:16. The English reproduces the supplied file.

Book I, §126. Where the supplied transcript has “be 〈…〉 only to his Successive Heirs,” the 1690 parallel reads “be to descend only to his Successive Heirs.” The translation uses the verb of descent. The English column retains the supplied mark.
